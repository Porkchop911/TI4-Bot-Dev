## Nothing for nothing, 2026-09-16 (user-directed)

The user judged one-sided bundles wrong: "giving something for nothing shouldn't be a thing, at
least get some promise or concession", and "ban gifts, there has to at least be a commitment for
future action". Three changes followed. Nothing is committed.

### Changes

| Shape | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| FuturePayment | Proposer offered nothing and asked the recipient to pay later. Taken ~28% of the time as a pure beg. | Credit: goods now against one more next round. |
| Legacy one-sided trade shapes (a note nobody can pay for, "gift N commodities") | Passed into contacts as gifts. | The empty side owes a commodity next round, so the deal still happens with somebody on the hook. |
| Trade primary refresh | Not modelled. | New RefreshForCommodity template: the holder of an unplayed Trade card promises to spend the primary's refresh on the partner, against a commodity paid back later. Kept only when the primary names that seat (the self-replenish and the secondary do not count), broken at the deadline otherwise. |

Implementation: new `DealTerm::ReplenishFor`, new `DiplomacyEventContext::CommoditiesReplenished`
raised at the one chosen-other replenish site in `strategy_cards.rs`, and a candidate filter that
refuses any bundle with an empty side.

A first attempt banned one-sided shapes outright. That killed trading: the soak fell to
`transactions: 0` with offers down from 98 to 28, because most early-round trade shapes are exactly
that (a partner with no trade goods cannot pay for anything). Pairing with a commitment restores it.

### Measured, one round, seed 11, temperature 0.5, checkpoint-212544

|---|---|---|---|
| Trade take rate | 22% | 24% | **45%** (93 offered) |

Deals now carry promises that settle, and paying a promise is itself a decision seats take. The

## What diplomacy costs per PPO update, measured 2026-09-16

Same build, same bundle (`checkpoint-212544`), same flags and seed base, one update each,
`--no-checkpoint`, 96 games of 4 rounds at temperature 2.5 on CUDA:

| | decisions | rollout | optimise | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--diplomacy` | 237,220 | 25.3s | 8.5s | **33.8s** |
| without it | 119,832 | 12.5s | 4.9s | **17.4s** |

Diplomacy still doubles the work per update after the pruning. 1000 updates is about 9.4 hours with
it and about 4.8 hours without, which matches the overnight run (00:18 to 10:02).

**The cost probe is not representative of training load and should not be quoted as the cost.** It
plays 2 rounds with random deciders and reports diplomacy at +4% decisions (338 to 351). The trainer
plays 4 rounds at temperature 2.5, where seats open contacts constantly: in the reviewer runs, 534
contact options were offered in a single round and about a third were taken, each opening a
multi-step window. The probe's memory figures do hold and are a real improvement: journal 1,547
bytes over 5 events (was 28,413 over 137), state +3.5% (was x2.4), option payloads +7% (was x11).

**Consequence for the open question.** Empty contacts are not a tidiness problem: roughly half of
every update is spent inside contacts, and about 86% of them end with no offer. Requiring a contact
to have content is the single largest training saving available.

### Null swaps removed, 2026-09-16

The user spotted a deal reading "seat4 gives 1 trade good now / seat3 gives 1 trade good now". The
legacy generator enumerates every `give 0..3` against `want 0..3` in trade goods and skips only
0-for-0, so equal amounts are a literal no-op and unequal ones are a one-way gift with extra steps.
Inside a contact, `is_null_swap` drops any bundle whose immediate transfers are trade goods on both
sides. Trade goods only: a symmetric swap of anything else is a real deal. A commodity becomes a
trade good when it changes hands (21.5), so swapping commodities leaves both sides better off, and
swapping Support for the Throne, Ceasefires or Political Secrets trades the cards' effects rather
than a currency. The legacy window is unchanged for diplomacy-off games, where the goods-for-goods
shapes are legal transactions.

A first version also refused any bundle whose two sides were identical. That was wrong -- it killed
the commodity-for-commodity swap, which is the classic both-gain trade -- and the user caught it. The
rule is now goods-for-goods only, with a test that a commodity swap is still offered.

Verified: engine 1329 (with a test that a contact never offers goods for goods), soak green with
transactions 3 and offers 36 -- the same counts as before the filter, so the null swaps were never
producing accepted trades, only noise in the option set.
