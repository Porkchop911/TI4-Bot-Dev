# ARENA-001: scenario generator and first labelled corpus — 2026-09-16

Second pass over `plans/BATTLE_ARENA_AGENT_HANDOFF_2026-09-16.md`. The first pass proved one matchup
could be driven to an outcome; this one enumerates the scenario space, plays both role assignments,
runs across every core, and writes a labelled corpus. **This document replaces the earlier probe
report of the same name, whose results table, throughput figure and "not done" list are superseded.**

`crates/ti4-training/examples/battle_arena_probe.rs` (**uncommitted** — the handoff requests no
commits for this task). Nothing is written to disk unless `--emit` is passed.

## What it does now

- **Enumerates** legal fleets per profile up to `--max-ships` non-fighter ships, with a capacity
  check so fighters appear only where something can carry them. Capacity is profile-dependent: a Sol
  carrier carries 6, legalising fighter counts an ordinary carrier cannot.
- **Plays both role assignments.** A matchup and its mirror are two scenarios, not one.
- **Groups by `family()`** — order-independent — so a matchup and its mirror always land in the same
  train/val/test split.
- **Runs on every core** via `par_chunks`, one chunk per worker, merged in chunk order so the panel
  does not depend on which thread finished first.
- **Emits one row per scenario** with three outcome rates over the dice panel, plus a manifest.
- Still drives the real engine through the public `combat::resolve`. No second rules implementation.
- Two declared, versioned policies answer sustains and casualties by unit facts with deterministic
  tie-breaks, never by option order.

## Throughput

| | fights/s |
|---|---|
| single-threaded, per-fight setup | 1,344 |
| 32 workers, per-fight setup | 9,705 |
| 32 workers, setup hoisted per worker | **17,740** |

`fixtures::game()` runs the whole `start_game` path and `plain_systems(1)` walks every system in the
galaxy to take one — both were paid per fight, before a die was rolled. Hoisting them to one
constructed `GameState` per worker, cloned per fight, was worth 1.85× on cap 3 and 2.38× on cap 4 on
top of what threading bought. 13.2× overall.

Scaling is ~13× on 32 workers, not 32×. Serial cost remains and has not been isolated; the measured
rate is reported rather than a theoretical one.

**Correctness across the rewrite:** cap 3 reproduced `75,325 / 77,172 / 2,071` and cap 2 with four
factions reproduced `692,762 / 705,957 / 25,953` — identical counts before and after both the
parallelisation and the setup hoist. That is the check that the per-fight clone does not leak fleets
between fights.

## The corpus

Cap 3, 64 dice seeds, Hacan, `cheapest-fresh`: 19,321 rows from 1,236,544 fights in 69.7s.

- **Label:** `attacker_win_rate` / `defender_win_rate` / `mutual_rate`, summing to 1. Three fields
  rather than one, because a defender win and mutual destruction are different results that a single
  attacker rate cannot distinguish. Verified: 0 rows violate the sum.
- **Rate, not outcome.** Dice decide one fight; the quantity to predict is the probability. 64 seeds
  puts per-scenario label noise near ±6pp instead of ±17pp at 8.
- **Features are factual** — unit counts by id, faction, upgrade flag. Derived combat strength is
  left to the model.
- **Split:** deterministic FNV-1a over `family()`, 80/10/10 (15,481 / 1,958 / 1,882). Verified:
  **0 families span more than one split.**
- 0 NaN labels, 0 errors, 0 contaminated positions.

### Two properties a consumer must not ignore

**34.6% of rows are foregone conclusions** (attacker rate exactly 0 or 1), and the label stdev is
0.406 — the distribution is bimodal, not concentrated near 0.5. A third of the training signal is
"the bigger fleet wins", learnable from unit counts without any combat understanding. Raw accuracy
or MSE on this corpus will look excellent for reasons unrelated to the skill being taught. Evaluate
stratified by contestedness, or downweight the easy rows. Recorded in the manifest.

**Uniform enumeration does not match play frequencies.** A 3-dreadnought stack carries the same
weight as a fleet that actually occurs in a game. Uniform coverage is the point — it is how combat
knowledge is obtained without playing games — but the design's plan to freeze this predictor into
the actor's input layer propagates any miscalibration into PPO's decisions. Reweighting toward
played positions is likely needed before integration.

**Labels are conditional on the casualty policy**, recorded in the manifest. The two rules move
mutual destruction from 2,071 to 832 on identical positions, and in the first pass changed surviving
damaged dreadnoughts 1 → 9 against cruisers 49 → 36. A label is an outcome *under a named policy*,
not combat odds.

## Corrections to earlier claims in this session, all the assistant's

Carried forward from the first pass:

1. **The casualty rule moved the win rate modestly, not "sharply"** as predicted. The attacker fleet
   in that matchup loses on its merits; the policy made it somewhat worse.
2. **The flat "mean rounds 3.00" was coincidence, not structure.** Rounds spread 1 to 5 under both
   policies and the mean landed on 3.00 twice by chance. `MAX_ROUNDS` is 50 and was never approached.

New in this pass:

3. **Sizing was ~3× low**, reported from the collapsed enumeration, which counted each matchup once.
4. **A false mirror diagnosis.** I claimed mirrors were not collapsing; the counts were exactly
   triangular and the filter had always worked.
5. **The defender "landslide" was a design artefact.** Canonical ordering handed the attacker the
   lexicographically smaller fleet in every pair, producing 58,010 defender wins to 18,839. Playing
   both orderings gives 75,325 / 77,172 — near symmetry.
6. **"Attacker fires first" was wrong.** Fire is simultaneous (LRR 78.6): `combat.rs` rolls for the
   attacker, then the defender, then queues both sides' hits. The real asymmetries are anti-fighter
   barrage (78.3), space cannon, the nebula defender bonus, and retreat order (78.4).
7. **Throughput was reported twice from a stale binary.** Two `link.exe` 1104 failures left a
   pre-parallel exe on disk and its numbers were reported as parallel. Cap-6 runtime was estimated at
   27 minutes (stale binary, assumed linear scaling), then ~2.0 hours, then ~64 minutes — only the
   last from a measured rate on the binary that would run it. A build that does not relink now aborts
   the run rather than falling through to it.
8. **Mutual destruction was overstated.** From one symmetric dreadnought duel at 19/64 I inferred it
   was common; corpus-wide the mean mutual rate is 0.0116 and only 1.0% of rows exceed 0.10. The
   three-way label is still correct — it costs nothing and removes a real ambiguity — but the
   problem it solves is smaller than claimed.

## Limitation carried forward

`CombatWindow::pending_choice` and `resolve` are private to `ti4-engine` (`ti4-policy` reaches them
only from an in-crate test), so the public `combat::resolve` drives the window. It consumes the
scoring pause internally, which an isolated fight has no way to service, so **a pause cannot be
recorded as a failure by this probe.** Unchanged from the first pass.
