## Implementation and compatibility evidence

- `TimingContext` is constructed only by the driver for a typed emission and carries the live
  `GameState`, content/source scope, the one game-owned `Table`, recorded dice, domain-separated
  RNG, and the trace-local `EventSequence`.
- The legacy context-free API remains available for its established event-only callers, but refuses
  any stateful effect or predicate instead of silently resolving it without game services.
- `Game` owns both resolver and event allocator. `STRATEGY_CARD_CHOSEN` is the first driver boundary
  migrated to `emit_with_context`; its payload matches the oracle's factual `player`, `card`, and
  `goods` fields. WHEN cancellation leaves card ownership and the legacy event log unchanged.
- Nested stateful effects allocate from the game-owned sequence, producing `OUTER#1` then
  `INNER#2`; they do not fabricate a second trace-local ID stream.
- Resolver priority and frequency lifecycle counters are synchronized from `GameState` immediately
  before each driver emission. This preserves per-turn and per-round frequency scopes rather than
  retaining a resolver-private clock.
- The wiring guard now fails if `game.rs` stops calling `timing.emit_with_context`. This proves the
  production driver reaches the resolver, without pretending that other rule subsystems emit their
  not-yet-settled event vocabularies.
