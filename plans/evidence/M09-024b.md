## What was implemented

- `crates/ti4-training/src/vocabulary_corpus.rs` — the three sources, each returning its own
  `Contribution` so a silently-empty source is distinguishable from a redundant one. The replay
  collector extracts through the bound `SeatObservation` the engine hands a decider, so discovery
  sees exactly what live play sees; a pass that reached around that boundary would enumerate names
  no live decision can produce.
- `crates/ti4-training/examples/vocabulary_discovery.rs` — the driver, which reports each source,
  its unique contribution, the family distribution, growth per source, and either writes the
  artifact or stops.

Three focused tests on the parts that are pure: source (c) covers every selectable seat and every
objective alias; every name source (c) invents belongs to a registered family (a guessed name would
occupy a column for ever while naming something no extractor emits); and the unique-contribution
arithmetic.

## Limitations

1. **`V_cap` is still unmeasured for the architecture that will actually be built**, because which
   families it includes is the open question. The figures above are exact for the corpus as
   emitted today.
2. **No frequency data.** The pass records whether a name was ever emitted, not how often. If the
   review considers pruning by frequency rather than by family, that needs a second pass — the
   collector would have to count rather than insert.
3. **Four rounds.** The replay uses the round-4 horizon the branch is measured at. A longer horizon
   would emit more names, so 203,843 is a lower bound on the full-game vocabulary, not a ceiling.
4. **Source (c) is deliberately narrow** — decomposition and met flags only. `option:` word
   features depend on option text the engine builds at a decision, so enumerating them would mean
   guessing strings; they are the replay's job.
