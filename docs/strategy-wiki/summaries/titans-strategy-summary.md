# Titans of Ul: strategy summary draft

**Source:** SCPT episode 458, “The Ultimate Guide to the Titans of Ul,” published 2026-05-27. Complete, checksum-verified ASR transcript; speaker identity is not claimed. The episode mixes Prophecy of Kings-era faction knowledge with an early Thunders Edge breakthrough discussion. These are strategic opinions and reported interactions, not a current-rules or errata review. The `Slumber State Computing` passages especially describe early research/playtest games.

## Strategic thesis

Titans convert exploration into a durable board network. Saturn Engine II supplies inexpensive reach and a credible fleet, while sleepers and Hel-Titans create structures, defense, production, and future access. This makes Titans unusually flexible at stage-one scoring, control objectives, and many structure requirements. The constraint is command-token income: ships, activations, and deployments all compete for the same pool. Draft influence, sell Terraform when opening money changes a real action, and keep the cruiser upgrade moving toward round two. (C01, C02, C08, C09, C10)

## Opening economy and map

**Influence is the first slice question.** The speakers want influence in the slice because Titans have many useful activations and their cruisers each occupy fleet capacity. One speaker says a 0-resource/8-influence slice is workable; the 7-resource/3-influence alternative feels worse because Elysium can supply resources while command tokens remain the limiting input. This is a preference, not a universal map formula: planet access, skips, objectives, and neighbors still matter. A tech skip is valuable for reaching the upgrade and later technology choices. The speakers disagree about how much blue technology deserves priority, while agreeing that yellow/green routes fit Titans well. (C01, C09, C24)

**Terraform is opening liquidity.** It adds one resource, one influence, and all three planet traits to a non-home, non-Mecatol planet. One speaker prefers selling early, usually asking around three trade goods and adjusting to the buyer; the other supports getting immediate value when it enables the desired tech, Warfare build, or round-one score. Raise the ask when an attachment or four-of-a-kind objective makes the note unusually valuable. Waiting can improve its objective value, so do not treat three trade goods as a fixed market price. A Jol-Nar Research Agreement exchange can be better when Titans receive useful technology, with Hyper Metabolism specifically praised; this is one of the few swaps the speakers elevate over a cash sale. (C02, C03)

A useful opening goal is to score a feasible public objective in round one, then pursue Mecatol Rex. The ideal is to score, take Politics, and take Custodians; if that cannot be arranged, the speakers prefer scoring on tempo and targeting an Imperial bonus point in round two over sacrificing the opening score for a speculative Custodians grab. Imperial can be taken after another player reaches Mecatol. The recommendation depends on objective feasibility, speaker order, and the opponent's approach: Saar is named as a potential Mecatol contest, though a heavy Saar push can leave other systems open to Titans' cruisers. (C20)

## Build the engine

**Saturn Engine II is the essential upgrade.** The guide describes it as a cost-two cruiser with combat six, movement three, capacity two, and sustain damage. That package gives Titans an unusually cheap all-purpose ship for reaching objectives, the Fracture, and fights. Prioritize obtaining it early, ideally by round two; the exact technology route should flex with skips, breakthrough access, and strategy-card timing. The speakers acknowledge its limits: it uses fleet capacity, has less carrying potential than carrier-heavy fleets, and lacks bombardment. It is versatile rather than best at every specialized task. (C08, C09, C24)

Tech primary is not an automatic first pick. The speakers rank Leadership and Trade higher, with Politics also high when it secures speaker and the round-two plan. Technology sits in the middle: primary or secondary can accelerate Saturn Engine II, but an early Warfare pop may leave no useful Warfare secondary to follow. Leadership is especially valuable because tokens fuel Titans' multiple activations and deployments. Trade helps a two-commodity faction pay for its opening. Diplomacy is lower priority unless a spend objective, refresh, or breakthrough timing makes it directly useful. Construction is usually low priority because sleepers can become structures, though the primary can solve an infantry shortage or enable extra opening fleets. (C21, C22)

The speakers recommend considering a Warfare opening when a nearby three-planet system creates a strong sleeper and structure line. It can create a staging position for expansion and accelerate the commander unlock. Remember the distinction they stress: Warfare creates the activation opportunity, but sleepers placed then are awakened on a later activation, not during that same action. Construction may instead be useful where its build and the Hecatoncheires deployment add enough ground forces to take more planets. (C19, C22, C23)

## Sleeper timing and board presence

Plan Scan Link, Terragenesis, and Awaken as separate timing windows. Scan Link explores one planet in the activated system only if a Titans unit was already there. After exploring a planet without a sleeper, Terragenesis can place or move one. Awaken is available when activating a system that already contains a sleeper; the guide emphasizes that the sleeper must predate that activation. In practical terms, explore and place now, then awaken on a later activation. The speakers warn that the printed timing is easy to misread. (C04, C05)

An unredeemed sleeper can be valuable. Opponents see it and must consider that Titans may later activate the system and turn it into a Hel-Titan. Coalescence makes this a real projection threat when another player enters: Titans can awaken into the contested area and participate in combat. The speakers say they once underrated this option and now see it as a defining skill. The five-sleeper limit means placement still requires selectivity; this is deterrence and future access, not guaranteed control of the planet. (C06, C07)

Spread Hel-Titans across planets when the goal is broad defense and production. Their structure/ground presence, shield, sustain, and production make planets harder to take and help with structure objectives. Multiple Hel-Titans in a system can be useful for production, but concentrating several on one planet often projects less power than distributing them. Keep one Hecatoncheires in reinforcements when possible: when a qualifying PDS is placed, the mech's deploy effect can add a mech and infantry instead. The guide describes using Transit Diodes to remove and place PDS as a way to trigger this elsewhere. Its rules discussion also cautions that Hell-Titans cannot be moved by effects that merely move ground forces; verify exact interactions before relying on them. (C10, C11, C26)

## Economy, leaders, and negotiations

Titans' commander, Tungstantus, rewards actual production with a trade good. The guide notes that a Hel-Titan's production can generate that good in the same production window, allowing it to fund the current build. The speaker also describes spending it immediately to avoid Mentak pillage. This and the unlock condition of five structures can make early Hel-Titan placement pay twice: toward the commander and future production income. A three-planet system can accelerate the unlock, but starting infantry, planet access, and deals may leave Titans one short. (C13, C19)

The hero refreshes Elysium and increases its values. The speakers disagree about whether the added resources or influence are usually more important. They often favor influence because tokens are the binding constraint; spend for resources when a concrete tech, objective, or double-tech plan makes it worthwhile. (C12, C14)

Terraform and coalescence create bargaining options. Trade Terraform for immediate money or a useful alliance; the episode names Arborec, Cabal, Naaz-Rokha, Crimson, and Jol-Nar as alliances with particular appeal in different situations. The early Slumber State Computing discussion adds possible deals involving shared defense, an alliance swap, objective progress, trade refreshes, and action-card draws. These examples came from a small number of research games, and the speaker openly questions whether some were good trades. Make the exchange specific and price each side's benefit; consent and trust are necessary. (C02, C03, C16)

Be especially cautious about helping Yssaril coexist under the breakthrough. The speakers worry that repeated status-phase card value could give Yssaril a major advantage; they doubt ordinary payments compensate for that risk. Conversely, the breakthrough can support Push Boundaries when Titans coexist on planets whose owner retains control, but this is presented as conditional, early-era advice. Do not confuse preparing a scoring position with meeting an objective: check the revealed requirement and the actual board state before claiming the point. (C15, C17, C28)

Tellurian, the agent, is described as a flexible hit cancel rather than ordinary sustain damage. Preserve it for an important round-two fight or a late game contest; once it no longer protects Titans' own path, it can be used to disrupt an opponent. The timing and target should be chosen for the actual win condition. (C18)

## Late game and counters

The Fracture is a natural Saturn Engine II destination. Three movement can carry the cruiser through the ingress, across the Fracture, and into the adjacent system, opening routes to relics and other slices. A Fracture-adjacent ingress in the home slice can also serve as a staging or production position. The speakers rank the Imperial bonus and public scoring ahead of relic hunting in round two; take relics when those priorities are covered. Hel-Titans need special positioning or Transit Diodes for some Fracture plans, while the cruisers provide more straightforward access. (C26, C27)

Titans can pivot toward many control and structure objectives, and the breakthrough discussion adds possible coexistence-based lines. The speakers call out stage-two structures as especially comfortable and describe a possible Rule Distant Lands setup. Treat those as options to evaluate, not automatic scores. Make an Example is singled out as more awkward because it can require building destroyers, while opponents with large carrier fleets or ground-force volume can test Saturn Engine II's limited capacity. X-89 is proposed as a late-game answer to a ground-force mismatch; Fleet Logistics remains useful for an action-phase win, so the choice depends on what the table demands and whether both routes are realistically affordable in tech. (C09, C15, C25, C28)
