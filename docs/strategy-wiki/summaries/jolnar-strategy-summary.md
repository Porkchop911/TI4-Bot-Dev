# Jol-Nar Strategy Summary — draft

**Thesis.** Play Jol-Nar as a flexible, wealthy technology broker whose opening is constrained by two starting infantry and production timing. Stabilize the first round, negotiate explicitly around Research Agreement and technology, then adapt the technology portfolio to the revealed scoring race. These are strategic opinions from SCPT 340 (published 2024-02-29), not current-rules verification. Source times are exact audio seconds.

## Connections and counters

Research Agreement and E-Res Siphons are separate bargaining assets: Research Agreement returns to Jol-Nar after another player gains the technology Jol-Nar researched, while E-Res Siphons rewards an opponent activation in a system containing Jol-Nar ships. The guide advises selling access to useful technologies and treats Siphons as both income and a deterrent. Neither trigger is automatic; coordinate timing and ship placement. Trade or taking Technology can give Jol-Nar more control over when the conversation happens. (C04, C05, C09, C10)

Brilliant lets Jol-Nar turn a Technology-secondary follow into its primary instead, while Analytical allows ignoring a prerequisite on non-upgrade technology. That breadth supports flexible research, but the hero cannot replace unit upgrades. Choose upgrades around map, objectives and likely combat. (C10, C14, C18)

PDS II is a round-one option when Jol-Nar has a threatening neighbor; Dreadnought II and Carrier II can preserve range and deliver ground forces. The guide links these upgrades to being able to replace Gravity Drive later. Anti-Mass may look expendable until an empty-space stage two appears, so the hosts recommend waiting for objective information before using the hero when possible. (C16-C20)

Opponents can exploit the early infantry bottleneck if Warfare timing fails. A committed attacker may ignore E-Res deterrence, and a thin invasion force remains vulnerable to Fragile. Preserve home-system ground forces when Mahact can threaten space. If deals fail, prioritize reliable development over expected Research Agreement income. (C02, C06–C08, C23)

## Opening alternatives

The guide’s safer opening is a deliberately modest round one: secure a workable pair of planets, use the best available production timing, and negotiate rather than trying to match factions that expand more easily. A Warfare primary is the direct way to control the build and make sure the ground-force shortage does not turn into a catastrophic start. If Warfare is unavailable or its secondary is timed well, following it can preserve resources through Sarween Tools; this line can yield more than the primary, but only if the timing works. The tradeoff is about taking the card versus following its secondary, not simply whether Jol-Nar “needs Warfare.” (C02, C08)

A more ambitious Custodians opening is possible through a researched Cruiser II plus production, but the hosts resist treating this as the default. If the point is uncontested or changes the scoring race, take the opening; if it requires sacrificing defense and early development, score later with Jol-Nar’s strong technology access. Similarly, Diplomacy is an alternative only when the table has already committed to an order and exchange that helps the faction. Without that agreement, a safe Leadership pick or later Construction can better fit the underlying problem. (C11–C13)
