# Yin Brotherhood strategy summary

**Draft from SCPT 257, “Super Yin Brotherhood Strategy Guide” (published 2022-07-27).** The complete transcript is ASR-derived and its compatibility is unreviewed. These are the hosts’ strategic judgments and examples, not current-rules verification. The guide folds in Codex 3’s then-new leader suite and older Yin discussion, so component names, leader text and rules interpretations need review.

Yin’s central challenge in this guide is converting a thin economy into enough units, tokens and influence to make its narrow abilities matter. A solid start and defensive threats can buy time, but the hosts do not see a reliable faction-driven way to accelerate into the scoring pack. The recommendations below emphasize conditional investments, table deals and opportunities that can be scored and retained.

## Strategic frame and faction kit

**C01.** Treat Yin as a low-momentum faction whose defensive tricks rarely create a lead; plan for survival and scoring opportunities rather than assuming the kit will pull the faction into contention. *When it applies:* Limited games and guide’s map/table assumptions; not a win-rate result. [SCPT 257 47:59.10–48:41.52 (s000742–s000755)]

**C02.** Use Yin’s solid opening pieces and Sarween Tools as a modest platform, while remembering the two-commodity economy is tight. Darien’s 4/4 can fund either a round-one technology or tokens, but its values are spent one at a time. *When it applies:* Round one: choose the 4/4 value needed for the immediate tech or token plan. [SCPT 257 08:00.78–09:39.82 (s000113–s000142)]

**C03.** Indoctrination is a one-time start-of-ground-combat choice: pay two influence to replace one opposing infantry with Yin infantry. It does not repeat each combat round and does not target arbitrary ground forces. *When it applies:* Only at combat start, with enemy infantry and influence available. [SCPT 257 10:03.86–11:12.70 (s000148–s000167)]

**C04.** Use Indoctrination as a cheap-combat skip or offensive edge against a planet defended by a lone infantry. Do not count on it as a strong defensive wall: opponents can attack with mechs, bombard, or bring enough force to take the planet anyway. *When it applies:* Best into lone infantry; weaker against mechs, bombardment or a larger invasion. [SCPT 257 18:24.90–20:07.12 (s000280–s000302)]

**C05.** Moyin’s Ashes can turn the Indoctrination replacement into a mech for one additional influence, but the guide stresses that the replacement still has to be an enemy infantry and the mech must already be in reinforcements. *When it applies:* Three influence total, an opposing infantry, and a mech in reinforcements. [SCPT 257 15:46.08–17:25.88 (s000233–s000266)]

**C06.** Devotion trades a Yin cruiser or destroyer for a hit after a space-combat round. It may help in a sustained fleet battle or close a firepower gap, but cannot trigger after the opponent retreats and ends combat. A lone destroyer is poor “gum”: it may die before the trigger, and sacrificing the blocker can reopen the space. *When it applies:* Use in an ongoing fight; a lone destroyer can die early or cease blocking. [SCPT 257 11:40.00–13:06.44 (s000176–s000194); SCPT 257 13:06.44–13:43.58 (s000195–s000203); SCPT 257 20:36.10–21:27.92 (s000307–s000315); SCPT 257 21:27.92–22:24.92 (s000316–s000331)]

**C07.** Treat the Van Hauge as a defensive deterrent that can sit at home: its destruction also destroys the other ships in the system, making an attacker weigh the cost of clearing it before taking the ground. *When it applies:* Most useful at home when an attacker needs to clear space first. [SCPT 257 14:09.68–15:40.32 (s000209–s000230)]

**C08.** Sell Greyfire Mutagen readily for whatever the table will pay, rather than waiting for an ideal moment; its narrow requirement for multiple opposing ground forces including infantry makes demand uncertain. More combative, non-planet-swap metas can raise its value. *When it applies:* Sell for modest money; value rises in combat-heavy, non-planet-swap metas. [SCPT 257 22:36.76–23:18.70 (s000338–s000347); SCPT 257 23:19.34–24:46.56 (s000348–s000375); SCPT 257 25:43.20–26:13.74 (s000391–s000397)]

**C09.** Brother Milor’s revised agent can add fighters after a ship dies or infantry after a ground force dies, for the affected player. The guide sees the ground-force trigger as a meaningful improvement and a possible sellable aid for combats others did not plan around. *When it applies:* When a unit is destroyed; defensive promises are non-binding. [SCPT 257 31:32.06–32:54.28 (s000472–s000490); SCPT 257 32:55.16–33:45.18 (s000491–s000506)]

**C10.** Unlock Brother Omar by using either Indoctrination or Devotion; once unlocked, the commander can let Yin research another player’s technology while returning one Yin infantry to reinforcements to ignore that technology’s prerequisites. *When it applies:* After either faction ability, if another player owns a useful technology. [SCPT 257 34:20.08–35:05.46 (s000515–s000523)]

**C11.** Value Omar as timing-dependent access to another player’s tech, not as an extra free research or a dependable route to Yin Spinner or Impulse Core. Since Technology resolves starting with its primary player then in speaker order, Yin may be behind the table’s latest research. *When it applies:* Unlock early if possible; account for speaker order and prior research. [SCPT 257 35:48.84–37:08.04 (s000537–s000566); SCPT 257 37:43.66–39:03.28 (s000577–s000609)]

**C12.** The guide discourages pursuing Yin’s faction technologies by default: Yin Spinner is a useful post-production infantry placement but deep in green, while Impulse Core costs a cruiser or destroyer for a hit and sits deep in yellow. *When it applies:* Only when a skip and concrete use justify the investment. [SCPT 257 27:34.78–28:43.04 (s000415–s000430); SCPT 257 28:49.00–30:10.30 (s000432–s000454)]

## Leaders, deals and economy

**C13.** Dannel of the Tenth commits up to three infantry from reinforcements to non-home planets and resolves invasions there; the guide notes the units cannot be hit by Space Cannon and invasion resolution can create Indoctrination opportunities. *When it applies:* Non-home targets with a plausible invasion; committed units avoid Space Cannon. [SCPT 257 40:00.00–41:20.18 (s000624–s000635)]

**C14.** When possible, hold the hero until late in the round, then use it to contest a needed planet or prepare a control-objective attempt. The three infantry do not secure planets by themselves: Indoctrinating all three as mechs would cost nine influence, combats can fail, and opponents can retake planets. *When it applies:* Late in the round against a valuable target; budget influence and retaliation. [SCPT 257 41:23.56–42:13.66 (s000637–s000647); SCPT 257 42:13.66–43:18.34 (s000648–s000664); SCPT 257 45:01.96–46:19.42 (s000696–s000716)]

**C15.** The hosts report that the hero’s commit action could attack a Support for the Throne partner without activating a system and therefore without losing that support, but they frame this as an unsettled interpretation that may change. *When it applies:* Verify before use; the hosts compare it to a past interpretation later corrected. [SCPT 257 43:30.42–45:01.96 (s000668–s000695)]

**C16.** The hosts propose negotiating a no-retreat Devotion fight against a neighbor’s carrier to unlock Omar, using Devotion on one of that player’s fighters; an alternative version attacks with two Yin destroyers and gives the neighbor Milor so it can net fighters. Both plans depend on a willing partner and safe combat outcomes. *When it applies:* Requires an agreed neighbor, a carrier/fighter combat that does not destroy the carrier, the no-retreat deal, and Devotion timing; the alternate two-destroyer plan is a separate proposed version. [SCPT 257 52:43.36–53:41.84 (s000814–s000831); SCPT 257 54:30.14–55:36.32 (s000843–s000860)]

**C17.** Do not spend Yin’s only early trade ship—the starting destroyer—on an automatic round-one commander unlock. Its income role is valuable, and the guide prefers preserving that option before considering a first-action round-two play. *When it applies:* When the destroyer is the sole early trade route; reconsider in round two. [SCPT 257 63:36.56–65:04.02 (s000988–s001006)]

**C18.** Put money first because command counters, plastic and Yin’s influence abilities all compete for a thin economy. A successful guide game took Trade twice; the practical aim is to fund useful fleets and abilities, not to save every coin indefinitely. *When it applies:* Across the game; spend for a specific tactical or scoring opportunity. [SCPT 257 71:16.28–71:58.44 (s001102–s001111)]

**C19.** A modest round-one alternative is to expand through the slice, take useful blue technology if available, retain some money, and build at home; the guide treats a stable opening as success even if Yin neither scores nor wins Custodians. *When it applies:* When no high-confidence Custodians line exists; adapt to objective and map. [SCPT 257 71:58.44–72:48.64 (s001112–s001122)]

## Strategy cards

**C20.** Leadership is attractive when Yin needs command counters. Following Technology, Warfare and Diplomacy secondaries can also be worthwhile if the table order and cash allow, but the guide labels that multi-secondary opening conditional rather than routine. *When it applies:* When command counters bind and useful secondaries are available. [SCPT 257 65:43.14–66:57.78 (s001015–s001027)]

**C21.** If Custodians is not realistically available, the guide is willing to take Diplomacy for the cash and let the other players contest the point rather than give up Yin’s survival plan merely to deny it. *When it applies:* When Yin is outside a credible Custodians race and cash or resulting round-one development matters more than contesting the point. [SCPT 257 66:58.62–67:48.64 (s001029–s001045)]

**C22.** Politics is a low round-one priority unless a specific line—such as a reachable Gravity Drive opportunity through an unlocked commander and Creuss access—makes speaker control actionable. Do not sacrifice staying on track just to hold speaker. *When it applies:* Only when speaker yields a real scoring or technology line. [SCPT 257 67:48.64–69:01.18 (s001046–s001059); SCPT 257 69:03.84–69:45.96 (s001062–s001069)]

**C23.** Take Construction early only when a revealed structure objective makes it a credible scoring route; otherwise the hosts prefer Diplomacy and would defer the structure point rather than spend the round-one pick on Construction. *When it applies:* Early only with a revealed structure objective Yin can actually meet. [SCPT 257 69:51.30–70:44.26 (s001071–s001088)]

**C24.** Trade is a high-priority card for Yin, with extra value when a wormhole or accessible neighbors increase the number of trade partners. Use income to buy units, tech and counters; do not assume the two-commodity faction earns as much as richer Trade users. *When it applies:* When commodity washing or accessible neighbors improve the return. [SCPT 257 62:52.58–63:33.50 (s000977–s000986); SCPT 257 70:44.54–71:58.44 (s001089–s001111)]

**C25.** Warfare is most compelling when it supports a concrete plan—Custodians, a rift gamble, an extra expansion or a home build after Diplomacy—rather than as an automatic extra move. Account for the destroyer’s separate trade and commander-unlock roles. *When it applies:* When a concrete tactical or Custodians plan justifies the primary. [SCPT 257 72:48.64–73:57.00 (s001123–s001138); SCPT 257 73:57.00–75:07.18 (s001139–s001160); SCPT 257 63:36.56–65:04.02 (s000988–s001006)]

**C26.** Technology is still a sound round-one pick: Yin starts with one yellow tech but wants a blue mobility and carrier path, and the source says the eventual value can justify early research even when it does not immediately change the turn. *When it applies:* Round one if the economy and public objective permit. [SCPT 257 75:02.72–76:32.26 (s001160–s001184)]

## Technology, objectives and counterplay

**C27.** Prioritize Gravity Drive and Carrier II, with Dark Energy Tap or Antimass Deflectors as a route depending on the map. The guide favors the range and transport flexibility for inexpensive fleets carrying infantry to opportunistic planets. *When it applies:* Default direction, adjusted for map, skips and objective needs. [SCPT 257 78:37.04–79:59.93 (s001220–s001239)]

**C28.** Prefer Carrier II to Cruiser II as the usual upgrade: the guide argues Yin gets useful transport and fighter support while progressing in blue. Dreadnought II can still work in a slower, richer fleet plan, but is not the default investment. *When it applies:* Carrier II by default; costlier upgrades need economy and objective support. [SCPT 257 79:28.70–79:59.93 (s001230–s001239); SCPT 257 80:30.00–81:12.50 (s001240–s001248); SCPT 257 86:10.38–87:05.42 (s001317–s001328)]

**C29.** Fleet Logistics rises when Imperial and Yin’s hero can combine for a late Mecatol Rex play: take the planet, then use a second action to exploit Imperial timing. The guide sees this as a conditional scoring line against fleets that do not cover the space. *When it applies:* Requires Fleet Logistics, an Imperial window and force to take and hold Mecatol. [SCPT 257 82:32.12–84:00.74 (s001267–s001286)]

**C30.** Treat Light/Wave Deflector as a strong late-game technology, often accessible through Omar after another player researches it, while Sling Relay mainly buys a stall or occasional command-counter savings because the Van Hauge is usually a passive defender. *When it applies:* Light/Wave needs a reachable path; Sling Relay needs a worthwhile stall or second use. [SCPT 257 83:37.72–84:28.98 (s001282–s001292); SCPT 257 84:29.88–86:10.38 (s001293–s001316)]

**C31.** Dark Energy Tap is a gamble Yin may accept for frontier draws, because the guide especially values possible command counters and trade goods. Its one successful Shard of the Throne draw is presented as luck, not a repeatable plan. *When it applies:* Only if a frontier route makes the gamble worthwhile. [SCPT 257 81:12.50–82:26.88 (s001249–s001265); SCPT 257 98:09.14–98:44.00 (s001494–s001499)]

**C32.** During slice or map selection, favor several planets of the same type and useful attachments or tech skips to improve the number of public objectives Yin may qualify for. These slice features create eligibility or a chance at favorable reveals; they do not score an objective by themselves. *When it applies:* At slice selection, improve eligibility; the slice itself does not score an objective. [SCPT 257 99:22.14–100:59.12 (s001507–s001535)]

**C33.** Treat Mahact as a severe Yin counter in the guide’s assessment: its mobility can answer scattered hero planets, its hero can take the Van Hauge, and attacks can expose Yin’s commander for Mahact’s tech value. In a faction draft, the hosts warn against choosing Yin into that matchup. *When it applies:* When Mahact’s relevant mobility and leader tools are present; assess seats. [SCPT 257 101:21.66–102:10.44 (s001541–s001553); SCPT 257 102:10.66–103:25.52 (s001554–s001570)]

**C34.** In the last rounds, if Yin is behind, the guide suggests letting speaker control go, taking Trade when useful, blocking points and stretching the game without provoking annihilation. This is active spoiler play intended to buy time and disrupt opponents; it does not guarantee Yin points. *When it applies:* If behind late and disruption can slow opponents without provoking a punishing response. [SCPT 257 103:32.62–104:51.88 (s001573–s001591); SCPT 257 105:27.78–106:56.62 (s001601–s001623)]

**C35.** A rare Van Hauge–Milor interaction may help meet some space-combat secret objectives: the hosts propose letting the flagship explode, then using the agent to place fighters so Yin technically wins the combat. They call it an unusual opportunity, not a routine strategy. *When it applies:* Requires qualifying combat, available agent and a secret objective actually met. [SCPT 257 112:52.72–113:53.76 (s001724–s001734); SCPT 257 113:53.76–114:45.52 (s001735–s001745)]
