# Council Keleres strategy summary

**Scope.** This draft summarizes the Council Keleres guide in SCPT episode 472, published 2026-09-02. It is a podcast strategy guide, not a current-rules ruling. The episode itself calls its coverage a rough draft and leaves several Law’s Order and Executive Order interactions unresolved. Its source record marks compatibility unreviewed. Timestamps below are audio seconds; segment IDs identify the precise ASR passages.

## Strategic thesis

Council Keleres is an adaptable, technology-hungry faction whose ceiling comes from turning trade goods, agenda access, and repeated production into a strong late action phase. Its default path is to secure safe early production and access toward Mecatol Rex, then use the board position and plastic to contest the Fracture and Styx. This plan depends on having time to snowball and on the table allowing Keleres to establish itself. If opponents block the route or the game ends quickly, expensive tech and production may not turn into points. The speakers also advocate using Executive Order frequently despite its high variance, since a favorable agenda can supply money, points, or tempo unavailable through ordinary scoring. [C12 · 78:17–79:37 · s001223–s001246; C13 · 81:21–86:13 · s001267–s001347; C22 · 114:26–121:06 · s001821–s001935; C28 · 136:15–141:41 · s002189–s002287]

## Choose the Council variant for the table and slice

At setup, Council Keleres chooses one unused faction’s home system, tokens, and matching hero. Advice for one version does not automatically apply to the others. The episode transcript hears the Xxcha variant as “extra”; that mapping is inferred from the registered variants and the hero discussed, so retain the ASR caveat. The hosts favor the Mentak start overall. They describe the Xxcha start as a worthwhile alternative when the slice has resources but little influence, or when its added influence makes a credible round-one Custodians attempt easier. The Argent start is judged weakest: its three-planet home system gives awkward resource/influence totals and poor home production, while drawing attention to a fragile setup. These are the speakers’ ranking and strategic impressions, not universal performance guarantees. [C11 · 22:57–29:50 and 29:50–30:11 · s000334–s000454]

The three heroes are likewise variant-bound. The Argent hero can create a combat surprise by adding its flagship and up to two cruisers or destroyers at the start of a space combat in a system where Keleres controls a planet. One speaker recounts using it to save a forward dock and preserve scoring access, but the hosts generally find its low-impact ships, fleet-pool limit, and interaction with the flagship awkward. Treat it as a situational combat trick, not a reliable offensive plan. [C19 · 102:17–106:14 · s001627–s001694]

The Xxcha hero adds votes and can reward correct predictions about other players’ agenda choices. Executive Order gives it additional opportunities, but player decisions determine how much value it returns; the hosts correct their own explanation of timing during the discussion. The Mentak hero instead draws three action cards with component actions. That selection can be uneven, though even modest component actions can be useful. The speakers describe an exceptional line with Overrule, movement, Fleet Logistics, and a Fracture route as plausible but rare. Neither hero’s line should be read as advice for the other variants. [C20 · 107:49–111:04 · s001725–s001763; C21 · 111:18–114:26 · s001767–s001818]

## Round one: solve production and infantry

The opening challenge is turning Keleres’s starting money into a board position. Construction is a favored pick because its primary can build at home, repair the thin infantry start, and place a forward Space Dock that later supports Agency Supply Network. A straight, planet-rich slice helps; a dock adjacent to Mecatol is a bonus rather than a requirement. Avoid confusing the first forward dock with a PDS plan: the speakers are primarily looking for production and access. [C23 · 121:51–123:48 · s001947–s001972]

Technology can be an unusually high-priority first pick because Keleres has several valuable technologies and can tolerate following Warfare afterward to obtain infantry. The suggested first-round double-tech line depends on the selected home system, payment plan, and table’s Warfare pick; the speakers explicitly say it is too expensive for a round-one Mecatol grab. If Warfare is unlikely to be selected, any opening that relies on its secondary risks leaving Keleres short of plastic. One suggested Politics line can preserve starting commodities for the proposed round-one plan, while assuming someone else takes Warfare and accepting an infantry risk. [C24 · 123:54–126:44 · s001974–s002017; C25 · 127:05–129:43 · s002023–s002053]

Leadership becomes more appealing when a temporal anomaly scar makes its token valuable toward faction tech. Warfare is usually best spent on a home-system build rather than scattering the small starting force into multiple exposed systems. Trade remains useful after a separate action pays for the breakthrough, leaving its proceeds available for other builds. Diplomacy is a low-priority pick in the speakers’ view: Keleres already has money, and the primary does not solve its need to spend that money productively. Following Diplomacy can still make sense when its secondary fits the board. [C26 · 130:00–132:46 · s002062–s002107; C27 · 132:48–136:13 · s002111–s002184]

## Convert money into production, then into position

Council Patronage refreshes commodities at the start of the strategy phase and grants a trade good. Law’s Order can spend one commodity or trade good at the start of any player’s turn to treat laws as blank through that turn. These are separate tools: the first gives flexible funding; the second is a situational answer to a law. The guide suggests, for example, blanking Political Censure before playing Repeal Law, which its speakers say removes the action-card restriction while preserving the point, or preventing Minister of Peace from disrupting a planned winning attack. Confirm the applicable ruling and timing before relying on an interaction. [C01 · 33:34–35:10 · s000503–s000529; C03 · 37:54–45:11 · s000578–s000600 and s000697–s000704]

Using Law’s Order at the start of another player’s turn can create a table-etiquette problem when Keleres reacts after learning what that player intends to do. The episode does not settle what counts as angle-shooting and suggests agreeing on a convention. Its many examples are cherry-picked; most turns or games may not present a worthwhile use. [C04 · 41:04–44:42 · s000639–s000691]

Agency Supply Network lets a production resolution be followed by another of Keleres’s production abilities in any system. With multiple docks, it can distribute cheap plastic across the map, reinforce Mecatol, and prepare forward positions without activating each destination. Sarween Tools can make separate builds efficient because the hosts say its discount applies to each build. Yet production only matters when Keleres has time and resources to spend: in shorter Thunder’s Edge-era games, the network may come online after the decisive scoring window. The hosts still rate it highly, while lowering its status from an automatic first-priority technology. [C13 · 81:21–86:13 · s001267–s001347]

IHQ Modernization is presented as close to an automatic breakthrough: Keleres gains Custodia Vigilia, a 2-resource/2-influence legendary planet card, adjacency-based neighbor access to players near Mecatol, and two command tokens when another player scores Imperial’s second-clause point. The planet card enters exhausted. The guide distinguishes card-based checks from system or planet control: it says Custodia can help Push Boundaries and some resource/influence secrets, but not Expand Borders, control six planets outside home, or Making History’s system condition. These objective interactions are guide-reported and should be checked against exact objective wording. [C14 · 87:51–93:12 · s001375–s001463; C15 · 94:05–94:44 · s001484–s001490; C36 · 168:52–171:28 · s002785–s002824]

For technology, the guide’s core list is Gravity Drive, Executive Order, Agency Supply Network, Carrier II, and X-89. Bio-Stims is a strong accelerator; Sarween Tools is useful but not mandatory. Fleet Logistics is prized for an extra action in an action-phase win, Fighter II adds value to the many fighters Keleres builds, and Light/Wave Deflector may matter when late Fracture access is needed. Transit Diodes, Integrated Economy, and Space Dock II can also be useful, but there is not enough time to acquire every desirable technology. Space Dock II is most attractive when production is the real bottleneck or a low-production start needs help; if forced to pick a luxury technology, the speakers usually prefer Fleet Logistics. [C28 · 136:15–141:41 · s002189–s002287; C29 · 142:01–144:36 · s002297–s002332]

## Agendas, trade, and action timing

Executive Order exhausts its technology, reveals the top or bottom agenda card, and has players vote as if Keleres were speaker; Keleres may spend resources and trade goods as votes. The hosts advocate using it often, particularly with Bio-Stims, because a revealed agenda may create an unusual point, money, or tactical advantage. They also describe it as high variance: opponents vote first, the deck may offer nothing useful, and other players’ cards can change the result. Keep influence flexibility rather than assuming the desired outcome. [C08 · 53:05–54:49 · s000825–s000840; C12 · 78:17–79:37 · s001223–s001246]

Separate agenda resolution from scoring objectives. The speakers report that Representative Government does not prevent the Executive Order vote, that Drive the Debate cannot score during this action-phase agenda, and that when an agenda awards multiple victory points those points resolve in initiative order. They say effects whose timing belongs to another window happen later. Argent’s Zeal interaction is explicitly uncertain. These are claims from a rough-draft guide, so check exact card text and current rulings. [C09 · 57:33–59:52 · s000882–s000913]

The bottom of the agenda deck can be a resource: if Keleres expects another Executive Order, a useful agenda can be left there for a later reveal. This is only partial control. Other players and Politics can change what returns, and an early Executive Order gives opponents more time to interfere. Treat the plan as an option rather than a scheduled point. [C10 · 60:44–64:22 · s000931–s000995]

Keleres Rider creates a recurring trade opportunity after an agenda is revealed. The guide describes a common deal of one trade good without a guarantee or two if Keleres helps secure the buyer’s desired result. Executive Order and Bio-Stims can increase the number of windows; the hosts estimate four to eight trade goods in a favorable round, but explicitly say that depends on agendas and buyer demand. Negotiate early, since a late request for the Alliance may reveal that its value has become too high to trade safely. Opponents can contest free agenda-phase Rider value, though they may have less ability to block an action-phase vote. [C07 · 49:17–51:31 · s000775–s000791; C30 · 144:36–149:24 · s002334–s002436]

Suffi An’s commander rewards a component-action action card with an additional action after it is unlocked by spending a trade good following such a card. Keep the unlock distinction clear: the action used to unlock the commander does not itself grant the extra action. Afterward, component actions such as Executive Order can precede a tactical action when the timing and available cards allow. The commander may be more valuable to factions with reliable component actions than to Keleres, so price an Alliance according to what the recipient can use. [C16, C17, C18 · 96:54–101:58 · s001507–s001617]

## Trade partners and counters

Some deals amplify Keleres’s build engine. The guide calls Titans a valuable partner because terraforming Custodia Vigilia improves the planet and Titans’ Commander may add value alongside repeated Agency Supply Network builds; it proposes sharing trade goods as one possible price, without a universal fair amount. It also describes the Sardakk Alliance as enabling plastic to be built at a high-production dock and placed elsewhere. The exact interactions and current component wording are not independently verified here. [C31 · 150:00–152:37 · s002451–s002501; C32 · 154:02–155:43 · s002522–s002561]

Jol-Nar’s Research Agreement and Deepwrought technology access are presented as ways to spend Keleres’s money on technology. Winnu can contest Mecatol, but its Alliance can be excellent if Keleres arrives first; the hosts even suggest coordinating access to Mecatol and the Fracture. Empyrean can make repeated commodity trades profitable, while its Blood Pact may help opponents contest an Executive Order vote. Cabal’s Alliance can add fighters or infantry to builds, and Ghosts may be useful for Gravity Drive access. These are separate, table-dependent examples, not a required package. [C34 · 161:36–164:20 · s002652–s002707; C35 · 164:33–168:16 · s002714–s002777]

Mentak’s Pillage is a direct economic counter because Keleres expects frequent trade goods. If Mentak is an opponent, negotiate protection or alternative value before assuming that all income is available. The Mentak Council Keleres start itself is unavailable if Mentak is already in the game; this counter advice applies when Keleres has chosen another unused variant. The hosts consider the economy damaging but also debate whether Pillage income reliably converts into wins for Mentak. [C11 · 22:57–29:50 · s000334–s000454; C33 · 155:50–159:59 · s002562–s002642]

At the Fracture, Keleres may arrive later than early-access factions and have to dislodge a player already there. It can build substantial hit points but is not described as especially punchy. The guide names Barony’s Destroyer II fleet as a late-game counter, particularly because it may scale into the final round. The broader weakness is tech dependence: Keleres may need Light/Wave for the late route while also wanting Fleet Logistics or other tools, and cannot assume every needed technology will arrive in time. [C22 · 114:26–121:06 · s001821–s001935; C36 · 170:09–171:28 · s002808–s002824]

## Additional conditional advice

- **C02** — Trade Agreement is a credible bargaining chip because Council Patronage forces a commodity refresh at the start of the next strategy phase, letting its holder turn those commodities into trade goods if they choose. Best when the other player values the refreshed commodities and can transact; negotiate the agreement’s price and use rather than assuming guaranteed value in every table. (2166.14–2168.84s; s000545, s000550, s000552, s000553, s000554, s000555, s000556).
- **C06** — The flagship’s six capacity can support the faction’s growing plastic, while its two-influence activation tax and each Keleres mech’s separate one-influence landing tax can make key systems costly to invade; the speakers regard the mech tax as more central than the flagship. Defensive value matters when opponents need to activate or commit ground forces into the system/planet; available trade goods can soften the tax.
