# Nekro strategy summary — draft

**Thesis.** Nekro has a weak opening and must acquire technology indirectly, then turns opponents’ research into a flexible midgame toolkit. Repair the ship and infantry base, negotiate affordable combat access, and use stored money, cards, and technology to meet the objectives actually revealed. Keep a safe home system and more than one plausible final scoring route. Preparation is not scoring; confirm each objective’s prerequisites.

Source: SCPT 323, “Super Nekro Virus Guide,” published 2023-11-01. The guide says Nekro had no direct post-POK changes when recorded; compatibility with current rules is unreviewed. Claim IDs match the JSON. Times and segment IDs refer to the assigned ASR transcript.

## Opening and technology

### C01 — Opening plastic

Treat the opening as a recovery phase: the starting Dreadnought gives fleet presence, but only two infantry and one Carrier leave limited capacity and weak early ground reach; prioritize plastic and expansion before chasing a technology engine.

Evidence: s000304,s000305,s000308,s000309,s000310,s001644,s001645; 1160.62–1199.84s; 6166.10–6177.72s.

### C02 — Warfare priority

In round one, prefer another player taking Warfare; if no one will and the secondary is needed to repair the opening, take Warfare yourself, even when Trade is also attractive. Opponents can pressure Nekro by making it spend its own pick on Warfare.

Evidence: s000966,s000967,s000968,s000969,s000972,s000975,s000977,s001292,s001294,s001298,s001303; 3747.18–3797.36s; 4976.02–5016.68s.

### C03 — Plastic before token spending

Do not spend the round-one economy primarily on Leadership tokens or Technology-secondary research when the opening still needs ships and infantry. Nekro cannot research; a Technology primary/secondary converts the research step into three command tokens, so buying plastic can address the more immediate bottleneck.

Evidence: s001163,s001164,s001167,s001168,s001169,s001170,s001171,s001172,s000426,s000431,s000432,s000433,s000434,s000435,s000436,s000437,s000438,s000439; 4538.86–4575.56s; 1657.36–1752.10s.

### C04 — Trade when the opening allows

Trade is an excellent opening when it can fund plastic and payments that keep a target ship from retreating, but it is secondary to securing needed Warfare in the speakers’ recommended priority. Evaluate both cards against the opening bottleneck instead of treating Trade as automatic.

Evidence: s001285,s001287,s001288,s001289,s001292,s001297; 4957.46–5000.36s.

### C05 — A negotiated, gradual technology plan

Track opponents’ technology acquisitions and plan small, controlled combats around them. The guide recommends a ‘tortoise’ approach: negotiate a modest target ship, pay its owner not to retreat, then destroy it to gain the technology while avoiding an unnecessarily costly early invasion.

Evidence: s001019,s001020,s001021,s001022,s001023,s001026,s001027,s001028,s001029,s001030,s001034; 3971.42–4038.40s.

### C06 — Pay for the ship that stays

Make the no-retreat arrangement reciprocal: explain which ship Nekro wants to destroy, agree where the combat occurs, and compensate the opponent for losing the ship. The hosts reject the idea that opponents should donate technology access for free.

Evidence: s001600,s001601,s001602,s001604,s001605,s001606,s001607; 6000.08–6047.64s.

### C07 — Exploit combat timing

Use the timing of Technological Singularity to gain technologies from units destroyed during combat, including Anti-Fighter Barrage and separate space and ground combats in the same activation. Look for multi-combat activations when several opponents’ units can be destroyed, rather than expecting non-combat destruction to qualify.

Evidence: s000386,s000387,s000397,s000398,s000405,s000406,s000407; 1463.74–1509.12s; 1510.10–1573.48s.

### C08 — Anti-fighter barrage targets

Destroyer II is an attractive combat target because improved Anti-Fighter Barrage can destroy enemy units before later combat rounds and still trigger the technology ability; ordinary Destroyer II is useful even if Argent’s Strike Wing Alpha II is unavailable.

Evidence: s000413,s000414,s000415,s000416,s000418,s000420,s000421,s000422,s000423; 1590.86–1648.38s.

### C09 — Gain versus research

Distinguish gaining a technology from researching one: the guide says Nekro’s copied technologies do not require prerequisites, while effects that would research instead yield three command tokens. It contrasts this with effects that explicitly gain a technology, which can still grant one.

Evidence: s000426,s000431,s000432,s000433,s000434,s000435,s000436,s000437,s000438,s000439,s000440,s000441,s000442; 1657.36–1752.10s.

### C10 — Two Valefar slots

Treat Valefar Assimilator X and Y as two limited faction-text slots: on a qualifying technology gain, Nekro may put a token on an opponent’s faction technology and use its text. Replacing a copied unit-upgrade text can reveal the underlying upgrade again, so changing a token does not necessarily erase Nekro’s original upgrade.

Evidence: s000481,s000482,s000483,s000484,s000485,s000486,s000487,s000488,s000494,s000495,s000496,s000497,s000503,s000504,s000508,s000510; 1867.28–1936.78s; 1964.18–2000.20s.


## Fleet, leaders, and technology choices

### C11 — Choose the Alastor’s job

The Alastor can serve as a hard home defense or as an offensive threat. Its start-of-space-combat ability lets Nekro’s ground forces participate as ships while staying on their planets; its printed capacity is less central to that use. Decide early which role the flagship should serve, then use other production for fleets sent outward.

### C12 — Mechs with the flagship

Mordred’s combat bonus rewards fighting a player whose technology carries one of Nekro’s Valefar tokens. The Alastor’s ability to bring ground forces into space combat also makes carrying several Mordred mechs a substantial fleet option; the guide notes the token can be moved between combats to change which opponent triggers the bonus.

### C13 — Leader economy and action cards

Use Nekro Malleon’s action-phase agent each round for two trade goods when the chosen player discards an action card or spends a command token, and value Nekro Acidos’s action-card draw after later technology gains. Together, these abilities support both cash and tactical card access.

### C14 — A targeted hero gain

The hero offers a late tactical pivot: with Nekro units in a system, choose a planet with a technology specialty, destroy another player’s units there, gain trade goods based on that planet’s resource and influence values, and gain a matching-color technology. The guide recommends looking for a needed color or Integrated Economy through a yellow skip when the board supports it.

### C15 — Integrated Economy remains contested

Integrated Economy is contested. One contributor argues an early round-three copy can multiply ground combats, technology gains, action cards, and control-objective reach; the hosts argue that saving the hero until a stage-two objective appears gives valuable information and can preserve cash that is not yet held. They favor waiting for the objective, while acknowledging an early Integrated Economy game can work.

## Strategy cards and objectives

### C16 — Choose the prediction window

Use the prediction ability deliberately: it can be used once per agenda phase, so the hosts advise waiting past a low-value first agenda when a second agenda may offer a clearer For/Against prediction. They suggest seeking a roughly even split of votes when feasible and using Politics to influence which agendas appear.

Evidence: s000364,s000365,s000366,s000367,s000368,s000369,s000370,s000371,s000375,s000376,s000378,s000381,s000383,s000384; 1391.94–1463.32s.

### C17 — Agenda and speaker control

Because Nekro cannot vote, the hosts recommend Politics as a way to control speaker and see the agenda cards, while using action cards and prediction to manage agenda outcomes. They caution against assuming round-one Custodians: the starting fleet is weak for the race and Gravity Drive is not guaranteed.

Evidence: s001042,s001046,s001047,s001048,s001049,s001050,s001051,s001052,s001056,s001058,s001059,s001243,s001244,s001245,s001246,s001248,s001250,s001253,s001257; 4068.34–4135.18s; 4798.52–4873.94s.

### C18 — Leadership and Diplomacy

Nekro skips the Technology secondary, which can leave more strategy tokens for other secondaries; Leadership is therefore a lower-priority pick than for factions that need it to follow Technology. Diplomacy is situational, especially when readying a valuable planet helps meet a visible round-one objective.

Evidence: s001188,s001195,s001196,s001197,s001200,s001204,s001210,s001215,s001216,s001218,s001219,s001221,s001223,s001226,s001230; 4601.04–4685.68s; 4687.74–4783.44s.

### C19 — Construction as an investment

Construction is usually a low-priority pick, but Nekro can spend its extra command-token flexibility on structure objectives and follow Construction when it is offered. The hosts frame Politics-secondary action cards and trade goods as a partial substitute for the Technology secondary, not an equally essential replacement.

### C20 — Technology priorities

For technology priorities, the guide places Gravity Drive first among blue technologies, sees Bio-Stims and Neural Motivator as useful green options, and highlights Scanlink Drone Network and Predictive Intelligence as attractive yellow picks. Nekro’s command-token abundance can make Scanlink uses easier to afford, though Predictive Intelligence still offers late flexibility.

### C21 — Unit upgrades and combat tech

Acquire useful unit upgrades whenever opponents make them available; the guide especially calls out Dreadnought II, Carrier II, and sometimes Fighter II for movement and board coverage. Duranium Armor and Assault Cannon are situationally attractive, particularly when a hero gain or combat timing can make them useful immediately.

### C22 — Powerful faction texts

Treat faction unit-upgrade technologies as high-value Valefar targets because they add useful unit capabilities to a flexible fleet; the episode specifically praises Super Dreadnought II, Advanced Carrier II, Saturn Engine II, Strike Wing Alpha II, and Hybrid Crystal Fighter II. Other faction texts can be powerful but may create table-specific minigames or bargaining risks.

Look for utility faction texts that address a specific endgame need: the hosts cite Salvage Operations for late trade goods, Wormhole Generator for final-round access, and Aerie Hololattice for production. ## Scoring and endgame

### C24 — Diversify spare economy

Expect difficulty with Custodians and early points because the opening is slow and Nekro cannot dictate early technology access. Use the saved economy and command tokens to diversify into fragments, attachments, structures, and Politics when the board offers a credible scoring or denial opportunity.

Evidence: s001665,s001666,s001669,s001672,s001676,s001682,s001683,s001684,s001685,s001690,s001691; 6230.46–6299.94s; 6281.14–6313.86s.

### C25 — Score opportunities safely

Take accessible bonus points and build toward objectives rather than sandbagging out of caution, but do not divert so much plastic that the main position collapses. Technology stage-one objectives are easier later than in rounds one or two; control objectives become attractive after Nekro repairs its ship and infantry base.

Evidence: s001702,s001703,s001704,s001705,s001706,s001707,s001708,s001709,s001710,s001711,s001712,s001716,s001722,s001724,s001727,s001728,s001729,s001733,s001734; 6331.44–6415.06s; 6418.44–6455.72s.

### C26 — Late technology and spend objectives

Nekro can threaten stage-two technology and economic objectives late: the guide favors two and four technology colors when available and notes that the hero can fill a missing color; for spend objectives, reserve the hero’s unclaimed trade goods and action-card value as a surprise resource source.

Evidence: s001798,s001799,s001802,s001805,s001806,s001807,s001812,s001817,s001820,s001821,s001830,s001832,s001833; 6592.54–6639.58s; 6653.40–6713.38s.

### C27 — Control and structure objectives

After acquiring movement and combat upgrades, Nekro can contest control objectives with a broad mix such as Carrier II, Light/Wave Deflector, Cruiser II, mechs, and Dreadnought II. The guide sees this breadth as a late-game strength and recommends pursuing structure objectives when the investment is affordable.

Evidence: s001836,s001838,s001843,s001845,s001846,s001847,s001850,s001852,s001855,s001858,s001859,s001860,s001861; 6714.00–6798.52s; 6798.52–6817.56s.

### C28 — Secret objectives

Keep action-phase secret objectives that match Nekro’s evolving technology access; the guide recommends pivoting at the end when a newly copied technology completes a requirement. A faction-technology secret is treated differently: the hosts say Nekro must actually have Valefar copying faction text to satisfy it.

Evidence: s001872,s001877,s001878,s001879,s001880,s001881,s001882,s001884,s001885,s001886; 6847.68–6901.44s; 6903.02–6921.40s.

### C29 — A tempo-based flagship line

If Nekro has established a lead in tempo by rounds two or three, protect speaker control and consider a forward space dock with Sling Relay: the guide proposes using the Alastor offensively early, then returning it to home defense later. This combines flexible production with a hard-to-attack home system.

Evidence: s001890,s001891,s001892,s001893,s001894,s001895,s001896,s001897,s001898,s001900,s001901,s001902; 6927.14–6992.86s.

### C30 — Finish with multiple routes

The guide’s preferred finish is to stay on public-objective tempo, collect a bonus point when safely available, retain speaker for the late game, and protect home from a wind-slay. In the final round, a well-positioned Nekro may have multiple scoring routes, including a stage-two objective or relinquishing Support for the Throne if the remaining points still win.

Evidence: s001912,s001913,s001914,s001915,s001916,s001917,s001918,s001919,s001920,s001921,s001922,s001923,s001924; 7032.26–7112.14s.

## Disagreement and source limits

Integrated Economy timing is disputed: one contributor reports a successful early round-three plan, while the hosts favor waiting for the stage-two objective and its information; they still allow early use when repeated ground combats repay it. The offensive Alastor plan depends on movement access, production, and tempo. The hosts call several faction technologies powerful or amusing as strategic opinion, not rules status.
