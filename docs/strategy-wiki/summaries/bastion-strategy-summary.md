# Last Bastion strategy summary

**SCPT 441 · Last Bastion Beginner’s Guide · published 2026-01-28.** Draft synthesis of the complete ASR transcript. The hosts explicitly call their Thunder’s Edge analysis preliminary and expect rules clarifications and experience to change it. Timestamped speech is evidence for what the hosts said, not verification of current rules. No speaker attribution is claimed.

## Strategic thesis

Last Bastion’s game is an early economic and plastic problem followed by a midgame defense and scoring position. The guide’s central advice is to use **Liberate** and map-specific resource value to get the faction functional before chasing its dramatic combat “closers.” A sound slice, enough infantry, and a timely build can turn a weak start into a durable Mecatol Rex position; without those foundations, the faction’s late punch may arrive after the scoring window. The speakers do not present it as a reliable Custodians rush or a general Fracture explorer. They expect its strongest games to establish ground forces and production, then make a costly win-slay difficult. [C01–C06, C23–C25]

## Opening and economy

**Study the slice before choosing a line.** The speakers repeatedly call Last Bastion draft-sensitive: nearby planets matter both for raw resources and for whether Liberate readies them, while tech skips can change the opening technology route. Their slice examples are “Christmas land,” not a universal script. Tiamat’s double-yellow skip and a blue skip can make an Integrated Economy plan plausible; Tempesta plus a suitable influence planet can create a spectacular Mecatol route; a strong nearby planet that does not arrive ready may be less valuable than it appears. They recommend mapping the actual activations, infantry count, resources, influence, skips and station access before committing. [C05, C12, C23]

**Solve plastic before indulging the technology plan.** The group ultimately agrees that if round one forces a choice, building units and expanding usually beats following Technology. Last Bastion starts with only three infantry and a limited fleet; more plastic can take more planets and thereby improve both resource and influence access. They rate Construction highly because it adds a Helios space dock and can establish production; Trade is especially valuable because the economy is strained. Warfare can be the better pick or follow when the slice cannot be reached efficiently otherwise. These are priorities, not a fixed card draft: position, pick order, table timing, and available trade can reverse them. [C06–C11]

Politics is described as a round-one trap in the abstract: the hosts doubt that Last Bastion should sacrifice its early build and economy just to pursue Custodians. They favor developing, then taking Imperial when a scoring position is ready. One host says he would not recommend a Custodians attempt as the default, while the other does not offer a counterargument; this is the guide’s judgment, not a rule. [C08, C24]

Trade is urgent if the player can actually reach partners. The home space station raises commodity value and permits trades with other station controllers without adjacency, but the starting cruiser’s nebula movement restriction can leave a player isolated from ordinary partners. Confirm that someone can transact in time for the intended build; “Trade is money” only helps if the table and map produce accessible deals. [C03, C10]

**Starting technology has two defensible branches.** The hosts mostly favor a blue start—Antimass Deflectors or Dark Energy Tap—toward Gravity Drive, then Carrier II and Dreadnought II as needed, with Fleet Logistics or Light/Wave Deflectors as a later scoring or reach tool. The guest is more open to Sarween Tools when the slice has a blue skip or when early production and Integrated Economy matter. A breakthrough can supply a yellow prerequisite, which the speakers warn players not to miscount. If the breakthrough is missing, Sarween into Scanlink Drone Network is offered as a yellow alternative before returning to blue. There is a real disagreement over whether Technology can outrank Warfare in a suitable slice; neither path is unconditional. [C01, C12–C14]

Integrated Economy is a slice-specific alternative, not the default. The guest’s case needs the right yellow skips (the example is Tiamat), Sarween, a timely Technology primary and a sequence that lets Liberate ready a captured planet before the follow-on build. That can combine expansion with building units along the route. The other host accepts the case but stresses that the double-yellow skip is a large map-dependent ask; they also prefer Helios II when protecting and improving existing territory is the more realistic plan. [C13–C14, C23]

## Production, combat and position

Helios V1 raises its planet’s resource value and provides production; that makes the home planet less barren and makes later Helios investment valuable. The guide treats the V2 space-dock upgrade as the most important technology target for the faction: it further raises planet value and production. It is expensive in prerequisites and competes with movement, but the speakers believe its economy and defensive production solve more of Last Bastion’s core problem than an early speculative combat tech. The stated production numbers and component wording are transcript paraphrases, so check the current card before rules-sensitive play. [C14]

Use **Liberate** carefully. As described in the episode, on taking a planet the ability readies it if the planet has enough of your infantry relative to its resource value; otherwise it places an infantry. The speakers stress that the condition counts infantry, not all ground forces. Because Liberate and exploration are both “when” effects, their practical advice is usually to resolve the readying before exploring: a newly found attachment may raise the planet’s resource value and leave it unready if exploration came first. If you cannot take the planet, remember the infantry placement still matters to later expansion. Mecatol is a one-resource planet in the discussion, so one infantry can make its capture immediately useful through Liberate. [C04]

Galvanize turns a chosen surviving unit into a more dangerous combatant: tokens add a die to combat rolls and unit abilities, and Phoenix Standard offers a post-combat way to galvanize a participant even when the battle was lost, provided a suitable unit remains. The hosts emphasize concentrating this punch where ground control or a consequential fight matters; a token on a unit is a commitment, and the finite tokens cannot be treated as freely movable. Ground forces help hold a hard-won position. The episode compares this localized strength to Sol and Sardakk, but describes Mecatol as the more natural midgame objective than a reliable early Fracture expedition. [C15–C16]

Proxima Targeting VI is situational. Its bombardment protection and repeated bombardment can be valuable in a long, prepared ground defense—especially with galvanized units concentrated at Mecatol or another position the player must hold. The hosts reject the idea that it is automatically bad, but say it needs a specific opportunity already in view and substantial setup. They explicitly lack a settled ruling for the interaction with X-89 Bacterial Weapon; do not treat the speculative combination as established. [C17]

The Egeiro flagship is presented as an occasional tool, not a priority purchase: its one die, movement and cost make it hard to justify, though its production can replace a carrier or add a small unit at a forward position. The A3 Valiance mech’s death-triggered infantry galvanizing may strengthen a critical ground defense, but the hosts question whether token availability and the same-system restriction will let it matter often. The hero can punish a large enemy force when a galvanized unit is destroyed, but the episode’s examples include unresolved questions about what counts as combat value and which targets qualify. Treat the threat as a deterrent and verify wording before relying on a particular interaction. [C18–C19]

## Leaders and table deals

Dame Briar can galvanize another unit of the same player in the destroyed unit’s system. The speakers suggest that using the agent on another player’s combat can help unlock the commander: Nip and Tuck unlocks once three galvanized units are on the board. In their reading, the commander’s most valuable protection is that action cards cannot be canceled by Sabotage; this can secure a decisive card play, but the unlock timing is not guaranteed. The speakers also mention the Necrovirus simulator-token clause without a firm strategic use. [C20]

The Alliance and Raise the Standard are both pitched early to fund the weak start, and the speakers discuss selling the Alliance to gain needed plastic or an alliance swap. Price depends on timing and whether the money gets the player online: they float roughly three to four trade goods in a round-one example and around six or a Support for the Throne in a round-two example, not as a market tariff. A later Alliance sale can hand a rival Sabotage-proof action-card use, so weigh the buyer’s threat and avoid casually empowering a likely winner. Deepwrought or Crimson swaps are discussed as attractive access to a blue skip or other value, while those factions may also become more dangerous. Raise the Standard can be sold repeatedly outside its timing window according to the discussion, but the specific transaction and timing should be checked at the table. [C21–C22]

## Scoring, counters and limits

The speakers do not identify a dependable objective category that Last Bastion simply excels at; their conclusion is that the kit is Mecatol-focused and the early economy is poor. Use the faction’s expansion and production to meet the public objectives actually revealed, and take a scoring window when the engine is ready. Do not mistake occupying Mecatol for a point: the advice is to develop first, then pursue Imperial or another available score with adequate ground and fleet support. Their stories of a fortified Mecatol resisting a table-wide win-slay describe a possible endgame state after the economy, position and scoring tempo are solved—not immunity or a guarantee. [C08, C16, C24–C25]

Opponents can attack before the “closers” are online. The guide’s counterplay implication is temporal: pressure the weak opening, contest key planets and deny the setup before galvanized units, production and scoring position accumulate. Conversely, Last Bastion should avoid spending its early turns on speculative movement or technology if doing so leaves too little plastic to secure its slice. The speakers themselves admit their preferences for tempo and experimental technology can distract from this faction’s more patient setup. [C06, C25]
