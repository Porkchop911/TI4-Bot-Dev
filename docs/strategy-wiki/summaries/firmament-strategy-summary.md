# Firmament / Obsidian strategy summary — SCPT 449

The Firmament makes plots and scores setup objectives; the Obsidian inherits those plots and converts them into economy, technology, and situational combat effects. The hosts usually favor a round-three transition, while acknowledging round-two payoff lines and rare reasons to wait. They describe a hard opening with a high-execution ceiling. Citations point to ASR passages; rules interpretations remain unverified.

## Opening and the flip puzzle

**C01 — Choose starting technology around your actual route.** With no prerequisites, the green/yellow start is awkward. If aiming for Plane Splitter’s green/yellow route, Psychoarchaeology can support the green side; with a useful blue skip in the slice, the hosts prefer Psychoarchaeology. If not pursuing the breakthrough route, they lean Sarween Tools for early plastic despite calling it a weak technology, or Neural Motivator when Sarween is not needed. A double yellow skip can supply the yellow faction technology; a green or red skip makes Sarween the discussed fallback. Do not treat one opening as universal. [20:34–22:29; s000268–s000294]

**C02 — Protect round-one economy and basic reach.** The hosts call the starting force and two-planet, three-resource home system exceptionally weak: technology and plastic compete for scarce resources, and Warfare can leave too little for infantry or fleet. Compensate before chasing speculative faction lines. [22:46–24:01; s000299–s000311] [24:31–27:54; s000315–s000369]

**C03 — Do not flip just for six home-system resources.** Chronos and Talon change from three resources and three influence together to six resources and no influence. The hosts reject an automatic round-one flip because influence funds command tokens, especially later; they concede the resource spike can tempt in round one or a resource-poor slice. [25:12–27:16; s000329–s000361]

**C04 — Treat the flip as a conversion with a timing cost.** At least one plot is required. The faction purges its Firmament sheet, leaders, planets, and note for the Obsidian package; the home system and in-play plots flip and ready if controlled. Spend home planets first, and plan for lost Firmament effects and unlocks. Early flip extends plot value; the hosts’ default medium plan first builds a useful package. [28:09–32:20; s000371–s000440] [32:20–36:03; s000440–s000467]

**C05 — Enervate is a timing-dependent bonus, not a reason by itself to rush.** It follows puppeted players’ strategy-card secondaries without command tokens, or Leadership’s primary instead. The hosts’ practical line is to place it on the Leadership player with the Firmament hero immediately before flipping, if Leadership remains unplayed, to try for three extra tokens. If Leadership has already resolved or is likely to resolve before the hero, they recommend ignoring this plot; the token payoff is not reliable. [34:00–36:03; s000443–s000467]

## Plots worth planning around

**C06 — Siphon is strongest with a reliable source of commodities.** Each time the target gains commodities, gain the same number of trade goods. The hosts favor Hacan and call a round-one pairing a possible exception to their slower flip plan. Crimson’s unlocked commander can also trigger it after combat; its Alliance may add more. This still does not grant Hacan’s wider toolkit. [36:05–38:18; s000469–s000500] [45:46–46:20; s000612–s000623]

**C07 — Sieve is targeted denial whose value rises late.** It destroys all units on one target-controlled non-home planet when revealed, then one of that player’s infantry each Status Phase. A valuable planet or late-game clear can justify it, but units can move before the flip, wasting an early commitment. Hosts do not prioritize it for a medium flip. [38:20–40:15; s000503–s000528]

**C08 — The combat plot (ASR name uncertain) is a called shot.** It adds one to combat and unit-ability rolls against the target. Use it for a likely fight or possible deterrence; hosts compare it situationally with Sieve. It also removes one target infantry at Status Phase. The name sounds like “a sale”; verify the printed name. [41:46–43:35; s000548–s000567]

**C09 — Extract is their highest-priority plot.** On reveal it gains one non-faction technology of the target; when that player later gains another, spend four resources to copy it. Target a likely researcher of technology you want but cannot reach—hosts name Nekro, Deepwrought, Jol-Nar, X-89, Light/Wave Deflector, Fleet Logistics, and early Carrier II. Check current tech and breakthrough first. [43:39–45:46; s000569–s000611]

**C10 — Pair Extract with Siphon when the table makes both productive.** Put Extract on a flexible technology faction and Siphon on a commodity-rich one, then use the hero to add or double a productive plot. The hosts present this as an aspirational medium-flip package; trade timing, target research, and player cooperation can all break it. [45:46–46:49; s000604–s000623] [92:19–94:04; s001217–s001243]

**C11 — Black Ops can buy a plot and help the recipient.** The recipient gets two command tokens and a trade-good amount the ASR renders as the word to while Firmament places a plot with that player's token; purge the note. Hosts expect acceptance and advise requesting Siphon or Extract. Verify the printed trade-good count. [56:37–60:32; s000752–s000806]

**C12 — Malevolency is mostly leverage and disruption.** After a tactical action, Obsidian may spend one influence to pass the note to a neighbor; at Status Phase a non-Obsidian holder loses one fleet-pool token. Hosts disagree on its value. It may pressure a neighbor with few tactical tokens left, but is not dependable point denial. [60:57–64:09; s000810–s000855]

**C13 — The early secret-objective engine depends on turn order and cooperation.** Firmament can copy a secret already scored by another player if it meets the requirement, placing a plot with that player’s token rather than earning a point. Because initiative order determines whose secret becomes available first, scoring after another player can create a plot sooner; Leadership may score before a target, while Imperial can put Firmament later. Share secret information and negotiate reciprocal disclosure or the note when useful, but assess the secret and Sieve risk rather than assuming every secret should be exposed. [28:04–29:40; s000371–s000390] [105:08–107:14; s001108–s001129] [105:20–106:50; s001387–s001405] [110:30–111:55; s001455–s001470]

**C14 — The round-two hero line exists, but requires an exceptional scoring sequence.** The Firmament hero moves a plot to a new player and moves a player token onto another plot, allowing an extra placement and a duplicate economic target across two plots. The hosts’ minimum round-three plan is two public objectives plus a secret, then use the hero before flipping; copying another player’s secret can count toward the objective threshold if the rest of the scoring requirements are met. A round-one copied secret plus a public and personal secret might unlock the hero for round two, but they call this a cooperative, high-roll route—not the baseline. [92:19–96:18; s001217–s001276]

## Tech, leaders, and components

**C15 — Plane Splitter can open a fracture, but inspect who benefits first.** Research it while still Firmament: gaining the technology puts the fracture into play and distributes ingress tokens to Mecatol Rex or Thunder’s Edge (if present) and to technology-skip systems. The hosts favor researching it late before the flip if the fracture has not appeared, so the board and likely recipients are clearer. They caution that movement factions may exploit the new access more easily than Firmament; a slice without useful skips or a hostile ingress allocation weakens the plan. [65:05–68:23; s000857–s000904] [68:47–69:22; s000911–s000923]

**C16 — The Obsidian Plane Splitter can reposition an ingress token.** At the start of strategic actions, move an ingress token into a system containing or adjacent to your units. The Obsidian side cannot be researched, so hosts advise researching it as Firmament first; gaining it by another effect after flipping gives only the Obsidian side, not the fracture-opening effect. They suggest reclaiming a route or denying a leader’s route to the fracture; movement and access remain prerequisites. [69:53–71:34; s000929–s000954]

**C17 — Neural Parasite flips from a weak economy tool into a hostile, risky one.** The Firmament side can add one infantry to a controlled home-system planet at Status Phase while Obsidian is in play; hosts question the value of investing in this before a flip. Obsidian instead destroys another player’s infantry in or adjacent to a system containing yours at the start of your turn. They warn that repeatedly targeting one neighbor can provoke heat and leave that player unable to participate, without advancing your own win. They mainly consider researching it for the fracture-opening effect of an Entropic Scar. [72:13–75:54; s000955–s001001]

**C18 — Keep the ordinary blue path in view; use Extract for supplements.** Their default is Antimass Deflectors or Dark Energy Tap, Gravity Drive, Carrier II, Dreadnought II, and Fleet Logistics as needed. A more adventurous green/yellow route can cover Plane Splitter and other options if a target’s future research is dependable. They warn against relying on Extract for core blue prerequisites if the chosen player already owns or is unlikely to research them; establish movement and a viable blue route first. [106:50–109:12; s001406–s001438] [109:21–110:16; s001439–s001454]

**C19 — Captain Aroz can bridge a narrow secret-objective gap.** While Firmament, the commander treats planets in systems containing your ships as controlled for secret scoring. The hosts say it can rescue difficult planet-type or resource/influence objectives when a ship can reach the system; the ability is niche, and their proposed Become a Martyr interaction with the Firmament agent is explicitly uncertain pending a ruling. Flipping resets the commander unlock, so do not assume the effect survives as Obsidian. [86:48–90:28; s001133–s001193]

**C20 — Sharsiss is the key Firmament plot multiplier.** The hero should be timed after enough objectives are scored to unlock it and before the transition, so its new placement and reassignment can shape the Obsidian’s active plots. The hosts’ preferred output is an extra Siphon or Extract placement; they dislike spending the hero on a weaker plot, while acknowledging a round-two play can be decisive if the cooperative secret-score sequence happens. [92:19–96:18; s001217–s001276]

**C21 — Sharsiss Hollow can refresh the Obsidian economy.** The Obsidian hero readies all planets and is purged. On the flip round, the home system can be spent before the flip, readied by the flip ability, spent again, then readied again by the hero and spent a third time. This is a strong resource burst when timed early enough to use it, though the hosts note a late flip can leave more resources than there are useful actions. Extract is one outlet for the resulting resources. [97:12–98:31; s001277–s001293] [98:32–99:06; s001294–s001303]

**C22 — Myru Vos’s Firmament form helps movement and secrets; Vos Hollow has different timing.** The Firmament agent can protect moving ships from Space Cannon when they are not transporting units and can let them pass through enemy ships; hosts report selling this to Ral Nel for a high-movement destroyer play, but say the use is situational. After flipping, Vos Hollow can force an opponent to destroy another ship of the same type during a combat elsewhere. It offers surprise targeted attrition, but both forms are matchup-dependent and the hosts disagree over which side they would rather retain. [81:20–83:20; s001063–s001079] [82:48–85:14; s001080–s001106]

**C23 — Preserve Firmament mechs when their defensive role still matters.** Viper EX-23 permits coexisting ground forces when units are committed to the planet; if still coexisting when it flips, control transfers and the other player’s units become the coexisting ones. The hosts value Firmament’s version as a difficult-to-take home-system defense and see a very narrow offensive use: take a key planet, flip, and exploit the changed control—potentially with Imperial. This is a late-flip incentive, not a routine capture plan. [52:36–53:46; s000699–s000707] [53:34–55:50; s000709–s000748]

**C24 — Do not build the Obsidian flagship for its extra die alone.** Heaven’s Eye has the more useful plot-dependent movement and repair text, while its Obsidian side gains a die but loses that text. The hosts question its value given the faction’s economy, and say a War Sun may offer more practical functionality in an unusually rich game. The Firmament flagship may be attractive only if its text is central to a plan; otherwise the build is hard to justify. [48:05–50:17; s000648–s000667] [50:17–51:07; s000667–s000683]

## Strategy-card priorities and uncertainty

**C25 — In round one, seek money first and arrange Warfare support.** If speaker, the hosts usually prefer Trade, then Technology, with Diplomacy a poor fallback because of scoring order; they hope another player takes Warfare, as Firmament often cannot afford the secondary. Leadership can be right when tokens or a token objective demand it, and Warfare can fit niche objectives, but these are exceptions. Construction is a desperate alternative when a build is essential. [101:36–103:51; s001319–s001360] [103:51–104:55; s001361–s001386]

**C26 — Technology’s round-one value includes information and secret timing.** It may be useful even when the first technology is not ideal: going later in initiative can let Firmament observe and copy a secret scored earlier, and plan its own secret and Black Ops trade. This interacts with the initiative-order risks above, so value depends on speaker order, which other cards are taken, and whether anyone will share or score a usable secret. [105:20–106:50; s001387–s001405]

**C27 — Fracture access and movement shape the draft.** The hosts want a nearby Scar and a green or yellow skip as optional support for Plane Splitter, while advising against treating that line as guaranteed. In most games they return to blue movement and fleet technology; if Extract reliably supplies blue tech, a green/yellow route becomes more plausible. Slice, table technology choices, and actual ingress placement decide which plan is safe. [106:50–110:16; s001406–s001454]

**C28 — The timing disagreement remains real.** One host’s abstract advice moves toward flipping early so plots operate longer and the Obsidian hero can recycle planets; the other favors round three after two public scores, a personal secret, and preferably both Siphon and Extract. Round two is an exceptional payoff line; round four may make sense for a specific Sieve or mech use, or Plane Splitter timing, but risks missing economic benefits. Round-one wins are acknowledged but not treated as repeatable evidence. [32:20–34:00; s000435–s000442] [92:19–100:45; s001217–s001317]
