# Ghosts of Creuss strategy summary (draft)

**Source:** SCPT 326, “Super Ghosts of Creuss Guide,” published 2023-11-22. Complete ASR transcript derived from the verified transcript pointer in the source-segments header. Speakers are unidentified. Exact cited intervals and overlapping segment IDs point to the recording; ASR and publication date do not establish current rules compatibility. This is strategic source synthesis, not rules validation.

Ghosts trade a shaky early fleet and exposed wormhole network for exceptional movement flexibility and a powerful late-game finish. The hosts’ recurring advice is to make early choices that leave enough plastic and income to survive, keep the table willing to use the IFF, then exploit the hero and returning wormholes near the end. This works best in a cooperative, trade-friendly table. In a hostile game, the faction can still function, but its midgame economy and movement are less forgiving.

# Mobility, opening position, and the core tradeoff

**C01 — Treat Ghosts as a movement faction with weaker midgame development but unusually strong late-game closing options.** The guide’s comparison is specifically to Cabal; the advantage depends on surviving the middle rounds and building enough plastic. (6:47.49–7:30.81 [s000095, s000098, s000100])

**C02 — Use Quantum Entanglement to treat alpha and beta wormhole systems as mutually adjacent, while remembering that this ability does not include gamma or delta wormholes.** This is the hosts’ reading of the faction ability; they flag expansion-specific exceptions and an unresolved edge case about Aetherstream. (15:32.66–16:16.82; 17:18.88–18:05.36 [s000243, s000249, s000251, s000267, s000271])

**C03 — Exploit Slipstream’s extra movement from home or alpha/beta wormhole systems, but do not count on it from the Creuss Gate or gamma wormholes.** The guide treats this restriction as strategically important; moving the flagship’s delta system does not make it a Slipstream start point. (19:02.22–20:42.16 [s000313, s000315, s000292–s000294])

**C04 — Compensate for the narrow starting fleet by prioritizing production and reinforcing the systems you can reach through the wormhole network.** Ghosts begin with one carrier, two destroyers, two fighters and a space dock; spreading out without support can leave forces stranded. (11:39.28–12:22.24; 56:38.00–58:17.47 [s000173, s000175, s000827, s000835, s000846])

# Round-one plan and strategy cards

**C05 — Consider an early Malice take using the agent: the hosts describe sending the starting carrier there, flipping Malice for two trade goods, and gaining access to an extra early income source.** This is a common opening option, not an obligation; sometimes waiting or choosing other reachable systems is better, and the extra trade good needed for another carrier still matters. (34:36.22–36:36.84 [s000511, s000519, s000523, s000526, s000529, s000531])

**C06 — In round one, look for a third dollar or trade good beyond home and Malice if you want to afford a second carrier while also taking Sling Relay.** Industrial or hazardous planets, favorable Trade timing, or a neighbor’s exchange can close the gap; if resources are scarce, change the opening rather than assume the purchase works. (67:03.06–68:14.90 [s000969, s000977, s000979, s000981, s000984])

**C07 — Take Leadership as a safe opening when you need to secure the two-system expansion, command tokens, and flexibility to follow Technology, Diplomacy or Warfare.** It protects a baseline round more than it guarantees an exceptional one; its ceiling rises if other cards and trade timing line up. (68:20.88–69:59.86; 70:36.80–70:54.20 [s000988, s001000, s001006, s001008, s001014])

**C08 — Prefer Trade when an early pick is available: it can fund Malice, the second carrier, technology and additional production in one round.** The hosts describe the biggest upside when Ghosts pick first or second and receive favorable commodity exchanges; availability and table terms determine the actual return. (79:45.62–81:38.42 [s001149, s001151, s001153, s001157, s001161, s001177])

**C09 — Technology is a comfortable opening when timing matters: take Sling Relay, use it to build a dreadnought that can reach Malice, and keep the carrier available for another system.** This line is presented as stable rather than spectacular; it is useful when Trade is unavailable and the action sequence fits. (84:06.82–84:40.86 [s001213, s001216, s001218, s001220, s001224])

**C10 — Take Warfare when a hostile or unreliable table makes expansion and production timing too risky; Ghosts can rebuild at home and still send the new plastic toward useful systems.** This may mean delaying first-round technology, but the hosts consider early plastic more important than preserving theoretical card value when expansion would otherwise fail. (83:25.40–83:58.70; 86:06.42–86:16.74 [s001201, s001204, s001207, s001252])

**C11 — Treat Diplomacy as timing-sensitive: take a valuable system before refreshing it when possible, and coordinate with Technology and Warfare players before selecting the card.** If Malice is the only system available to refresh alongside home, Diplomacy can be poor; a different early planet may make the card worthwhile. (71:03.64–72:35.28; 73:04.40–73:29.90 [s001019, s001021, s001028, s001031, s001047, s001050])

**C12 — Use Politics selectively in round one: income from an agreed speaker sale and speaker control can set up round two, but it risks postponing urgent Ghosts problems and a Custodians opportunity.** The hosts favor it when Trade and table timing can support the opening, not as the default fix for limited plastic or a narrow first expansion. (74:08.12–75:14.52; 75:38.00–76:14.78 [s001063, s001066, s001070, s001075, s001083, s001089])

**C13 — Choose Construction when a forward dock is genuinely needed and Diplomacy would mainly benefit other players; otherwise favor immediate plastic or another opening goal.** The hosts like a forward system with negotiated wormhole access and a dock but warn that round-one Sling Relay plus a dock can overconcentrate resources in infrastructure. (78:22.84–79:45.62 [s001119, s001122, s001126, s001134, s001139, s001147])

# Table politics, IFF, leaders, and defense

**C14 — Adopt a friendly, flexible IFF posture because other players’ use of your wormholes can create access and bargaining opportunities for Ghosts.** Early and midgame openness can encourage trades and divert opponents toward each other; late game may call for a specific placement, and wormhole access also exposes Ghosts to attack. (33:14.36–34:32.38; 62:24.28–63:11.96 [s000491, s000500, s000504, s000506, s000509, s000910, s000916])

**C15 — Use Creuss Iff when the recipient can place or move a wormhole to a planet they control or a non-home system without another player’s ship; it is most useful for seeding future Ghost routes before ships occupy the intended systems.** The hosts emphasize that a player cannot use the note in a system containing another player’s ship unless the planet-control option applies; explain the restrictions before trading it. (30:25.36–31:43.30; 101:01.60–101:28.90 [s000457–s000458, s000464, s000469, s001507, s001509])

**C16 — Unlock Sai Seravus by establishing units in three alpha/beta wormhole systems, and use its ability to add fighters when capacity ships move through wormholes into the active system.** The fighter appears per eligible ship, not per wormhole; the destination must be the active system and unused capacity must remain. The hosts describe Carrier II and adequate fleet supply as helpful for making the ability pay off. (37:42.92–38:39.18; 38:44.36–40:42.60 [s000548, s000550, s000554, s000556, s000560, s000575, s000581])

**C17 — Place mechs to protect planets and preserve ground-force coverage; treat the Icarus Drive’s wormhole-token move as an occasional late-game option rather than its main job.** Removing the mech sacrifices a ground unit and can weaken defense; the hosts estimate they use the effect only a small number of times and suggest it is less attractive when IFF or Wormhole Generator suffices. (27:25.68–29:17.16; 61:10.18–61:28.22 [s000417, s000428, s000433, s000438, s000888, s000890])

# Technology priorities

**C18 — Make Carrier II an early technology priority because capacity and movement support wormhole jumps and the commander’s fighter generation.** The hosts usually place it after round-one Sling Relay, and regard its value as greatest when Ghosts can regularly move through wormholes; exact path depends on skips and available Technology picks. (89:55.78–92:32.06 [s001321–s001322, s001351–s001352, s001354])

**C19 — Keep the technology plan shallow: prioritize Sling Relay or Dark Energy Tap for the opening, Carrier II, then Light/Wave Deflector; additional blue tech is optional rather than a reason to delay ships.** The hosts say DET is attractive when Ghosts can survive without opening Sling Relay, and Light/Wave’s value is strongest because Ghosts need to concentrate limited combat power where it matters. (88:59.38–92:55.54; 93:42.10–93:58.06 [s001303, s001305, s001321, s001350, s001355, s001364, s001387])

**C20 — Consider Fighter II as a useful supplement: fighters generated during wormhole movement can remain behind as a defensive screen, reducing the need to build fighters directly.** The ability requires eligible capacity movement and unused capacity; fighter upgrades are optional if Ghosts have more urgent plastic or tech needs. (38:51.96–39:37.94; 95:10.44–96:10.84 [s000562, s000566, s001410, s001414, s001426])

**C21 — Treat Dimensional Splicer as a situational defensive bonus rather than a required red-tech detour; the speakers differ on how much its start-of-combat hit changes outcomes.** Research it when a cheap red skip or free agenda-phase opportunity makes the cost small and wormhole systems need extra defense; prioritize ships and core movement first. (48:15.84–50:22.08; 50:22.08–52:04.00; 96:19.26–97:07.36 [s000689, s000692, s000700, s000717, s000720, s000722, s000738, s001430, s001441])

**C22 — Research Wormhole Generator when table cooperation is drying up or Ghosts need independent late-game wormhole placement; it can also create a stall and set up the hero.** The hosts call it optional, often a round-four or round-five purchase if extra resources remain; weigh its cost against a mech’s cheaper one-off relocation and the opening technology priorities. (52:04.00–56:34.98 [s000744, s000750, s000754, s000760, s000768, s000779, s000786, s000795, s000801, s000812, s000821])

# Hero timing, objectives, and economy

**C23 — Save Riftwalker Meian for a high-impact system swap: the hero can create access for control objectives, move units into reach, or expose a target to attack.** The hero unlocks after three scored objectives; the swapped systems must contain wormholes or units and cannot be the Creuss home system or Wormhole Nexus. Plan around the actual board and objective requirements. (42:21.20–43:30.10; 43:34.86–46:58.28 [s000607, s000610, s000615, s000617, s000624, s000628, s000632, s000634, s000668, s000671])

**C24 — Use the hero’s late-game reliability to prepare difficult control objectives instead of spreading forces thin to qualify for every possible objective early.** The guide cites Rule Distant Lands and Achieved Supremacy as examples; partial setup is not scoring, and the necessary final control and objective conditions must still be met. (112:56.22–114:12.34; 115:22.12–116:00.66 [s001670, s001674, s001677, s001680, s001685, s001689, s001714, s001722])

**C25 — Stay friendly while sharing routes, but avoid making your own territory an easy target: seed a compact, mutually reinforcing wormhole network and defend valuable systems.** The hosts recommend choosing one objective to secure rather than thinly contesting every point of the board; expect that some attacks are the cost of offering wormhole access. (129:01.64–133:00.60 [s001936, s001943, s001951, s001955, s001968, s001982, s001989, s001993])

**C26 — Value Malice as an early economic foothold, but plan for retreat or eventual loss rather than over-defending it at the expense of stronger wormhole-linked bases.** The hosts expect its early trade goods and access to help, while later preferring high-value systems that reinforce Ghosts’ economy and defensive network. (63:20.60–64:09.52; 111:22.02–112:42.74 [s000920, s000923, s000927, s001649, s001653, s001660, s001663])

# Agenda risks and endgame

**C27 — Treat wormhole-related agendas as major survival risks: the hosts favor opposing or burying Wormhole Research and Wormhole Reconstruction because their against outcomes can destroy ships or lock systems with command tokens.** The risk is greatest when valuable fleets or home positions depend on alpha/beta wormholes; the guide acknowledges that another player’s assets in wormhole systems can make opposition easier. (117:41.02–119:36.12; 119:33.90–120:32.76 [s001754, s001760, s001763, s001767, s001781, s001785, s001789, s001793])

**C28 — Enforced Travel Ban can favor Ghosts if passed because Quantum Entanglement still preserves Ghosts’ alpha/beta adjacency while other players lose movement through those wormholes.** Its against outcome can destroy PDS, so consider objectives and structures before prioritizing it; the hosts generally advise a Politics player to bury the three dangerous wormhole agendas. (120:54.16–123:39.46 [s001805, s001807, s001815, s001818, s001820, s001828, s001832, s001838])

**C29 — Nexus Sovereignty is favorable in either outcome as the hosts describe it: it limits movement through Wormhole Nexus alpha/beta holes or places a gamma wormhole at Mecatol Rex, creating a route for the agent.** This is especially welcome if Ghosts hold Malice; a gamma link to Mecatol can make Fleet Logistics more attractive for a hero-and-action finish. (123:39.46–125:07.58 [s001839, s001844–s001845, s001849, s001860, s001862, s001864])

**C30 — Do not assume Ghosts will win the Custodians race; the hosts see early access as possible but less reliable than using movement, Imperial timing, or a strong late-game objective push.** Starting influence and table timing still matter despite Malice and wormhole mobility; this is a long-run tendency, not a prediction for every map or game. (136:23.90–137:48.82 [s002051, s002055, s002061, s002063, s002065, s002068])

# Supported component interactions

- `leader:ghostagent` enables `faction:ghost`: The agent’s early tactical adjacency makes the described home-to-Malice opening possible. (34:36.22–36:29.66).
- `technology:cv2` synergizes-with `leader:ghostcommander`: Carrier II’s capacity and movement complement the commander’s wormhole-triggered fighter generation. (38:44.36–40:49.74).
- `technology:wg` enables `ability:slipstream`: Wormhole Generator can place a route at a useful origin for Slipstream movement. (52:35.44–52:49.36).
- `technology:fl` synergizes-with `leader:ghosthero`: Fleet Logistics supports the immediate follow-up action after a hero swap. (97:13.44–98:15.94).
- `promissory:iff` enables `leader:ghostcommander`: A partner can use IFF to help establish the wormhole systems needed for the commander unlock. (41:12.10–42:15.22).
- `promissory:iff` conflicts-with `unit:ghost_mech`: The mech’s ability to relocate a wormhole can undercut buyer confidence in an IFF deal; trust and timing matter. (29:23.52–30:07.62).

The hosts disagree about Dimensional Splicer’s impact, Wormhole Generator’s value in a friendly game, and how reliably Ghosts can pursue Custodians. Map, table cooperation, skips, and objective timing change these choices. The guide is from 2023 and does not check later errata or current rules. Speech recognition is uncertain for several names; component names here follow the supplied reference where available.
