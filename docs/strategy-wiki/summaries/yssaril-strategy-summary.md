# Yssaril Tribes strategy summary

Draft from SCPT episode 358, “Super Yssaril Tribes Guide” (published 2024-07-03). Ruleset compatibility is unreviewed. The complete faster-whisper small.en transcript is a verified ASR draft; timestamps point to overlapping speech, but proper nouns are noisy.

## Strategic thesis

### C01

**Advice:** The action-card engine compounds over time: cards answer problems across movement, combat, economy, technology, and agendas, while Scheming lets Yssaril prune toward useful options. The hosts describe a tailored late-game toolkit, not a guaranteed win.

**When it applies:** Best over a longer game; results still depend on cards, board, scoring pace, and player decisions.

**Evidence:** 43:39.81–43:47.65 (s000853); 43:50.47–43:54.09 (s000855); 45:12.99–45:15.07 (s000880); 45:16.77–45:19.03 (s000882).

## Priorities and options

### C02

**Advice:** The opening has two carriers, a cruiser, two fighters, five infantry, and a home PDS. The hosts call this broadly useful but describe the early ground position as thin until mechs can be deployed.

**When it applies:** Expand with the fleet while accounting for the action cards required to place mechs.

**Evidence:** 13:27.65–13:33.71 (s000245); 13:33.73–13:39.11 (s000246); 25:11.83–25:15.89 (s000492); 25:24.61–25:28.01 (s000495).

### C03

**Advice:** Neural Motivator is a natural starting technology: it draws an extra action card, and Scheming adds a card when drawing then permits a discard. That supports selection rather than a simple draw-and-keep pattern.

**When it applies:** Most useful when there are weak cards to prune or several new choices; it does not promise a particular card.

**Evidence:** 13:48.71–13:50.13 (s000251); 19:13.79–19:20.13 (s000365); 19:20.15–19:23.79 (s000366); 19:49.75–19:56.21 (s000380); 20:00.01–20:06.69 (s000382).

### C04

**Advice:** Politics secondary is attractive because additional cards can build a large hand without the ordinary limit forcing Yssaril to prune down to seven. The hosts describe card draw as positive momentum.

**When it applies:** Not an automatic follow; command tokens, timing, and card utility still matter.

**Evidence:** 22:06.25–22:11.65 (s000417); 22:23.41–22:25.93 (s000422); 22:36.31–22:40.21 (s000428).

### C05

**Advice:** Stall Tactics turns an action-card discard into an action-phase stall without passing. Using it can also trigger mech deployment, giving the stall an additional payoff and an early deterrent.

**When it applies:** Use when delaying is valuable and a mech can be deployed on a controlled planet.

**Evidence:** 17:05.55–17:08.75 (s000319); 17:15.55–17:19.21 (s000324); 24:47.63–24:54.39 (s000485); 24:54.41–24:59.49 (s000486); 25:00.41–25:02.69 (s000488).

### C06

**Advice:** Mechs cover some early ground weakness: opponents may avoid attacking a planet Yssaril can hold, even when its ships are weaker. The hosts also say killing Yssaril carriers may be worthwhile to force spending.

**When it applies:** This is deterrence rather than immunity; an opponent may target ships or force expenditure.

**Evidence:** 25:24.61–25:28.01 (s000495); 25:31.53–25:34.25 (s000497); 25:41.31–25:45.19 (s000500); 25:45.21–25:48.95 (s000501).

### C07

**Advice:** The flagship’s two movement and ability to pass through enemy-occupied systems can enable an early surprise, such as reaching Mecatol Rex. Light/Wave Deflectors or In the Silence of Space can make its special movement redundant.

**When it applies:** Build around this only when map and timing provide a target; the hosts also value its combat profile.

**Evidence:** 23:21.85–23:22.87 (s000451); 23:34.63–23:38.07 (s000459); 23:38.09–23:39.95 (s000460); 24:24.77–24:28.41 (s000477); 23:57.91–24:00.11 (s000468); 24:15.17–24:17.65 (s000474).

### C08

**Advice:** Spy Net lets a player look at Yssaril’s action cards and take one. The hosts say players often sell or hand over a negotiated card instead of exposing the full choice, and call this common practice potentially contrary to intent.

**When it applies:** Clarify the exchange before trading the note; the hosts question what “look” means and describe a trust convention.

**Evidence:** 25:55.03–26:01.25 (s000505); 26:01.27–26:04.57 (s000506); 26:26.31–26:30.19 (s000515); 26:42.03–26:47.95 (s000518); 28:58.65–29:01.53 (s000571); 29:06.31–29:08.77 (s000574).

### C09

**Advice:** Ssruu copies another player’s agent text, even if exhausted. The hosts emphasize that this does not grant every other component referenced by that text; Yssaril is still using Ssruu.

**When it applies:** Value depends on agents present and useful timing.

**Evidence:** 30:09.53–30:17.05 (s000595); 31:34.49–31:36.95 (s000615); 31:37.39–31:40.85 (s000616); 31:49.37–31:51.19 (s000621); 31:56.21–31:57.85 (s000625).

### C10

**Advice:** So Ata unlocks with seven action cards. The hosts say this often arrives around round three or four because Yssaril may spend cards on mechs, and advise against warping play around the commander.

**When it applies:** Track the threshold while balancing card use and mech deployment; the speakers admit it is easy to forget.

**Evidence:** 32:45.85–32:50.23 (s000643); 33:07.27–33:09.19 (s000651); 33:12.51–33:14.15 (s000653); 33:21.87–33:24.21 (s000656); 33:26.65–33:29.35 (s000658).

### C11

**Advice:** After an opponent activates a system with Yssaril units, So Ata can inspect that player’s cards, promissory notes, or secret objectives. The hosts usually favor secret objectives as the most useful information, but note that a late card search can matter.

**When it applies:** Reactive trigger; choose information by game state.

**Evidence:** 33:31.45–33:33.39 (s000660); 33:37.59–33:40.65 (s000663); 33:41.15–33:43.19 (s000664); 34:03.53–34:07.95 (s000669); 34:09.49–34:11.69 (s000671); 34:28.37–34:29.41 (s000680).

### C12

**Advice:** Kyver’s hero makes each opponent show a card, then lets Yssaril take it or force three random discards. The hosts see little choice for Yssaril: even an unwanted card can be a stall, while discarding three may remove valuable options.

**When it applies:** They identify an exception when the leader has few cards and Yssaril wants to strip them.

**Evidence:** 36:00.31–36:07.35 (s000714); 36:07.37–36:12.33 (s000715); 36:12.35–36:18.39 (s000716); 36:21.69–36:26.43 (s000718); 36:48.75–36:53.83 (s000724); 37:32.19–37:33.47 (s000735).

### C13

**Advice:** Transparasteel Plating can stop players who have passed from playing action cards during Yssaril’s turn. It is situational but accessible from the starting green technology, useful when late responses matter.

**When it applies:** Compare with Hyper Metabolism by economy and whether opponents hold important cards.

**Evidence:** 38:46.53–38:48.05 (s000755); 38:52.63–38:54.41 (s000759); 38:54.63–38:58.43 (s000760); 39:35.71–39:40.17 (s000774); 40:00.43–40:05.45 (s000784); 40:23.93–40:28.93 (s000790).

### C14

**Advice:** The preferred tech engine centers on Bio-Stims and Magi-on Implants, with Gravity Drive for movement. Bio-Stims can ready Magi-on for another use that round; the hosts describe the resulting card access as powerful.

**When it applies:** Tech intensive; shift with skips, economy, timing, or urgent movement.

**Advice:** The no-skip outline adds a choice between Hyper Metabolism and Transparasteel, then Magi-on, followed by blue access such as Dark Energy Tap or Antimass into Gravity Drive and possibly Carrier II, Fleet Logistics, and Light/Wave.

**When it applies:** Adaptable route, not fixed order; options may remain useful through round five.

### C16

**Advice:** With a green skip, Yssaril can omit Hyper Metabolism or Transparasteel; a blue skip may bypass Dark Energy Tap or Antimass for Gravity Drive. Dreadnought II with a yellow skip is mentioned as nonessential.

**When it applies:** Use skips to shorten research; pursue upgrades when objectives or cards justify them.

### C17

**Advice:** The hosts usually avoid red and yellow technologies and favor green and blue. They prefer Carrier II over Fighter II, while acknowledging the preference is personal; Fighter Prototype could make Fighter II appealing.

**When it applies:** Pivot for an objective, slice, or card; several upgrade objectives can justify a detour.

### C18

**Advice:** The hosts first rate round-one Leadership mid-tier, then raise it to high-mid because following Politics can start card flow and offer an early-useful card. Spending an action card to deploy a mech can also create an opening deterrent.

**When it applies:** This describes benefits of following; taking Leadership is not universally best.

**Advice:** Politics is not usually the round-one pick despite card synergy: free cards are less valuable before Yssaril has room to use them, and Politics alone rarely secures Custodians. It rises if the table denies tech or an agent creates access.

## Component connections

- **ability:stall_tactics → unit:yssaril_mech (enables):** Using Stall Tactics can trigger mech deployment on a controlled planet, if a mech remains available.
- **technology:bs → technology:gd (enables):** The green engine and later movement technology form the guide’s broad research plan, subject to economy and prerequisites.
- **leader:yssarilagent → leader:hacanagent (synergizes-with):** When both factions cooperate, copying Hacan’s agent can help produce a commodity refresh and negotiated split.
- **technology:tp → technology:hm (conflicts-with):** The guide presents these as alternative early green technologies, chosen by economy and tactical need.

## Source limits

The hosts criticize familiar Yssaril play patterns and revise their own ratings during the episode. The summary keeps competing openings: Leadership to follow Politics, early Trade for economy, and a blue-first route when movement matters. These are conditional options, not one fixed pick order.

The guide uses anecdotal table judgments and does not verify current rules, edition compatibility, or engine legality. Component IDs come from the supplied reference. Magi-on Implants is absent there and remains prose-only. The episode separates preparation from scoring: a large hand, likely objective route, or partial technology path does not itself earn a point.
