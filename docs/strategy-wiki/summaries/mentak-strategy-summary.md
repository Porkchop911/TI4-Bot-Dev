# Mentak Coalition — strategy summary draft

**Source:** SCPT episode 344, “Super Mentak Coalition Guide,” published 2024-03-27. Podcast advice from complete ASR; rules compatibility, errata, and name recognition remain unchecked. The hosts’ thesis: Mentak still makes money, but in a richer *Prophecy of Kings* economy that is not enough. Convert income into technology, plastic, position, and points.

## Opening and development

### C01 — Convert income into tempo
Mentak’s Pillage income is a traditional economic edge, but it no longer ensures a lead in the richer POK economy. The hosts value a round-one Promise of Protection payment when early tempo matters more than later Pillage income. [25:21.58–26:08.34 · s000452–s000465; 2:08:41.36–2:09:25.32 · s002506–s002519]

### C02 — Plan around the single-carrier start
Mentak start with one carrier and two cruisers. The missing second carrier is a serious opening constraint: technology, a carrier, and a home build compete for resources and actions. Expect a modest round if timing or resources do not solve this. [33:38.12–34:05.04 · s000630–s000638; 34:56.18–35:28.76 · s000647–s000658]

### C03 — Treat the starting techs as options, not a fixed route
Sarween Tools and Plasma Scoring provide yellow and red prerequisites, not a complete plan. PDS II is convenient when unit-upgrade objectives justify it, not mandatory. Mentak lack starting blue technology, so Cruiser II or blue mobility requires deliberate access. [36:04.24–37:05.70 · s000667–s000679]

### C04 — Use Ambush in small fights
Ambush adds punch in a scrappy cruiser-versus-lone-ship fight. Its timing is before Anti-Fighter Barrage, so fighter screens can absorb its hits. Treat it as selective leverage, not a substitute for a strong fleet in a screened fight. [1:03:17.72–1:03:35.76 · s001201–s001208; 1:03:36.42–1:04:09.34 · s001210–s001224]

### C05 — Make Cruiser II an early opportunity, not a long-term plan
The guide wants Cruiser II early while it remains relevant, but says not to build the whole game around it: the useful window closes quickly and the technology is opportunistic. If that window has passed, move on. [1:06:00.14–1:06:22.74 · s001271–s001280; 1:06:29.12–1:06:56.84 · s001283–s001295]

### C06 — Draft for round-one timing
Trade is attractive, but timing Warfare, Technology, or Diplomacy may matter more. Ideally another player takes Warfare while Mentak take Technology; if Warfare remains available late, take it rather than lose the carrier build and movement. If Trade, Warfare, and Technology are unavailable, the guide elevates Diplomacy. Negotiate timing during the draft. [1:11:50.00–1:12:03.60 · s001375–s001380; 1:13:45.08–1:14:57.76 · s001421–s001435; 1:15:45.02–1:15:51.84 · s001451–s001453; 1:18:49.22–1:19:34.04 · s001513–s001524]

### C07 — Use Warfare to move, build, and protect flexibility
With Warfare, move the carrier, build at home, then lift the home token; an initial cruiser move can create a more awkward position. The hosts float Mecatol Rex as a provocative round-one destination, not a default. Warfare also supplies a needed token or activation. Warfare also helps prevent Mentak from being stalled out: it adds an activation or command counter, beyond simply passing. The line does not fix the weak start by itself. [1:20:29.47–1:21:52.81 · s001544–s001573; 1:22:09.83–1:22:36.57 · s001577–s001584]

### C08 — Use Diplomacy to refresh a skip when Cruiser II can still matter
With a green skip, Diplomacy can refresh it and reach Cruiser II while the upgrade is timely, reducing dependence on a Warfare/Technology sequence. Spending the home system twice is a consolation if that line fails, not a reliable centerpiece. Check skip access and timing before committing. [1:13:58.36–1:14:57.76 · s001424–s001435; 1:15:17.72–1:15:42.34 · s001442–s001449]

### C09 — Do not rely on Politics for Custodians
Politics alone does not make an early Custodians attempt reliable: Cruiser II is hard to combine with Politics, while Gravity Drive still depends on blue access. The hosts call a Jol-Nar Research Agreement a possible deal, not an assumption, and prefer fixing other opening weaknesses. [1:15:54.20–1:16:29.02 · s001455–s001468; 1:16:57.24–1:17:25.88 · s001477–s001484]

### C10 — Keep Construction secondary until it solves a real need
Forward docks and PDS are not urgent round-one needs. Space Dock II can later spend a large trade-good reserve quickly and put more units on the board. Revisit Construction when that has a concrete use. [1:17:26.14–1:17:49.44 · s001485–s001495; 1:18:02.94–1:18:21.84 · s001500–s001506]

## Technology and economy

### C11 — Let skips guide the technology plan
The hosts resist a fixed faction-tech script. Let available skips guide the path; Cruiser II is the clearest early exception, though the hosts are unsure that even Cruiser II or Mirror Computing is universally core. [1:26:24.45–1:28:08.45 · s001676–s001710]

### C12 — Green-plus-yellow skips support the classic economic route
With green and yellow skips, the suggested route is Cruiser II, Salvage Operations, then Mirror Computing. Salvage adds post-combat value, but the path has little combat punch. Consider Space Dock II or other useful technology; the sequence alone does not make Mentak strong in combat. [1:28:15.83–1:29:04.75 · s001713–s001730]

### C13 — Blue access can address transport and combat
The blue-and-yellow route can substitute Gravity Drive for Cruiser II, then reach Salvage Operations and Mirror Computing. Blue access opens Carrier II for transport and soak, and Dreadnought II for combat. Choose this over early Cruiser II when carrying capacity and punch matter more than early reach. [1:31:28.16–1:33:16.12 · s001775–s001807]

### C14 — In a no-skip game, consider War Suns only when money and time align
Without skips, the hosts suggest useful yellow technologies and AI Development Algorithm toward War Suns, with flexible order. A War Sun is a late pivot for surplus in a rich game where Mentak can research and buy one in time, not a default. [1:33:16.12–1:33:56.60 · s001808–s001819; 1:34:47.14–1:35:13.86 · s001829–s001835]

### C15 — Salvage Operations is post-combat value
Salvage Operations grants a trade good after a space combat whether Mentak win or lose; on a win, it can produce a ship of a type destroyed there. The hosts distinguish this from a draw and caution against counting the new ship for timing-sensitive objectives. [44:16.68–44:30.94 · s000811–s000815; 44:55.98–45:52.00 · s000822–s000840]

### C16 — Mirror Computing raises spending value but can strand a reserve
Mirror Computing makes each trade good worth two resources or influence when spent for those purposes, not for objectives requiring trade goods. The hosts praise its ceiling but warn that saving trade goods for a possible stage-two objective can leave the reserve idle; spending it for resources or influence does not count as spending trade goods toward an objective. [45:56.56–46:12.64 · s000844–s000850; 46:37.92–47:33.32 · s000860–s000873; 47:47.38–48:00.44 · s000877–s000885; 1:29:05.41–1:29:19.99 · s001731–s001735]

### C17 — Psychoarchaeology can help turn planets into doubled trade goods
With Mirror Computing, Psychoarchaeology can make exhausting a planet for a trade good attractive because it may spend for more than the planet’s printed resources. Mentak can spend earlier and replenish during the action phase. This depends on both technologies and an expendable planet. [1:29:05.41–1:29:19.99 · s001731–s001735; 1:29:44.09–1:30:28.48 · s001746–s001756; 1:30:37.00–1:30:48.40 · s001760–s001764]

## Pillage and position

### C18 — Use Pillage as bargaining leverage, not just income
The threat of Pillage is bargaining power: include Mentak in deals, or waive it to let a favorable outside deal happen. The hosts advise against Pillaging a transaction that includes Mentak because it sacrifices soft power and can make future deals harder. [1:44:29.40–1:44:42.14 · s002017–s002022; 1:45:39.26–1:46:21.24 · s002052–s002067]

### C19 — Vote on other players’ deals with a selective waiver
For a deal between opponents, ask whether it helps Mentak. If so, announce “no Pillage”; otherwise the threat can pressure who trades with whom. Do not shut down all table trade: Mentak still need transactions. When a Trade primary is selling trade goods, a negotiated payment for waiving Pillage across the related transactions can beat taxing every wash individually. The leverage depends on the factions and deals at the table. [1:42:11.52–1:42:54.88 · s001975–s001989; 1:46:42.78–1:47:22.18 · s002074–s002089; 1:41:05.30–1:41:37.06 · s001950–s001961]

### C20 — Set a credible tax policy, then moderate it
The guide offers a spectrum from agreed payment to frequent Pillage; judge the table’s wealth and trading habits. Round-one commodity washing makes small transfers hard to tally, so state expectations clearly. Later, waive a trigger when extra money matters less than goodwill or a useful deal. Excessive Pillage can suppress needed trade. [1:39:44.86–1:40:58.98 · s001920–s001946; 1:43:26.00–1:44:03.17 · s001998–s002005; 1:47:47.82–1:48:49.28 · s002099–s002112; 1:49:17.00–1:49:59.98 · s002125–s002141]

### C21 — Put cruisers where adjacency lasts and removal costs actions
Use cruisers as persistent board presence near wormholes or Mecatol Rex, not so far away that a lone ship is easy to remove. Good positioning can preserve adjacency after an attack and make clearing the cruiser costly. This protects Pillage leverage but does not make the ship untouchable. [1:50:26.12–1:52:00.86 · s002143–s002173]

### C22 — Prefer bounded no-Pillage windows to complicated passes
The hosts find formal Pillage-pass tokens cumbersome. For a specific negotiation, prefer an explicit promise such as “no Pillage for this action phase.” Mentak can still Pillage the other side of a transaction. Broad or invisible protection promises are a last resort because they give away leverage over later deals. [1:52:24.00–1:54:13.62 · s002187–s002225]

### C23 — Agree on Pillage expectations before async deals pile up
In asynchronous games, a bot may surface each Pillage opportunity, but Mentak must respond before transactions accumulate. Agree on a tax policy ahead of time and keep deals transparent. The hosts ask other players not to hide transactions, which can be difficult to reconstruct later. [1:54:33.86–1:55:43.74 · s002232–s002253; 1:56:58.84–1:57:06.82 · s002277–s002281; 1:57:31.86–1:58:48.08 · s002293–s002317]

### C24 — Use Suffi An as a sweetener, not a free service
After Pillage is used against another player, Suffi An lets both players draw an action card. The guide likes this as a sweetener that softens Pillage. Do not give the agent away for free or make the card the whole point; agenda-phase transactions may be useful to negotiate. [48:39.38–49:02.56 · s000897–s000900; 50:05.78–51:07.88 · s000919–s000931; 1:58:59.06–1:59:59.24 · s002323–s002344]

### C25 — Sell Promise of Protection when early tempo matters more
Promise of Protection prevents Pillage against its holder and returns if they activate a system containing Mentak units. The hosts disagree with advice to withhold it from rich factions: round-one payment can repair Mentak’s weak opening, while lost Pillage income may come later. Convert the payment into tempo and keep bargaining over other sides of deals. [42:26.82–43:24.08 · s000778–s000793; 2:02:09.06–2:04:58.48 · s002385–s002423; 2:05:37.12–2:06:38.90 · s002444–s002460]

## Combat and scoring

### C26 — Treat the commander’s note theft as opportunistic
After Mentak win a space combat, S’ula Mentarion can force the opponent to give a promissory note; it unlocks at four cruisers. The hosts call it rarely relevant because notes may be traded or low impact, though Ceasefire or Political Secret can matter. Track the opponent’s hand; do not build around it. [51:25.58–51:54.34 · s000936–s000942; 52:17.80–53:06.14 · s000950–s000970; 54:13.44–54:53.58 · s000993–s001008; 2:00:00.00–2:00:51.12 · s002345–s002354]

### C27 — Reserve the hero for a combat that changes the game
Ipswitch can be purged during a space combat to replace each opponent ship destroyed there with a Mentak ship of that type. It can win a difficult fight, defend home, or seize a position. It affects one combat: using it offensively removes its defensive threat and cannot stop coordinated follow-up attacks. [57:52.94–58:55.26 · s001086–s001116; 1:01:33.02–1:02:34.66 · s001170–s001183]

### C28 — Use Moll Terminus against sustain-dependent ground defenses
Moll Terminus prevents opposing ground forces on its planet from using sustain damage. The hosts value it against sustain-dependent matchups and as rare ground-combat leverage. It only matters when invading that planet; bring enough infantry and a way to land them. [58:55.80–1:00:17.98 · s001117–s001146; 1:04:10.08–1:04:55.20 · s001225–s001245]

### C29 — Do not build around the Fourth Moon’s narrow effect
The Fourth Moon turns off sustain damage for opponents’ ships in its system. The hosts say its narrow reach and modest combat profile make it less central now that Mentak have other defensive tools. It can matter against sustain-dependent ships, but does not protect the whole board or reliably win alone. [40:12.90–42:11.92 · s000732–s000766; 58:09.54–58:26.04 · s001095–s001103]

### C30 — Treat economy objectives as an edge, not a scoring plan
Economic objectives are a strength, but other factions score them too and they are only part of the deck. Mentak are weak at several control and combat requirements, with little natural help for bombardment, Anti-Fighter Barrage, or Space Cannon. Cruiser II may create a temporary control chance, but the hosts describe it as a narrow opening rather than a broad objective advantage. [2:21:27.86–2:23:40.50 · s002798–s002849]

### C31 — Search for bonus points, but do not rely on a Custodians gamble
The speakers suggest buying fragments and exploring more than usual, and like Dark Energy Tap for exposing opportunities. They doubt an early Politics pick can reliably secure Custodians. A Mecatol Imperial line needs an unusual setup: mechs or War Suns may help, but getting them there depends on timely technology and access. Treat bonus points as upside, not a substitute for weak point tempo. [2:13:28.87–2:14:46.47 · s002601–s002640; 2:16:07.41–2:17:17.39 · s002671–s002700]

### C32 — Stay in the scoring pack rather than leading early
The guide warns against taking the lead early because Mentak’s one-combat hero can be answered by coordinated attacks. Aim for a later steal when opponents have spent actions elsewhere. An offensive hero enables a scoring play but trades away its defensive threat. [2:12:24.19–2:13:24.33 · s002580–s002598; 2:18:23.71–2:19:35.69 · s002723–s002752]

## Source and interpretation limits

This guide is dated 2024 and discusses POK/Codex III-era assumptions. Names including Mentak, Moll Terminus, Suffi An, S’ula Mentarion, and Ipswitch may be imperfect ASR; component IDs use the reference catalogue spelling. The hosts preserve disagreement about Promise of Protection and make table-dependent judgments about Pillage and technology. Citations document their advice; they do not establish current rules legality or prove a line wins.
