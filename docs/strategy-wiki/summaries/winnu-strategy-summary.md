# Winnu strategy summary

*Draft based on SCPT 352, “Super The Winnu Guide” (22 May 2024). The hosts speak from a particular tournament and async meta; they do not verify current rules. This summary preserves their strong preferences and their own caveats. Transcript timings and segment IDs point to complete discussion spans, not rules authority.*

## Strategic thesis

The guide's plan is to reach the late game alive, with enough plastic that attacking Winnu costs the table something, then use the hero's flexible timing to catch up. It repeatedly warns that Winnu's risk is political and defensive: a neighbor can make a weak opening fatal before the late hero matters. The hosts describe round-five tempo as excellent only if Winnu survives to use it. (C15, C26; 38:36–38:55, s000748–s000759; 61:03–62:19, s001216–s001239)

That diagnosis reverses the usual opening advice. The speakers say most factions should take a point when they can, but Winnu should not force a round-one score by emptying its slice or leaving a thin fleet. A point that advertises an early lead may invite a round-two attack; Winnu has a later catch-up mechanism. They allow natural, low-cost scoring and frame survival, not a blanket refusal to score, as the priority. (C16; 40:00–42:08, s000780–s000820)

## Opening economy and draft

**C01 — Put units on the board before chasing an ambitious line.** Winnu begins with only a carrier, cruiser, fighters, infantry and a PDS, according to the hosts. They characterize the force as insufficient for both useful expansion and a secure position, and say that Winnu's first concern should be elimination. The opening therefore needs to improve the actual board position. (12:49–13:43, s000249–s000263)

**C02 — Dark Energy Tap is the default starting technology.** The hosts favor it because it starts Winnu on the blue path and describe DET itself as valuable for opening options. They do not say to pursue every blue technology: their later line is DET, Gravity Drive, Carrier II, then a return to building. Which technology is practical depends on what the map and table offer. (14:19–14:41, s000276–s000283; 67:32–68:17, s001351–s001361)

**C03 — Plan around limited cash.** The guide describes a single home planet valued at 3 resources and 4 influence and three commodities. Its point is that the home economy cannot effortlessly pay for both technology and the plastic needed to protect the slice. Treat cash and production as constrained, while recognizing that trade goods and a favorable slice can alter the opening. (14:41–15:01, s000284–s000295; 15:34–15:53, s000314–s000328)

**C04 — Usually decline an early Custodians rush.** The speakers reject the tempting Politics → Gravity Drive → Mecatol plan when it leaves Winnu with too little plastic and an exposed slice. They report not seeing recent Winnu wins built around taking Custodians, and warn that an early point can make Winnu look like a threat. This is a default risk assessment; an unusually safe map or table may change it. (44:49–45:56, s000880–s000894; 49:41–50:20, s000982–s000991)

**C05 — Do not overvalue Blood Ties.** It removes the influence cost for the Custodians token, but the hosts regard it as an ability that can go unused all game and say six influence is often attainable. Its value arises only when Winnu actually takes Mecatol for Custodians; it does not repair the broader economy or defend the fleet. (16:11–16:41, s000338–s000348; 45:42–45:56, s000892–s000894)

**C06 — Reclamation is a rare Mecatol bonus with delayed use.** After a tactical action that gains Mecatol Rex, Winnu may place one PDS and one space dock there. The speakers emphasize that it happens after the production step, so those structures do not produce during the capture action. They describe the opportunity as likely once per game at most. (16:42–17:30, s000349–s000361)

**C07 — Use the Reclaimer mech when structures help.** A mech may place a PDS or space dock on the planet it captures. Several mechs capturing together can place several structures, and the guide sees this as useful for structure objectives and a reason Winnu need not rely on Construction. The structures arrive after production, so they cannot immediately build units in that same action. (24:48–26:18, s000503–s000530)

**C08 — Treat the agent as opening relief.** Berekar Berekon reduces by two the combined cost of units produced. The hosts value a use at roughly two trade goods and say it is principally a round-one tool for getting needed units onto the board. It remains a discount later, but does not create a new strategic capability. (26:31–27:28, s000535–s000558)

**C17 — Leadership is usually a low pick.** The guide says it does not directly improve Winnu's plastic position. The hosts can accept it as a late choice if Diplomacy and Warfare have already been taken and the available secondary timings are favorable. That is conditional draft advice, not a prohibition on Leadership or on needing command tokens. (42:08–43:11, s000821–s000840)

**C18 — Diplomacy is a slice-dependent money choice.** The speakers call it decent, especially if Trade is gone, but say Winnu's three-resource home limits its value and an influence-rich slice may make it pay other players more. In round one, Acquiescence can help sell a Diplomacy secondary: letting Winnu resolve a secondary without placing a token is unusually valuable when its opening actions are scarce. (43:11–43:50, s000841–s000857; 44:01–44:50, s000858–s000879)

**C19 — Construction is mainly for a structure objective.** Winnu's mech can create structures while taking planets, so the strategy card is normally less useful than Diplomacy or Leadership. The guide's niche is getting three structures outside the home system in round one by building a mech; the agent can offset part of that mech's cost. The hosts say there are other ways to meet the objective, too. (46:05–47:18, s000897–s000921)

**C20 — Warfare may be mandatory in a weak draft.** If Winnu is late to pick and no one else appears likely to take Warfare, the hosts say Winnu should take it rather than risk a round-one position with no useful expansion. They dislike combining Warfare with a rushed Gravity Drive and Custodians grab, which spends the plastic needed to survive. Acquiescence may be sold if another player wants the Warfare secondary. (49:08–50:33, s000967–s000997)

**C21 — Take free technology, then stop when plastic is the need.** Technology is a decent pick because its primary provides tech without spending the money needed for units. The proposed line is DET → Gravity Drive → Carrier II; stop there by default. A Technology secondary is an exception when Winnu is already at its production limit and still has about four trade goods. A slice-specific need can justify a different path. (50:33–51:48, s000998–s001024; 53:11–53:28, s001046–s001054)

**C22 — Make attacks unattractive with ships and fighters.** The guide names Carrier II plus a large fighter screen as an efficient way to build a threatening fleet, and says the commander's combat bonus complements it. Two mechs capturing a two-planet system and establishing forward space docks are another possibility, but the broad priority remains a defended board position. This is deterrence advice, not a promise of victory in a fight. (51:48–52:54, s001025–s001037)

## Winnu's pieces and interactions

**C09 — The commander is localized protection.** Once unlocked by controlling Mecatol or entering combat there, Rickar Rickani adds +2 to Winnu's combat rolls at home, Mecatol, and legendary planets. The hosts value this most for holding home and, if contested, Mecatol. It does not protect ordinary systems unless they contain a legendary planet, and the bonus matters less when Winnu has few units. (28:11–30:06, s000577–s000616)

**C10 — Do not buy the flagship expecting it to work alone.** Salai Sai Corian rolls based on enemy non-fighter ships, so a carrier fleet with many fighters can leave its special ability with little to roll against. The hosts call it counterable by heavy fighter screens and say they have seen it perform only with the commander. The ASR mangles the name; the component reference supplies the spelling used here. (17:42–18:43, s000364–s000388)

**C11 — The faction technologies are low-priority in this guide.** Lazax Gate Folding's wormhole benefit applies while Winnu does not control Mecatol, whereas its infantry action requires control; the speakers see little payoff in that split. Hegemonic Trade Policy has a narrow production-step use to swap Mecatol's resource and influence values when building, but the guide says the yellow investment is not worth it. These are dated strategic judgments, not current card rulings. (19:49–21:33, s000419–s000476)

**C12 — Price Acquiescence modestly and use its timing.** The Omega note lets a recipient resolve a strategy card's secondary without spending or placing a command token. The hosts value it around one trade good and see round-one Diplomacy as especially attractive, because saving a token can give Winnu a third action. Its value varies: Leadership's secondary costs no token, and Construction often requires the recipient to activate the system anyway. (23:20–24:47, s000478–s000500; 43:55–44:50, s000858–s000879; 47:20–47:53, s000923–s000937)

**C13 — Mathis Mathinus is the central catch-up tool.** The hero performs the primary of any strategy card, lets any number of players perform its secondary, then is purged. The hosts clarify that it is a component action, not a strategic action: Acquiescence does not apply, and Coup d'Etat cannot cancel it. The transcript varies the hero's spoken name; the component reference identifies Mathis Mathinus. (30:38–33:29, s000627–s000665)

**C14 — Imperial is the usual hero target.** The hosts describe using its primary to catch up on a public point, potentially pairing Custodians with an Imperial point if Mecatol is available. Non-Imperial uses are possible only in narrower windows. This can be a powerful swing, but it depends on scoring requirements and timing and can make Winnu the table's immediate target. (33:02–34:26, s000661–s000676; 33:22–33:46, s000666–s000673)

**C23 — Turn income into plastic, not influence by habit.** The guide argues Winnu can often skip expensive secondaries, including late secret-objective farming, because the hero can supply a late scoring opportunity. It recommends resource-rich slices; in one winning async example, an influence-heavy planet's income was still useful when spent on ships. Keep enough tokens for planned actions, but do not assume influence is the binding resource. (53:46–55:05, s001064–s001105; 57:50–58:22, s001161–s001168)

**C24 — Trade Alliance with the Mecatol endgame in mind.** The hosts warn against giving Alliance to a player who also wants Mecatol Rex: that player can apply Winnu's +2 bonus against Winnu in the Mecatol system. If that fight is unlikely, selling it for money or arranging late-game protection may be worthwhile. Because the note also works in its buyer's home system, its defensive value can matter to that partner. (58:22–59:52, s001169–s001199)

## Late-game timing, counters, and limits

**C25 — Be ready by round four.** One proposed sequence is to take Mecatol on the last action of round four, then use the hero and Imperial early in round five. The hero's timing can enable an action-phase score or favorable status timing depending on setup. The hosts call the setup demanding and warn that controlling Mecatol makes Winnu the table's obvious focus. (59:52–61:09, s001200–s001216)

**C26 — A favorable stage-two objective can be both opportunity and danger.** Winnu may suddenly have a large catch-up turn, but the table can recognize the threat and try to eliminate it before the score. The guide's response is to remain hard to stop early and evaluate revealed objectives and reach before accelerating. It says outright that winning is not easy and offers no guaranteed defense against a coordinated table. (61:03–62:19, s001216–s001239)

**C27 — Do not mistake tempo for slaying power.** The hosts say Winnu is not a win-slayer: it relies on staying far enough back that other players attack the leaders. Carrier II, Gravity Drive, and even later Light/Wave do not by themselves make a round-five slay likely. They describe the late hero as Winnu's one special trick, with a quiet, defensive opening as its best chance to remain relevant. (62:29–65:09, s001243–s001295)

**C28 — The reported wins followed the quiet line.** The speakers cite recent tournament games that avoided Custodians and coasted with a built-up position, as well as an async win where money went into plastic until the hero carried Winnu over the finish. They call these patient, low-profile games more representative than early gambits. These are anecdotes, not a measured win-rate comparison. (54:47–56:41, s001085–s001135; 65:03–65:28, s001294–s001298)

The guide's own limitation is central: this plan can make Winnu relevant, but it cannot force the table to leave it alone or guarantee a win-slay. It asks the player to accept a slow, sometimes unsuccessful game in exchange for preserving the late hero opportunity. Opponents may still attack Winnu early, especially if a scoring window makes the threat obvious. (C15, C26–C28; 38:36–38:55, s000748–s000759; 61:03–65:28, s001216–s001298)

## Component connections supported by the discussion

- **Agent → Reclaimer mech:** its two-unit discount can help fund the early mech in the structure-objective example (C08, C19; 46:34–46:54, s000904–s000910).
- **Commander ↔ fighter screen:** the bonus is most valuable when Winnu has enough units to exploit it; the hosts explicitly describe fighters as more threatening with the commander (C09, C22; 28:44–29:00, s000585–s000590; 52:21–52:31, s001029–s001031).
- **Gravity Drive → Carrier II:** this is part of the guide's blue mobility path, while the hosts caution it does not create slaying power by itself (C21, C27; 63:44–64:48, s001270–s001288).
- **Hero → Imperial:** the primary can provide the late public-point catch-up swing when objective and Mecatol conditions align (C13–C14, C25; 33:02–34:26, s000661–s000676; 59:52–61:09, s001200–s001216).
- **Alliance ↔ commander:** giving Winnu's note to a Mecatol rival can turn the commander's bonus against Winnu in that system (C24; 58:30–59:14, s001171–s001182).
- **Enemy fighter screen counters Winnu flagship:** opposing fighters reduce the number of non-fighter ships counted for its special dice (C10; 18:11–18:29, s000377–s000382).

## Evidence and uncertainty

This is one guide episode published 22 May 2024. Its compatibility with the current rules and objective set has not been reviewed. The transcript uses small-model ASR and contains several faction, speaker, and component-name errors; spellings above follow the assigned component reference where possible. Strategy, valuations, tournament references, and table predictions remain the hosts' opinions. In particular, their advice distinguishes having late scoring tempo from having the plastic to survive or remove a leader. The citations point to overlapping transcript segments for verification, not proof that the strategic claims are correct.
