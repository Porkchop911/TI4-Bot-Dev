# Naaz-Rokha strategy summary

**Source:** Space Cats Peace Turtles, SCPT 287, “Super Naaz-Rokha Alliance Guide,” published 2023-02-22. **Status:** Source-based draft; guide-era Prophecy of Kings advice, not a current-rules verification. The hosts’ central picture is a flexible explorer with exceptional mech combat, but a weak home production base and a deliberately high-variance plan. Build value through many useful explorations, preserve options, then decide whether the board supports an early point push or a patient endgame. Favorable relics can create a fast finish; they are never guaranteed. [C02, C06, C31]

## Exploration and fragment economy

**C01.** Put a mech on planets you expect to explore. Distant Suns gives an extra card and lets you choose which result to resolve, so the faction can filter its exploration while cycling the deck. The hosts debate declaration timing; this summary does not resolve their FAQ dispute. [12:13–13:06, 13:35–13:47; s000202–s000210, s000217–s000218]

**C02.** Think of exploration as a sequence of small advantages rather than a guaranteed jackpot. Fragments, attachments, refreshed planets, trade goods, and occasional standout relics compound over several rounds. More chances improve selection, but the guide also describes runs that miss the relics needed to change the result. [41:34–41:46, 42:24–42:38, 90:43–91:51; s000686–s000687, s000708–s000713, s001505–s001517, s001523]

**C03.** When you can choose the deck, the hosts favor hazardous and cultural over industrial while those decks remain productive. They slightly prefer hazardous for round one, while describing hazardous and cultural as close overall. Watch how quickly other players explore them; move to industrial when the preferred pools have been depleted rather than insisting on a stale target. [87:17–88:01, 88:14–88:38, 91:03–91:22; s001455–s001464, s001467–s001475, s001512–s001513]

**C04.** If slice selection gives you one, plan an early mech landing on a hazardous planet: that puts the extra-card selection to work during the round-one window the guide values most. Cultural can still be a good starting deck when hazardous is absent. Do not compromise the whole opening just to force one deck. [87:17–87:33, 88:14–88:51; s001455–s001458, s001467–s001475]

**C05.** Garv and Gunn add an exploration at the end of a player’s turn. Using the agent on yourself can combine that explore with Distant Suns, but its wording can also benefit the player whose turn just ended. Consider the beneficiary and timing before spending it. Timing is awkward; ASR varies the name, while the component reference says Garv and Gunn. [25:28–25:56, 25:59–26:31; s000388–s000396, s000400–s000404]

**C06.** Treat variance as part of the faction’s strategic cost. Naaz-Rokha’s best outcomes can yield more bonus points than most factions can access, but even a well-played game can draw weak relics and underperform. Keep scoring and board development on track instead of behaving as though the desired relic is already in hand. [35:02–36:03, 36:22–37:18; s000549–s000573, s000582–s000586, s000603–s000610]

**C07.** Save fragments when the immediate value of spending them is low. Fabrication can exchange matching fragments for a relic or one fragment for a command token, so delay preserves a choice among scoring, economy, and a useful stall. Spend when a concrete point or action depends on it. No required reserve size is given. [15:48–16:15, 42:24–42:58, 111:29–112:06; s000252–s000256, s000708–s000713, s001853–s001860]

**C08.** Black Market Forgery is often a modest, cooperative sale: another player can turn matching fragments into a relic, the note returns after use, and you can gain payment or goodwill. The hosts suggest a low asking price—roughly a trade good or a small favor—because the whole table can benefit from cycling relics. Cutthroat metas and Obsidian can change the bargain. [17:59–18:26, 77:33–78:30, 79:15–80:08; s000279–s000295, s001286–s001314, s001314–s001327]

**C09.** Try to buy isolated fragments from other players while keeping your own. A lone fragment may be of little use to its owner but can complete your relic or token plans. Selling your own supply gives up options; the speakers reserve that choice for a desperate position where the payment might keep you competitive. A hard no-deal table can deny this value. [82:00–83:02, 83:09–84:12, 84:44–85:11; s001368–s001378, s001380–s001397, s001410–s001413]

**C10.** Dart and Tai’s commander can create an explore after you take control of an opponent’s planet. That keeps the exploration engine relevant during a capture and can combine with Distant Suns if a mech is present. Make the attack for its board, scoring, or denial value; the explore is an added payoff, not a sufficient reason to start a costly fight. [25:59–26:41; s000400–s000408]

**C11.** Hesh and Prit’s hero offers a relic and up to two strategy-card secondaries. Plan their order: an early secret draw, for example, can precede a secondary that helps act on the new opportunity. Use the hero when those effects solve a concrete problem. Token sourcing with empty reinforcements remains unresolved in the episode. [26:45–27:50, 44:57–45:19; s000409–s000420, s000747–s000755]

## Technology, production, and combat

**C12.** Starting Psychoarchaeology and AI Development Algorithm make a broad set of technology paths accessible, especially when exploration finds skips. Treat that as flexibility to follow the slice, objective pool, and threat—not as a checklist to buy every attractive technology. The speakers intentionally decline to prescribe one path for all games. [10:00–10:47, 46:32–47:51; s000156–s001167, s000776–s000796]

**C13.** Consider Cruiser II when an early Custodians run or a distant control objective gives its speed a specific job. The hosts sketch a route through a blue movement technology, selecting Antimass or Dark Energy Tap to fit the slice, then Gravity Drive where useful. Check rival reach and your ability to pay for both tech and the cruiser. Skip the early commitment if those targets are unlikely; Cruiser II can remain a later option. [48:16–49:30, 54:07–54:57; s000802–s000828, s000895–s000904]

**C14.** Carrier II is a flexible upgrade when dispersed mechs need to move or an invasion needs extra capacity and fighter hit points. It helps support the faction’s high-impact mechs, but does not guarantee the fleet wins space combat before landing. They favor it, but fleet composition and production change its value. [49:30–51:08; s000829–s000852]

**C15.** Scanlink Drone Network gets more mileage when mechs can explore repeatedly: Distant Suns improves the quality of each added opportunity. Add it when exploration is your actual plan and the desired deck is still worth mining. Its value competes with movement and production needs; the hosts describe the improved odds but do not quantify them. [51:15–52:07; s000854–s000863]

**C16.** Dreadnought II can be a practical choice when weak production limits how much plastic you can build. Beside a Carrier II, fighters, and mechs, a dreadnought adds fleet durability. It is a fallback, not a universal priority. [52:07–53:03; s000864–s000875]

**C17.** Fix the starting space dock’s weak production early. The speakers often prefer a forward dock in a productive, useful location over an automatic home double dock; Sling Relay can add a build window when the dock is otherwise stretched. Construction makes sense when the slice supports that forward site. Home double docking and other production effects remain alternatives when it does not. [39:24–40:09, 53:35–54:06, 96:43–98:06; s000646–s000657, s000883–s000894]

**C18.** Round-one Custodians is an option to calculate against the table, not an automatic Naaz-Rokha plan. The guide describes a demanding mix of technology, a cruiser, influence, and cash; Trade or a favorable hazardous explore can help fund it. Compare opponents’ access, speaker order, and your actual resources before committing. The hosts explicitly call the cruiser a significant cost and outline other opening paths. [93:31–94:47, 98:06–99:51; s001555–s001585, s001638–s001668]

**C19.** If you intend to build a forward dock, exploring that site with a mech may reveal an attachment that improves the planet. Have a backup site in mind: a DMZ result can make the first choice less attractive. The hosts recommend planning the dock around production utility, rather than treating a favorable exploration as guaranteed. [96:44–98:06; s001618, s001625, s001627–s001628, s001631–s001632, s001635, s001637]

**C20.** Supercharge is not a routine purchase in the hosts’ view. Its combat-round boost benefits many dice, yet they question whether it changes close battles enough to justify the technology and resources. It can be a late defensive pickup when technology is available and better spending opportunities are gone; this is a marginal-value opinion, not a claim it never helps. [30:01–31:22, 56:14–58:01; s000465–s000481, s000932–s000956]

**C21.** Pre-Fab Arcologies is a late, conditional economic plan. Repeated exploration or refreshes can make a valuable attached planet pay out again, but the resulting money can exceed your production or your ability to spend it. Consider the tech only with a reliable refresh source and a useful planet. The hosts recognize good setups while warning that the prerequisites and action sequencing add work. [31:23–33:57, 58:01–59:42; s000483–s000532, s000958–s000987]

**C22.** Bio-Stims can be a better conditional add when a valuable attached or tech-skip planet is ready to refresh. The guide does not recommend forcing a deep green route just to combine Bio-Stims with Pre-Fab Arcologies. Consider it after the main movement and production plan is secure, or when a technology objective makes the route convenient. The hosts differ on its priority because its value depends on the planet available. [61:01–63:43, 63:29–63:42; s001014–s001060]

**C23.** Transit Diodes gives a late route to regroup mechs after they have been spread out for exploration. The hosts like it as a supplemental blue-yellow option for pivoting to defense or an offensive finish, but do not make it an early purchase or prescribe a single prerequisite sequence. [60:00–60:59, 76:13–76:36; s000997–s001012, s001255–s001263]

**C24.** Assault Cannon can surprise opponents because the source describes the mech as a ship at the start of space combat. The hosts sketch one carrier carrying two mechs triggering the effect before mechs help clear the ground battle. Treat this as a specialized late technology play that depends on reach, opposing fleets, and combat timing, not as a guaranteed invasion. [20:27–21:44, 65:09–66:18; s000306–s000320, s001085–s001101]

**C25.** Visz El Vir boosts the combat dice of mechs in its system and can carry them, amplifying the faction’s ground strength. Coordinate flagship movement with mech placement: the ship is a strong defense, but scattered mechs may be hard to assemble for an offensive finish. The hosts’ enthusiasm for its dice output coexists with that positioning constraint. [22:13–24:02, 74:11–74:49; s000327–s000359, s001225–s001232]

**C26.** Destroyer II may be preferable when enemy fighters are the obstacle to the faction’s plan for winning a fight quickly. The source contrasts that broadly useful matchup role with Cruiser II’s specialized movement and mech-assisted snipes. Choose based on neighbors and targets; the hosts differ on which upgrade ranks higher in a vacuum. [71:01–72:07, 72:08–73:33; s001176–s001212]

**C27.** Warsuns are an optional late offensive pivot if skips and money leave room for them, not a missing piece of the basic faction kit. The hosts doubt that Warsuns fill a necessary gap, while allowing they can suit an offensive endgame if you can reposition your mechs. Transit Diodes can make the flagship option more coherent, but no route is guaranteed. [73:33–74:12, 74:35–76:22; s001213–s001263]

## Position, agendas, and scoring

**C28.** Spread mechs across valuable exploration sites and guard planets carrying several attachments, but preserve a way to regroup for a late defense or scoring strike. Coverage and security matter while you explore; they compete with assembling the flagship or an offensive fleet. The right balance depends on the table’s threat and the endgame board. [106:21–107:38, 107:50–108:40; s001766–s001795]

**C29.** Watch legendary planets while Shard of the Throne is still relevant. The hosts prefer not to take on that risk casually and mention capturing an opponent’s Shard as one possible route. This is not a blanket ban: they describe a DMZ result as one situation that can change the calculation. [108:57–109:59; s001818–s001835]

**C30.** Frequent exploration can make attachment and tech-skip control objectives easier to prepare for. Preparation is not scoring: check the revealed objective’s actual threshold and timing before counting the point. The hosts use broad terms such as “easy” without walking through every scoring test, so the practical lesson is improved access to the needed board state, not automatic completion. [112:19–113:30; s001864–s001889]

**C31.** Keep several point routes open—Custodians, relics, secrets, and ordinary public objectives. A favorable run may support an early finish, while stage-two objectives serve as a fallback if bonus points do not appear. Continue to reassess the table and revealed scoring conditions; the guide repeatedly warns that good relics are a matter of luck. [43:27–45:56, 105:03–106:08, 116:28–117:19; s000722–s000755, s001739–s001760, s001938–s001946]

**C32.** Fragments can also be a reserve against command-token agendas. The hosts advise delaying conversion when practical so an agenda does not immediately erase value you just created. Spend sooner when a relic or token has a concrete use; this is a hedge against uncertainty rather than a plan that assumes a particular agenda will appear. [111:01–112:06; s001847–s001860]

**C33.** Avoid agendas that replace your mechs. The speakers view that outcome as a severe loss; they treat Articles of War as smaller because it prevents the space-capable side while leaving useful mech combat. Their assessment is conditional: suppressing an opponent’s ability may make the vote worthwhile, and their interpretation is not a rules ruling. [110:00–111:08; s001836–s001846]

**C34.** Keep Garv and Gunn for repeated exploration when you can. Alliance is a smaller negotiable tool for shared exploration, including reciprocal early control deals. Trade or share only when payment and the board arrangement justify helping another player or giving up immediate value. The hosts strongly discourage selling the agent but acknowledge an unusually large offer could change that judgment. [80:08–81:59; s001328–s001367]

**C35.** On a DMZ planet, the guide distinguishes the agent from Scanlink: Garv and Gunn can still offer one exploration, while Scanlink cannot use the mech-dependent Distant Suns selection there. The agent remains subject to its end-of-turn timing and beneficiary. This interaction does not make every DMZ planet worth exploring. [90:09–90:38; s001495–s001502]

## Component interactions

The source supports a few specific interactions rather than synergy inferred from names appearing together: mechs enable Distant Suns; Garv and Gunn adds an eligible exploration; Dart and Tai can extend the loop after a capture; the flagship boosts mechs; Assault Cannon uses the mech’s space-combat form; Transit Diodes regroups dispersed forces; Scanlink can support repeated refreshes for Pre-Fab Arcologies when the economy can absorb them. [C01, C05, C10, C15, C21, C23–C25]

## Evidence and limits

The source is one SCPT guide published February 22, 2023. Its episode header says compatibility is unreviewed; podcast advice, ASR wording, FAQ interpretations, exploration-deck composition, tech prerequisites, agenda timing, and relic interactions have not been independently checked against current rules. Some specialist names and faction terms are ASR-uncertain. Component-reference spellings are used where known; contested advice stays conditional. Partial setup is not counted as a point, and no speaker identities are assigned.
