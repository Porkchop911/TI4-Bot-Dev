# Hacan strategy summary — SCPT 360

**Draft based on the 2024-07-17 Super Emirates of Hacan Guide.** Podcast advice is not a current-rules check. Map, table, expansion mix and game horizon may differ. Names are normalized only where the component reference resolves ASR; no speaker identity is assigned.

## Strategic thesis

Hacan turns money into options: it can make deals, buy research tempo and soften costs. The episode’s repeated warning is that trade goods do not become ships or points by themselves. Hacan has a low production ceiling and a home system split across three planets, while its transaction abilities and Quantum Datahub Node create high utility. The broad plan is to score steadily, keep the table willing to deal, and avoid looking like the inevitable winner before Hacan converts its advantages.

## Opening alternatives

### C01 — Treat money as flexibility, not a win condition; Hacan’s mech, action-card exchange and Quantum Datahub Node create utility that can close a game.
A large bank does not itself score or defend. Check whether money can become ships or points before the table responds. Cash helps with deals and costs only when it becomes plastic, position or points. [SCPT 360, 33:42–34:53; s000598–s000622.

### C02 — Plan for the three-planet home system’s thin ground defense and low production ceiling; build fighters and heavy ships to defend the sky.
Ground forces are spread thin and Hacan is easy to slay; Sardakk can threaten the ground despite sky defense. Risk rises when Hacan has little plastic or an opponent can attack the ground. [SCPT 360, 38:18–39:57; s000681–s000719.

### C03 — Manage how far ahead Hacan appears; sometimes decline a public score if still on pace and table attention is dangerous.
Visible QDN and strong tempo can unite opponents; one host describes skipping a score while still on path. Not a default instruction to pass; use only when the score remains recoverable. [SCPT 360, 35:38–38:16; s000634–s000679.

### C04 — Expect a forgiving expansion opening from two carriers and four infantry; avoid spending resources just to ready the inefficient home 0/1 planet.
Round one is forgiving, but cash is tight for competing plans and the home planet is poor. Adapt to slice, objective and round-one race. [SCPT 360, 43:24–45:13; s000764–s000805.

### C05 — Take Trade as the preferred round-one pick when available and the table will transact; Hacan’s six commodities and free Trade secondary are valuable.
Trade refreshes the table and can leave Hacan with a large reserve; returns depend on accepted deals. The 13–14 TG estimate depends on player count and table terms. [SCPT 360, 48:35–49:22; s000891–s000911.

### C06 — Use Construction for a forward dock that can later return home with a Hacan mech after a planet trade.
Put the dock on a planet you can sell; the mech moves all units including structures to another controlled planet, improving home production later. Needs Construction, viable sale, a mech there and controlled destination; temporary investment. [SCPT 360, 46:42–48:15; s000838–s000880.

### C07 — Politics can set up first Leadership pick next round and Custodians, but compare the bonus with public scoring and plastic.
Politics is an alternate line, not mandatory; do not chase Custodians automatically. Depends on order, race, objective and slice; hosts lean public scoring if the race stretches Hacan thin. [SCPT 360, 45:44–46:40; s000819–s000834.

### C08 — Choose other opening cards by need: Leadership for tokens, Construction for a dock, Warfare is usually unnecessary with two carriers, Technology can accelerate Gravity Drive.
Diplomacy is low without a valuable slice target, while Construction and Technology have clearer jobs; card value depends on the opening. Alternatives; check slice, secondaries and objective first. [SCPT 360, 43:57–45:41; s000779–s000817.

Trade is the strongest default when available and the table will transact. Construction is a distinct forward-dock plan; Politics can set up a round-two Custodians attempt; Technology buys research tempo. Leadership fills a token need, while Warfare is usually expendable with two carriers. These are alternatives: a public objective or safe expansion can matter more than an attractive card.

### C09 — Prioritize Gravity Drive and Quantum Datahub Node; QDN’s deep yellow route can replace dependence on speaker and Politics.
The episode calls QDN a must-get while recognizing its investment and alternative access to speaker. Research tempo and useful card swaps must justify it; hosts acknowledge Politics alternatives. [SCPT 360, 21:45–23:35; s000382–s000408.

### C10 — Skip Production Biomes in the usual Hacan game: its green path, action and token cost too much for income Hacan rarely needs.
Too deep into green, duplicates the agent and has poor return for wealthy Hacan. Reconsider only if green path/token are unusually cheap and income unusually valuable. [SCPT 360, 20:00–21:45; s000350–s000381.

### C11 — Use Carth’s agent to replenish another player’s commodities more often than taking two for Hacan; ask elsewhere if one player hardballs.
Washing a partner can return comparable value, and Carth can target someone else if negotiations fail. Recipient must have commodities and accept; a modest favor may be reasonable. [SCPT 360, 24:20–26:11; s000420–s000466.

### C12 — Let Trade washes create Gila’s temporary ten-good unlock; do not force the commander in round one.
Money can briefly reach ten before transfer; the hosts say the unlock can wait until round two. Agenda votes matter when an important agenda or alliance makes them consequential. [SCPT 360, 27:57–29:20; s000489–s000518.

### C13 — Score objectives early enough to unlock Harrugh Gefhara, but do not contort the game for a huge free build.
They hope to use the hero around round three but say scoring remains primary and the free build may be small. Quick unlock helps when production is ready; limited capacity can make the build modest. [SCPT 360, 30:00–32:34; s000527–s000575.

Keep the point as the priority. Unlocking the hero is useful when production is ready, but a free build that cannot fit under the dock limit does not repay a missed score.

### C14 — Keep the trade network broad and low-friction instead of taxing each exchange; this can reduce resentment and ease later planet deals.
The hosts say constant taxes made deals harder; open transactions helped later negotiations. Political style only; Hacan can refuse deals or exclude a hostile player. [SCPT 360, 63:07–64:45; s001156–s001180.

### C15 — Trade planets when points or position are worth more than lost economy; the mech can move units and structures during the sale.
They describe sacrificing planets for control points because Hacan can replace some income, not because planets have no value. Need safe destination and scoring/maneuver benefit; loss can hurt control and production. [SCPT 360, 65:30–65:58; s001189–s001193.

### C16 — Trade Convoys can create repeatable late-game stall actions by returning the note through an activation of a Hacan-occupied system.
Play the note, activate Hacan’s system to return it, then pass it back for another action. Uses actions and tokens; useful only if timing helps both players. Stall is not passing. [SCPT 360, 18:12–19:53; s000319–s000345.

### C17 — Pay for tech-granting action cards when they materially improve research tempo toward the core plan.
They call tech tempo a hard limit and would pay premium money for Focused Research or similar cards. Cash competes with plastic, deals and scoring; buy for useful timing. [SCPT 360, 53:36–54:21; s000994–s001010.

### C18 — Treat Transit Diodes versus Predictive Intelligence as a close unresolved choice; one prefers Diodes for moving ground forces, the other Predictive.
One host prefers Transit Diodes, the other Predictive Intelligence; both say the choice is close. Choose by ground-force need, utility and yellow route. [SCPT 360, 57:09–57:55; s001052–s001066.

### C19 — Take Space Dock II as a time-sensitive bonus-tech option around rounds two or three, ideally before the hero build; otherwise abandon it.
Early Space Dock II can turn a hero build into many fighters; if its window is missed, set it aside. Needs bonus research and capacity; Dreadnought II remains an alternative. [SCPT 360, 54:53–57:07; s001018–s001051.

### C20 — War Suns are viable with early AI Development/red skip, but blue/yellow is easier because the War Sun route can delay QDN.
War Suns can be strong, but the route slows QDN and still needs screening. Choose when skips and tempo support both unit and utility plan. [SCPT 360, 52:12–53:17; s000962–s000986.

### C21 — Build defensive hit points, especially fighters and infantry, when home is threatened; expensive ships do not replace a screen.
Fighter/infantry hit points matter and money cannot exceed production capacity. Production, not cash, limits placement. [SCPT 360, 77:42–78:30; s001404–s001415.

### C22 — Use early objective pace to reduce dependence on Hacan’s home; hosts sketch stage ones, Imperial, hero and Mecatol by round four.
Clearing public objectives leaves fewer points vulnerable to home loss, but the pace is restrictive. Ambitious pace may require all five stage ones and restrict tempo. [SCPT 360, 79:31–82:00; s001426–s001460.

### C23 — Keep the winslay carousel active, but treat a round-six Hacan win as possible, not a script; QDN is powerful and visible.
One likes the round-six plan; another says it is visible and hard to negotiate. Shared advice is to let the carousel work. More plausible with another immediate target and useful card access; table/order dependent. [SCPT 360, 72:16–76:51; s001329–s001382.

The speakers disagree about the round-six setup. One likes the opportunity after the table spends round five stopping another leader; the other stresses that QDN makes Hacan’s plan visible. The shared takeaway is to let the winslay carousel work, not to assume the table will forget Hacan.

### C24 — The round-four bait-and-rebound line is explicitly speculative: appear threatening, accept a manageable slay, then recover with QDN and surviving plastic.
Presented as hypothesis, not consensus advice. Only table-specific and controllable; hosts do not know how to execute it. [SCPT 360, 78:17–80:06; s001413–s001432.

### C25 — Buy a few action cards for concrete late-game options rather than entering every hand; War Machine supports builds and surprise cards may preserve a win.
War Machine is useful for builds; later examples include Signal Jamming, but hosts advise restraint in brokering every hand. Look for cash-needing seller and an actual timing use. [SCPT 360, 68:20–69:50; s001240–s001273.

### C26 — Use negotiated gambits when defense cannot secure the win; hosts cite deals that let Hacan attempt scoring through an opponent’s PDS position.
Hacan may win through deals rather than defenses, including a partner-assisted scoring attempt. Needs trust, timing and incentives; failure can expose Hacan. [SCPT 360, 85:00–86:14; s001518–s001537.

### C27 — If QDN and tempo threaten an imminent win, opponents can coordinate pressure before the final round; Hacan’s split home defense is a route.
Opponents anticipate QDN around round four and may attack before the exchange becomes decisive. Applies when Hacan’s tempo threatens a win; early QDN alone may concern fewer players. [SCPT 360, 34:57–36:43; s000625–s000652.

### C28 — Against Sardakk, do not assume sky defense alone protects Hacan’s home; find a matchup-specific or political line.
Sardakk can threaten the ground past sky defense; hosts suggest another solution or support relationship. Specific matchup; elsewhere the hosts return to fighters and ships. [SCPT 360, 38:58–39:24; s000691–s000702.

## Component interactions

- Guild Ships supports non-neighbor transactions; Arbiters lets action cards enter those deals. The hosts describe the abilities together as a utility package.
- A forward Construction dock can return through a Pride of Kenara mech when a planet trade is available.
- Trade can briefly produce the ten goods for Gila’s unlock; QDN can support a later Imperial card exchange. Each line depends on timing and access.

## Counterplay and limits

The clearest counter is pressure on Hacan’s exposed home system: three separate planets dilute ground defense, and limited production constrains ships and screens. The hosts name Sardakk N’orr as a special matchup that can bypass sky-first defense. A strong, imminent QDN win can also unite the table against Hacan around round four. These are conditions to watch, not a universal instruction to attack whenever QDN appears.

The round-six carousel is debated, and the round-four bait-and-rebound line is explicitly called unresearched. The speakers also disagree on Transit Diodes versus Predictive Intelligence. The source is a 2024 guide; ruleset compatibility has not been independently reviewed, and recommendations remain opinions rather than verified rules.
