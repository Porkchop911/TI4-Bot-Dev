# Clan of Saar strategy summary

## Strategic thesis

Saar’s kit makes early expansion, movement, income, and production unusually easy. Manage the attention that advantage attracts: use mobile factories and infantry to build a flexible position, but avoid becoming the only credible leader. A large early board is leverage, yet it gives the table time to coordinate against you. Preserve late scoring options, including after losing your home planets. The guide’s recurring advice is to play tightly enough to remain a late-game threat rather than an obvious early leader. [C01, C02, C24]

This January 2024 SCPT guide is ASR-transcribed; later rules compatibility is unreviewed. “Mellon,” “Lysus,” and “Zobat” may be planet-name recognition errors. [C25]

## Opening choices and round one

**Default: take Construction when the pick is uncertain or you want a robust opening.** Saar’s floating factories are valuable and vulnerable to Reactor Meltdown, so a second dock both protects against losing the only one and unlocks the commander once three are on the board. It also gives flexibility to develop in more than one direction instead of sending every ship and build toward one threatening front. The guide frames Construction as insurance and a relatively low-escalation choice; it is especially attractive for a newer Saar player. It is not an absolute rule: Warfare is the major exception when Saar can exploit its unusually large movement and expansion ceiling. [C03, C04]

Construction can be played early because Saar does not need to land on a planet first just to move its home dock away; however, placing a dock still requires a planet in the system. If a structure objective or forward PDS qualification is at issue, timing and the objective may make taking a planet before the card more useful. Construction’s secondary still builds on planets, and Warfare’s secondary can use only one space dock in a system. These are easy details to mishandle when Saar has multiple mobile docks. [C05]

**Warfare is a high-ceiling alternative when expansion or custodians timing is decisive.** Moving the starting forces forward, lifting the token, and then spreading out can take several planet systems and potentially set up an easy Mecatol Rex approach. One suggested line is to establish the forward position, then use Warfare to clear the home-system token and move out again. Depending on slice and timing, this can produce a very large round-one footprint. But it brings the cost with it: Saar’s wealth and reach make it easy to draw immediate hostility, and a second-action Warfare play can disrupt neighbors’ expected technology or production timing. Choose this line when you can tolerate the political response and have a plan for what those systems do for your scoring pace. [C06, C07]

Leadership is a sound, medium-strength pick when you want command-token momentum and room to follow Technology, Warfare, or Construction. Saar starts with extra carriers and can activate several systems, so it can burn tokens fast; Fleet Pool can constrain later movement. Diplomacy offers resources and can enable custodians, but the hosts describe it as a less attractive route than Saar’s other options. Politics can preserve money for a later Mecatol attempt, but delays the point and risks another player taking it. Trade combines money for technology/plastic with goodwill; it is a lower-drama custodians route than Warfare. [C08, C09, C10]

Do not take Technology just because Saar can afford it. The agent already helps movement, Scavenge adds money as Saar expands, and plastic often creates more reach and threat than an extra technology. The guide’s rule of thumb is that Chaos Mapping is the essential faction technology; Gravity Drive is an opening-specific choice, and Carrier II is a strong follow-up. If Gravity Drive is what makes round-one custodians possible, prioritize it. Otherwise, Chaos Mapping first can support a slower game, followed by Carrier II for capacity and a deeper fighter screen. The speakers disagree in emphasis about how much Gravity Drive improves the game, but agree it is not mandatory in every position. [C11, C12]

## Economy, production, and force composition

Scavenge pays one trade good after Saar gains control of a planet. The mech can then be deployed by spending a trade good after that same gain, so the income can effectively fund a mech when you value the mech more than keeping the money. On an explored planet, exploration happens before the control trigger, so this timing does not let Saar spend a newly gained trade good to influence that exploration. Early expansion therefore funds both builds and selective mech deployment, but the trade good remains a real opportunity cost. [C13]

Chaos Mapping is powerful because it allows a unit to be produced at the start of each of Saar’s turns in a system with one of its units that has Production. It accelerates plastic without requiring a normal tactical build action, so the earlier Saar researches it, the more turns it can pay off. Do not treat this as a full production build or assume Sarween Tools applies: the guide stresses the wording distinction between “produce” and the Production ability. The start-of-turn unit also does not make Saar a stall faction; it tends to spend actions quickly. [C14]

The floating factories are mobile production and capacity platforms, but a high printed production value is only useful when Saar can pay for the units and move them where needed. The guide recommends prioritizing plastic over optional technologies, especially once Chaos Mapping is online. Carrier II adds capacity that can turn a captured system into a defensible one by adding fighters; Saar’s docks also contribute capacity. An infantry-first opening is a useful alternative to filling carriers with fighters immediately: infantry secures planets and does not consume ship capacity, while early opponents often lack the ground forces to dislodge it. Later, pivot into fighters once Carrier II and the board position support them. This plan is conditional on not leaving critical space fleets undefended. [C15, C16]

Floating Factory II can be worthwhile if Saar reaches it without delaying more urgent movement, production, or scoring needs. It improves the dock’s movement and production/capacity, but the two-yellow route is costly and the speakers say it often arrives late (around round four). Consider it when a rich game, technology objective, or existing yellow access makes the investment reasonable. Commander-enabled production routing still obeys production capacity and cannot send units to a dock that is blockaded. [C17]

## Mobility and tactical positioning

Saar’s agent, Captain Mendosa (ASR spelling; reference lists “Mendosa”), can raise one activated ship’s move value to match the highest move value on the board. The speakers note Saar’s own cruiser gives a round-one move-two option for a carrier or factory, before other movement modifiers apply. Gravity Drive can then add movement, which can make an early Mecatol grab possible. Treat the agent as an important option with an opportunity cost: using it for your own move is often valuable, but the guide says it can be sold when custodians are out of reach and a buyer’s deal is better. Terrain and modifiers still matter; the source’s nebula discussion is an example of why “move value” is not the same as total movement. [C18]

Chaos Mapping also protects Saar ships in asteroid fields from opponents activating those systems, helping Saar hold a mobile production and movement network. The guide warns that this is not blanket immunity: effects that move ships without activation, including the cited Benediction interaction, can pull a fleet out of safety. Do not pass while your only viable retreat or survival position depends on the asteroid field. Losing access at the wrong moment can make an otherwise threatening Saar position vulnerable to a coordinated attack. [C19, C20]

Dark Energy Tap is a flexible later option when the economy supports it. Frontier exploration can provide tokens or money, and retreat can help a mobile faction whose fleet has moved away from its controlled planets. The retreat option is especially useful when Saar has several ground-force positions or empty systems to retreat toward. Avoid a position where a retreat would strand one floating factory on another and lock both into a bad position. DET competes with more immediate technology or plastic, so take it when its exploration and escape routes solve a real late-game problem. [C21]

## Scoring and endgame

Saar can score objectives without controlling its home-system planets. That weakens the common win-slay line of taking home to prevent scoring and gives Saar a way to keep scoring after losing the home planets. It does not make Saar impossible to eliminate: Saar still needs planets for placing floating factories through Construction and can be defeated through other routes. Keep planets and infantry distributed enough to avoid being eliminated, and do not mistake home-system scoring protection for general safety. [C02, C05]

The hosts describe Saar as broadly capable of stage-one, secret, and stage-two scoring, with exceptions. Saar can pursue custodians, take Mecatol later with Imperial, and produce units when a unit-based secret requires them. A nine-ground-force secret is easier when Saar builds infantry onto a controlled planet while its dock remains in the system. These are preparation advantages, not automatic points: check the objective requirements and timing. [C22]

“Improve Infrastructure” is a notable bad fit because Saar’s floating factory is in space and does not count as a structure on a planet; the source says PDS is the relevant structure for that objective. Whether to invest in PDS depends on the revealed objectives, Saar’s path to ten, and whether the point can be replaced by a stage two, bonus point, or Imperial/Mecatol point. Do not sink resources into PDS by default. A PDS secret can change the calculation, while Dark Energy Tap can help cycle secrets if Saar needs another draw. [C23]

The hero can destroy other players’ infantry and fighters in a system adjacent to one of Saar’s space docks. The guide presents it as a late-game answer to a high-HP fleet that Saar might otherwise struggle to clear. “Adjacent” means an adjacent system, not units in the same system as the dock; the hero does not clear ground forces directly below a dock. Preserve the hero until a strategically important system and the relevant enemy units line up. [C24]

## Politics, deals, and counters

Saar begins with threat heat; acknowledge it openly. Scavenge income and mobile docks let Saar offer friendly planet trades, pay for goodwill, and use the promissory note to preserve ground forces. Rog’s Call is often more useful to Saar than to the recipient: it relocates Saar’s ground forces after an opponent commits to land, letting Saar avoid combat and redeploy those forces. The guide notes the recipient chooses where those forces go, so do not promise a destination you cannot control. [C25]

Extortion should be a credible choice, not a bluff made on the assumption that the other player will yield. Before demanding a Support for the Throne or payment, decide whether following through benefits Saar even if it creates a lasting enemy. A small payment to redirect Saar toward another threat, or a support swap that removes Saar from someone’s immediate concern, may be more reliable than making yourself the whole table’s target. Table culture matters: a group comfortable with aggression may accept a sharper threat; another table may coordinate to slow Saar down. [C26]

Against Saar, opponents should respect its mobility, income, and capacity to put pressure on home systems early, while avoiding trades that strengthen an already leading Saar player. Do not assume taking both Saar home planets is always a punishment: the source points out that this can remove Saar’s own contingency for Shard of the Throne scoring. If contesting Saar’s home, leaving one planet may preserve a way to deny that point later. Chaos Mapping’s asteroid protection can be bypassed by non-activation movement effects, so watch for those effects and time a strike when Saar leaves safety or has passed. Saar players should likewise avoid spreading so far that the entire table can coordinate against the only obvious leader. [C20, C27]
