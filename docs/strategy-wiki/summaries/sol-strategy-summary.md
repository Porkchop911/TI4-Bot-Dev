# Sol strategy summary — SCPT 328

Sol’s guide-era plan is to turn a dependable carrier start, extra command tokens and strong ground forces into early expansion, then win with capacity, infantry and a carefully timed hero. The hosts favor a modest technology path and flexible money. Advice depends on the slice, objectives, table economy and the attention Sol attracts. This is a source-grounded draft, not current-rules verification or a training label.

## Source and scope

Space Cats Peace Turtles, “328: Super Federation of Sol Guide,” published 2023-12-06; complete checksum-verified transcript, episode ID scpt-328-db4a3e239705. Advice is concentrated from about 00:18 to 01:42. No speaker identities are assigned. The guide assumes Prophecy of Kings, a six-player table and, for trade estimates, an X-minus-one commodity meta. Current compatibility is unreviewed.

## Starting position and ground game

**C01.** Sol starts with a dependable expansion package: two carriers, five infantry, three fighters, Anti-Mass Deflectors and Neural Motivator. The five infantry are a key part of occupying two systems even when a slice offers three planets; the hosts call the start solid rather than exceptional. **Source:** 00:18:15–00:19:31 (s000295–s000318).

**C02.** Advanced Carrier I has capacity six but remains a Carrier I: cost three, combat nine, movement one. The guide warns against treating its extra capacity as the movement upgrade; it carries most of Sol’s early ground force and fighters. **Source:** 00:22:26–00:23:37 (s000360–s000375).

**C03.** Advanced Carrier II is the central unit upgrade: movement and capacity eight are the useful gains. The hosts regard Sustain Damage as marginal because a loaded carrier can take fighter hits instead, and a Direct Hit can punish risking the hull. **Source:** 00:23:40–00:25:15 (s000377–s000402).

**C04.** Orbital Drop spends a strategy token to place two infantry on a planet Sol controls. The hosts call it expensive and situational, while stressing its value as a stall when the added infantry also matter. **Source:** 00:21:10–00:22:01 (s000339–s000353).

**C05.** Versatile adds one command token to each status-phase gain: Sol gets three in its first status phase, and the hosts note four per phase with Hyper Metabolism. That surplus supports secondaries and later flexibility, provided tokens are spent on useful actions rather than burned without purpose. **Source:** 00:22:01–00:23:16 (s000354–s000371); 00:57:03–00:58:29 (s000929–s000951).

**C06.** The hosts generally skip Spec Ops II. Their stated plan is to win ground fights through more transported units and hit points; improving Spec Ops from hits on seven to hits on six is not worth diverting early resources in their baseline plan. Gen Synthesis is described as a better return roll than the generic version, but not as a reason to buy the upgrade. **Source:** 00:25:16–00:26:18 (s000403–s000425).

**C07.** Genesis brings cost-eight, combat-five-on-two stats and capacity twelve. Its status-phase ability places one infantry in its system’s space area; the hosts disagree at first about how useful that is, then favor remembering the free infantry because Sol wants ground-force volume and is unlikely to fill the flagship’s capacity. **Source:** 00:26:21–00:27:23 (s000427–s000444); 00:28:13–00:28:31 (s000457–s000461).

**C08.** The Sol mech is a situational tool, not a build-around plan. After Orbital Drop, Sol may pay three resources to place a mech on that planet; the hosts advise remembering the option for a late need while rejecting a dedicated rush built around it. **Source:** 00:29:15–00:30:35 (s000473–s000489).

**C09.** Sol’s agent adds a die to one ground force in the active system for a ground-combat round. The hosts value it as a free way to swing low-number fights: in their 2-versus-1 example, the extra die can create the hit needed to avoid losing on a defensive tie. **Source:** 00:30:37–00:32:04 (s000491–s000521).

**C10.** Sol’s commander unlocks after controlling planets with at least twelve combined resources; home contributes four. At the start of ground combat on a controlled planet, it can add one infantry there, which compounds with Spec Ops I and the agent. **Source:** 00:34:29–00:35:29 (s000570–s000588).

**C11.** A single infantry planet can be much harder to invade once the commander is online: the guide’s example adds an infantry and combines the commander with the agent and Spec Ops to roll three dice on sevens. The speakers name heavy bombardment or focused mech fire as answers. **Source:** 00:35:30–00:36:34 (s000589–s000607).

**C12.** Sol’s hero removes its command tokens from the board and returns them to reinforcements. The guide treats this as both a scoring and tempo tool: hold a home fleet for defense, use the round to act or stall, then reclaim tokens and send that fleet out to finish a round or contest objectives. **Source:** 00:37:33–00:39:01 (s000626–s000649); 00:49:00–00:49:59 (s000812–s000826).

**C13.** Military Support is a repeatable table offer tied to Sol’s four commodities. The card’s described start-of-turn effect returns a strategy-pool token and lets its holder place two infantry from Sol’s reinforcements on a planet Sol controls, then returns the card to Sol. **Source:** 00:40:15–00:41:36 (s000670–s000692).

**C14.** The hosts say Military Support can be traded and played immediately during its start-of-turn window, and can still place infantry when Sol has no token in its strategy pool. On that interpretation Sol can offer it after spending the pool, including on its pass turn. **Source:** 00:41:05–00:42:34 (s000682–s000708); 00:42:34–00:43:06 (s000709–s000721).

**C15.** One host’s baseline sale price for Military Support is one trade good or an equivalent favor, and the recommendation is to offer it repeatedly rather than accept less. They reject vague future promises as payment; the other host says aggressive selling can attract heat. **Source:** 00:43:00–00:43:48 (s000719–s000728); 01:27:17–01:28:33 (s001421–s001442).

**C16.** The guide presents Military Support’s price as table-dependent. An active, deal-friendly meta may yield several trade goods or favors over a round; at a more violent table one host worries about arming opponents, while the other says Sol can out-capacity and out-punch them. Both note sales may add heat. **Source:** 00:45:37–00:46:39 (s000758–s000774); 00:47:01–00:47:36 (s000781–s000790).

## Openings and strategy cards

**C17.** In a baseline round one, prioritize Gravity Drive; the hosts say other technology is extra and should depend on objectives or slice needs. Their broader advice is to build plastic instead of double-teching when the faction has a shallow technology path and can acquire needed upgrades later. **Source:** 00:55:36–00:56:10 (s000904–s000910); 01:08:52–01:09:54 (s001125–s001148).

**C18.** Leadership is an unusually attractive round-one strategy card for Sol in the hosts’ view: Versatile turns its token gain into an early lead, as long as Sol spends the tokens on worthwhile actions. **Source:** 00:56:14–00:57:03 (s000912–s000928).

**C19.** Do not confuse taking Politics with following its secondary. The Politics secondary supplies action cards; the hosts say Sol’s command-token economy makes it easy to afford that secondary and can support taking several useful secondaries across a round. They also criticize researching Neural Motivator solely for cards Sol can obtain this way. **Source:** 00:57:03–00:58:29 (s000929–s000951).

**C20.** The hosts dislike Diplomacy as Sol’s own pick because it opens a round-one Custodians window for someone else and makes already wealthy neighbors richer. If another player takes it, they expect Sol to follow; in a last-pick situation they prefer Construction, especially when a structure objective or forward dock matters. **Source:** 00:58:44–00:59:36 (s000955–s000967); 01:00:41–01:01:44 (s000982–s001000).

**C21.** Trade is a strong round-one option when Sol wants persistent flexible money. The hosts accept that taking it probably concedes Custodians, while noting that a negotiated speaker-token purchase after another player takes Politics can sometimes preserve a Mecatol line. **Source:** 01:01:46–01:02:53 (s001001–s001020).

**C22.** The X-minus-one example explains why the hosts favor Trade over Diplomacy: trade goods persist and can pay varied economic costs, while refreshed planets must be spent in that round and also require a token. Under their six-player, four-commodity illustration, they estimate Sol can make about twelve trade goods while other players gain roughly two or three each. **Source:** 01:03:20–01:04:10 (s001027–s001041); 01:04:42–01:05:24 (s001050–s001062).

**C23.** Round-one Warfare is a viable but conditional Sol opening. The proposed line follows Technology for Gravity Drive, may follow Politics for cards, sells Military Support, saves Warfare until late, then moves again; the speakers budget six resources, with Trade and Military Support covering the example. **Source:** 01:05:38–01:07:03 (s001066–s001088); 01:08:18–01:08:49 (s001114–s001122).

**C24.** A round-one Custodians double-tech route is preserved as contested theorycraft: one host proposes Trade income, Gravity Drive, Advanced Carrier II and enough influence from Diplomacy; the other calls the line exceptionally unlikely. Both agree it needs favorable slice, timing and resources. **Source:** 01:12:10–01:13:46 (s001182–s001213).

## Technology and production

**C25.** Among supplemental blue technologies, the hosts favor Sling Relay; Light/Wave Deflector is valued for the hero, while Dark Energy Tap ranks last because Sol already starts with blue technology and is not desperate for frontier rewards. **Source:** 01:17:25–01:18:20 (s001284–s001293).

**C26.** Biostims and Sling Relay are explicitly recommended together. The speakers’ rationale is that extra start-of-turn windows create more chances to sell Military Support; they also describe using the combination to bring out capital ships while later home builds focus on fighters and infantry. **Source:** 01:18:20–01:19:57 (s001294–s001314).

**C27.** Scanlink Drone Network is an attractive way to spend Sol’s surplus tokens and economy on uncertain exploration returns. Predictive Intelligence is not a priority because Sol is not token-starved, though the hosts identify a conditional hero interaction after spreading tokens to stall and then reclaiming them. **Source:** 01:19:58–01:20:57 (s001315–s001326).

**C28.** The hosts recommend no red technology and do not prioritize AI Development Algorithm: Sol has money but can run short on production capacity, so they prefer spending economy to improve odds of winning rather than accumulating more resources. **Source:** 01:22:53–01:24:02 (s001358–s001372).

**C29.** Advanced Carrier II is usually enough for the transport plan. Fighter II and Dreadnought II are matchup or objective choices: Dreadnought II may add needed punch or bombardment, while Fighter II can help with maneuverability and control objectives that require reaching empty space. **Source:** 01:24:03–01:24:59 (s001373–s001384); 01:25:37–01:26:46 (s001396–s001414).

## Objectives and agendas

**C30.** For stage-one objectives, the guide sees Sol as comfortable in technology, control and economy, but weak in structures unless an objective gives a reason to invest. For stage two, it favors control objectives; economy is manageable, structures remain poor, and the hero plus ground-force tools help secure planets. **Source:** 01:37:43–01:38:25 (s001591–s001605); 01:40:50–01:41:40 (s001654–s001669).

**C31.** Mechanize the Military is described as a late pivot: Orbital Drop plus a home build puts Sol “halfway done,” and the speakers say Sol can still find time to finish it. That is preparation progress, not a claim that the objective is scored by placing those two mechs. **Source:** 01:40:06–01:40:53 (s001639–s001654).

**C32.** Agenda preferences follow Sol’s strengths and counters: Conventions of War is favored because it blocks bombardment on cultural planets; Economic Equality is opposed because Sol wants saved trade goods; Executive Sanctions is bad either way for its action-card plan; Prophecy of Ix is attractive because Sol already builds fighters. Regulated Conscription is contested by timing, but the hosts dislike its early cost increase to infantry/fighter production. **Source:** 01:32:01–01:33:18 (s001490–s001511); 01:33:18–01:34:50 (s001512–s001530); 01:34:50–01:36:31 (s001531–s001564).

## Deals, counters and draft context

**C33.** Sol may trade away its commander alliance for roughly two trade goods, a trade good plus a side benefit, or a mid-tier commander swap. The guide highlights Ghost when a wormhole is in the slice, Sardakk and Cabal for combat or production, Nomad for a cheaper flagship, and S.A.R. as a mutually useful swap. **Source:** 01:28:34–01:29:47 (s001443–s001458); 01:29:48–01:30:52 (s001459–s001471).

**C34.** Heat is Sol’s main weakness in the guide: the faction is visibly strong but lacks the relationship tools that help some other top factions defuse attention. The speakers also describe Sol as straightforward—build, expand and win through solid play—with the hero as its standout trick. **Source:** 00:50:29–00:51:15 (s000830–s000844); 00:51:53–00:52:27 (s000856–s000865).

**C35.** The hosts say Sol can remain a good pick even when it falls to a late draft position: some players avoid it as “boring,” leaving a strong faction available around fifth pick. They present it as a clear, low-complexity way to learn the game and still compete. **Source:** 00:54:29–00:55:03 (s000894–s000900); 00:52:52–00:53:38 (s000873–s000884).

## Supported connections

Biostims and Sling Relay are the clearest named technology pairing, through additional windows to offer Military Support. Predictive Intelligence is a narrower hero-turn option when Sol has spread tokens to stall. Versatile supports the round-one Leadership line by making its token gain unusually large. The agent and commander each reinforce Sol infantry in different ground-combat situations; Genesis adds another infantry while carrying Sol’s ground force. These are the hosts’ strategic connections, not independent rules findings.

## Limits

The episode is one guide, not a consensus review. Some advice is tied to a 2023 meta and the speakers’ table norms. The immediate-trade and empty-strategy-pool Military Support rulings are described as old and are not verified here. ASR doubts, differing opinions, partial objective preparation and the distinction between card choice and secondary are retained in the claim text and JSON.
