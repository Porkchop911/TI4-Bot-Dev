# Barony of Letnev — strategy summary draft

**Source:** SCPT 463, “The Ultimate Guide to the Barony of Letnev,” published 2026-07-01. This is podcast advice, not a current-rules review. The conversation is largely about the newly discussed Thunder’s Edge breakthrough, Graveliche Maneuvers; some older Prophecy of Kings advice is explicitly superseded. Times point into the verified episode transcript; claim IDs match `letnev.json`.

## Strategic thesis

The guide’s central change is that Barony’s breakthrough turns a mixed fleet into a fast fleet: while Graveliche Maneuvers is unlocked, a non-fighter ship can inherit the highest movement value of the moving ships that began together in its system. That broad movement, plus a combat bonus tied to ship-type variety, changes Barony from a faction that merely likes fights into one that can contest the center and the Fracture on an accelerated schedule. The speakers treat the breakthrough as the defining opening priority, even when acquiring it costs a secret objective or delays round-one economy. That is emphatic advice for the episode’s assumed Thunder’s Edge environment, not a claim that the same priority fits every table or rules era. **[C01–C03]**

## Opening and development priorities

**C01 — Take advantage of the resource-rich home, but remember the token and commodity pinch.** The speakers count Arc Prime and Rentera together as six home resources, while Barony has only two commodities and no home influence. That resource pool gives room to pay for a breakthrough or a technology/build sequence, but spending it all at once can leave no resources for secondaries or ships. The older suggestion that this automatically makes round-one double technology attractive is expressly walked back later: do not treat six resources as a standing instruction to double-tech. **[00:25:35–00:26:54; s000298–s000313; 00:26:54–00:28:13; s000314–s000330; 01:52:08–01:53:20; s001398–s001414]**

**C02 — Secure Graveliche Maneuvers before ordinary technology when the choice is real.** The speakers rank the breakthrough above a single technology because its movement benefit can apply across multiple ships and systems, whereas Gravity Drive improves one ship’s movement. They call it transformational and say they would accept a slow round two or discard a secret if needed. This is not an automatic discard rule: an available Imperial or Diplomacy line, a valuable secret, scoring, and the slice can change how to pay. **[00:55:14–00:56:21; s000657–s000674; 01:05:19–01:06:27; s000788–s000803; 01:07:36–01:08:53; s000826–s000840; 01:36:10–01:36:44; s001204–s001210]**

**C03 — Treat Diplomacy, Trade, and Imperial as different routes, not interchangeable “breakthrough cards.”** Taking Diplomacy gives control over when to refresh: one speaker prefers following Tech or Warfare first, taking a system, then using Diplomacy to refresh and pay for the breakthrough. Trade is repeatedly praised because Barony needs money and it can support both breakthrough costs and later action. Imperial can pay for the breakthrough through its secondary if it is in play, but that can let another player take Imperial while Barony ditches its secret; Leadership can instead provide a token and a later Imperial draw. If Imperial is absent, the speakers identify Diplomacy as a possible alternative. These are conditional lines dependent on card order, slice, and the opportunity cost of secrets or secondaries. **[01:05:53–01:07:30; s000796–s000820; 01:43:10–01:44:08; s001289–s001293; 01:47:40–01:49:09; s001338–s001357]**

**C04 — Draft for influence and tokens as well as resources.** With no home influence, the speakers suggest roughly six influence in the slice as a practical minimum, especially because Barony has two commodities and needs command tokens to move and keep acting. They would accept a lower-resource, higher-influence slice more readily than a slice that compounds the influence shortage, since the home system already supplies resources. Force projection does not solve a token shortage: if opponents stall Barony out or it cannot afford tokens, ships cannot convert into useful actions. **[01:31:02–01:32:01; s001136–s001145]**

**C05 — Politics is a strong route to a round-two Mecatol Rex attempt.** The speakers value Politics highly because Barony starts with blue technology, can use its movement package to approach Mecatol Rex, and can follow the player who takes the first approach. They describe Barony as a plausible second-round Custodians contender and note Titans as a serious rival: Barony may have stronger dice, while Titans bring sustain. This is a contested space, not a guaranteed point; assess the competing fleet, order, and chance to make a deal. **[01:44:34–01:46:06; s001305–s001318]**

**C06 — Trade is the most consistently praised pick; Politics is also high, while the middle of the card order is situational.** One speaker calls Trade a top pick for money, scoring support, and breakthrough access. The other puts Trade and Politics first, with Leadership, Diplomacy, and Tech next according to slice and table. Construction and Warfare divide the speakers: Construction’s double home docks and production can preserve the same reach as Warfare once Graveliche is active; Warfare is attractive when its secondary build, an objective, or a juicy nearby system makes it pay. Tech remains useful, but neither speaker favors routine double-tech. **[01:47:40–01:49:09; s001338–s001357; 01:49:09–01:50:00; s001358–s001367; 01:50:27–01:51:29; s001368–s001385; 01:51:30–01:52:07; s001386–s001397; 01:54:52–01:56:01; s001438–s001454; 01:53:22–01:54:17; s001415–s001429]**

**C07 — Use Graveliche as a fleet-wide movement relay only for ships that begin together.** The cited example starts with a carrier, dreadnought, and destroyer; the destroyer’s movement lets the others move two. Gravity Drive or temporary movement bonuses can raise that ceiling. The critical condition is that the ships start in the same system and move as one group; the guide’s “tugboat” metaphor does not grant movement to ships elsewhere. A rare Fighter II case is raised as a possible carrier-like escort, with capacity checked at the destination, but the speakers frame it as an unusual edge case. **[00:52:43–00:54:12; s000621–s000633; 01:03:03–01:03:36; s000759–s000765]**

**C08 — Do not delay the movement breakthrough for Gravity Drive by reflex.** The episode says Graveliche can already make round-two movement adequate, and in that setting the breakthrough outranks a single-ship Gravity Drive. Gravity Drive remains a core technology and can extend the system’s reach when needed; the guide’s advice is about sequencing, not abandoning it. The speakers also acknowledge the interpretation was discussed as a ruling-era interaction and could change with later rules clarification. **[00:55:14–00:56:21; s000657–s000674; 01:36:10–01:36:44; s001204–s001210]**

**C09 — Consider the Scepter of Emelpar when it can refund breakthrough spending.** If Emelpar is nearby, the speakers describe spending home resources on Graveliche, moving to take the relic, then using its refresh to recover four resources for technology or plastic. They call it a high-priority opportunity because Barony combines a strong resource home with exceptional reach. This line depends on the relic being present, reachable, and worth the action and exposure; the two influence are a bonus, while the resource refund is the stated reason. **[01:03:36–01:04:59; s000766–s000783]**

## Fleet, technology, and combat

**C10 — Destroyer II is a serious early option, sometimes ahead of Gravity Drive.** One speaker ranks it among Barony’s core technologies and describes using it to contest the Fracture’s two-planet system: destroyers pressure fighters while the fleet fights for space and mechs help on the ground. It also threatens Mecatol Rex and can deter an opponent. This is board-dependent; inspect the fleet and ground force. The other speaker is persuaded by the early punch, while noting that prior games sometimes had more movement than they could use. **[02:01:34–02:02:40; s001526–s001536; 02:03:57–02:05:01; s001554–s001568; 02:05:03–02:06:43; s001570–s001594; 02:06:35–02:07:10; s001594–s001600; 02:07:51–02:08:27; s001611–s001618]**

**C11 — The recurring core technology package is Gravity Drive, Non-Euclidean Shielding, and Carrier II, with Destroyer II as a strong competing priority.** One speaker names the first three as the ideal set; the other adds Destroyer II as an early favorite. Non-Euclidean Shielding doubles the hit cancellation from sustain, making the flagship, dreadnoughts, and mechs more durable. Duranium Armor becomes more attractive because repair can restore more value. Adapt to objectives, skips, and tempo; this is not a checklist. **[01:58:59–01:59:51; s001491–s001502; 00:47:39–00:49:04; s000565–s000578; 00:49:04–00:50:38; s000579–s000591; 02:06:35–02:07:10; s001594–s001600]**

**C12 — Avoid routine Dreadnought II; the guide prefers destroyer upgrades and movement.** One speaker says Dreadnought II no longer adds enough for this plan and recommends accepting that Direct Hit may remove the flagship or dreadnought. This is partly a shift from earlier Barony advice, not a rules fact. The opposing view favors Destroyer II more than the first speaker, especially because Barony has extra fleet capacity and can use destroyers as escorts. **[02:00:00–02:01:15; s001507–s001520]**

**C13 — Use combat bonuses where the combat matters, without over-optimizing each die.** Graveliche adds a bonus to one ship’s roll based on ship-type variety; the agent adds a die to a chosen ship for that round. One proposed heuristic is to give Graveliche to a ship rolling the most dice when Munitions Reserves is not being used, or to improve a low-hit-chance ship when it is, then use the agent on the ship with the best resulting hit chance. Another speaker says the difference is marginal enough to focus on a critical combat and choose sensibly rather than calculate every possible percentage. The advice itself is contested at the fine-grained level. **[00:56:54–00:58:28; s000681–s000696; 00:58:30–00:59:30; s000697–s000713; 00:59:31–01:00:19; s000714–s000722; 00:58:30–00:59:30; s000697–s000713; 00:59:31–01:00:19; s000714–s000722; 01:00:19–01:01:41; s000723–s000739; 01:01:41–01:02:30; s000740–s000749; 01:11:17–01:12:48; s000876–s000895]**

**C14 — Munitions Reserves is a paid reroll for ordinary space-combat dice.** At the start of each space-combat round, spending two trade goods permits rerolling any number of Barony’s combat dice for that round; it does not reroll Space Cannon, Bombardment, or Anti-Fighter Barrage. It can be used again in a later round, but not repeatedly in the same round after its window has passed. The speakers call it useful but niche because Barony’s economy is often tight. **[00:29:42–00:31:21; s000351–s000365; 00:31:21–00:32:15; s000366–s000377]**

**C15 — Armada makes a low fleet pool workable, but does not remove the cost of running low on tokens.** Barony may field up to two more non-fighter ships per system than tokens in its fleet pool, allowing two ships even with zero fleet tokens. The guide therefore endorses taking some fleet tokens for command-token needs. The guide notes that Barony can still field five ships when another rule sets a general cap of four. Having no fleet token to lose can also avoid a specific effect that removes one. Neither interaction makes all fleet-token effects harmless or a token-starved game functional. **[00:32:15–00:33:34; s000378–s000391; 00:33:34–00:34:46; s000392–s000404; 00:34:48–00:36:18; s000405–s000429]**

**C16 — Spend fleet tokens deliberately, since both action tempo and ship cap matter.** Armada helps preserve a useful ship count while Barony moves tokens into tactics or strategy pools, but Barony still needs tokens to activate systems and avoid being stalled. A zero-fleet-pool position can create edge-case benefits against specific effects, yet the episode does not advise pursuing zero as a general goal. **[00:32:15–00:33:34; s000378–s000391; 01:31:02–01:32:01; s001136–s001145]**

**C17 — Preserve a mech in reinforcements if you want the Dunlane Reaper’s deploy option.** At the start of a ground-combat round, the mech can replace an infantry in that combat for two resources. It needs an infantry to replace and a mech still in reinforcements; the episode specifically warns that deploy does not scuttle a mech already on the board. Since only one can be deployed per round, having resources does not let Barony deploy several at once. The speakers suggest one reserve mech as a flexible option, with the others available for ordinary use. **[00:39:07–00:40:00; s000458–s000464; 00:40:30–00:41:25; s000465–s000474; 00:41:25–00:41:57; s000475–s000482]**

**C18 — The flagship is worth fielding when its shield removal and self-repair can decide space combat.** Arc Secundus is described as combat-capable, with capacity and bombardment; at the start of each space-combat round, opposing units in its system lose planetary shield and the flagship repairs. The speakers advise using it despite the chance of Direct Hit, because its ability can win combats; they do not claim it is invulnerable, and one speaker never managed to afford their planned Fracture use. **[00:37:22–00:38:06; s000439–s000447; 00:38:06–00:39:07; s000448–s000457]**

**C19 — Sell or trade War Funding when an opportunity appears, while planning around its timing.** After a roll, the holder can reroll all opponent dice and any number of their own. The note is potent but tricky to sell: Barony cannot initiate a trade while defending, so a defensive buyer must acquire it ahead of time. It rerolls misses too and may worsen the result. If nobody pays in the combat window, the speakers still prefer some value over keeping it unused. **[00:41:57–00:43:15; s000483–s000500; 00:43:16–00:44:37; s000501–s000520; 02:10:56–02:11:46; s001658–s001673]**

## Economy, scoring, and table interactions

**C20 — Expect to be cash-poor despite Barony’s resource home.** The guide says Barony spends money on combat rerolls, mechs, and other plans while starting with only two commodities. Trade and midgame Leadership are proposed ways to replenish resources and tokens; deals or carefully chosen extortion are another. The home system can pay early costs, but it does not erase the ongoing need for command tokens, plastic, and trade goods. **[00:29:33–00:30:46; s000349–s000358; 01:38:15–01:38:58; s001229–s001238; 01:38:59–01:39:56; s001239–s001249]**

**C21 — Barony can make credible leverage plays because mobility, ships, and combat give threats weight.** The speakers consider aggressive bargaining a real strategic option, not a universal opening. It works best when a target is ahead, exposed, or has something valuable to trade and Barony can actually reach the relevant system. They describe a round-four hero threat that extracted trade goods and technology, while cautioning that the leverage mattered because the opponents were ahead and the fleets could converge. **[01:23:49–01:25:19; s001026–s001051; 01:25:19–01:26:37; s001052–s001077; 01:26:37–01:27:51; s001078–s001090; 01:27:51–01:28:40; s001091–s001102]**

**C22 — Convert a sustain-heavy table into commander income only when combat can eventually end.** After Barony unlocks Rear Admiral Farran by having five non-fighter ships in a system, each of Barony’s units that uses Sustain Damage can gain one trade good. This is usually modest, with the speakers guessing roughly eight to ten trade goods over a game depending on fighting. They discuss unusually large loops involving repair and other factions’ sustain effects, but explicitly note that a combat that cannot produce a winner must stop; they regard a nominally finite but highly unlikely endpoint as a murky boundary, not a guaranteed “infinite money” engine. Mentak’s Pillage can also interrupt each one-good gain. **[01:14:38–01:15:26; s000911–s000919; 01:15:26–01:16:56; s000920–s000935; 01:16:56–01:17:43; s000936–s000947; 01:17:45–01:18:42; s000948–s000959; 01:18:43–01:19:56; s000960–s000979; 01:19:56–01:21:24; s000980–s000995; 02:21:41–02:22:29; s001804–s001819]**

**C23 — Treat outside sustain and combat effects as negotiated synergies, not automatic income.** Nomad’s Cavalry can give a Barony ship Sustain Damage, and the speakers suggest proactively offering a trade good when the sustain triggers; this can make the unit harder to remove under Non-Euclidean Shielding. They also describe possible sustain-and-repair trades with Empyrean, Titans, or Nekro, including a very unusual extended-combat example. These deals depend on the other player’s components, enough hit points and repair resources, and an ending that remains possible. **[02:13:27–02:14:29; s001701–s001708; 02:19:09–02:20:47; s001769–s001790]**

**C24 — Build for the sky without neglecting infantry and the ground.** Barony starts with three infantry and the speakers say they often run short while prioritizing fighters and space combat. Extra infantry can help secure planets and provide replacements for Dunlane Reaper deployments; the speakers suggest trading for Sol’s Military Support as one ground-force remedy. If Barony has no answer to X-89, the guide acknowledges that it may be better to defend space than to assume it will win a ground battle. **[02:14:39–02:15:49; s001711–s001724; 02:15:49–02:16:37; s001725–s001734; 01:37:09–01:37:56; s001215–s001224]**

**C25 — Coordinate technology and component trades with Jol-Nar or Last Bastion when the deal is mutual.** Jol-Nar can supply desired technologies such as Destroyer II or X-89, and the guide describes trading support or fragments for access when Barony has good tempo. Last Bastion’s Galvanize adds combat dice that interact with Graveliche, the agent, Munitions Reserves, and Destroyer II. These are opportunities to negotiate, not guaranteed access or proof that a one-way Support for the Throne is always worth its point cost. **[02:11:47–02:13:10; s001674–s001696; 02:16:53–02:18:01; s001737–s001753]**

**C26 — Get to the Fracture early enough to beat the ground-tech answer.** The guide’s desired pace is a round-two Mecatol Rex attempt and a round-three Styx/Fracture move, using Graveliche and other fleets as reinforcements. It calls X-89 the major ground-combat danger and recommends winning in space or arriving before opponents with X-89 can contest the landing. A forward dock and a separate fleet can prepare the follow-up; assess fleet strength and who will arrive first. These are aspirations and examples, not assured scoring or a guaranteed Styx win. **[02:26:15–02:27:34; s001865–s001880; 02:27:35–02:29:02; s001881–s001898; 02:29:03–02:30:34; s001900–s001917; 02:30:34–02:31:32; s001918–s001928; 02:31:37–02:32:46; s001930–s001942]**

**C27 — Crimson is a particularly bad matchup when its flagship can suppress Barony’s sustain plan.** The speakers say Crimson’s relevant flagship ability can turn off Barony’s sustain advantage, including at important locations such as Mecatol Rex or Styx, and they do not identify a reliable answer if the flagship is protected in the contested system. Treat this as a specific warning from the guide, not a general claim that Barony loses to Crimson in every position. **[01:34:18–01:35:21; s001180–s001194; 02:30:34–02:31:32; s001918–s001928; 02:31:37–02:32:15; s001930–s001937]**

**C28 — Favor Malice when its influence and money solve real slice problems.** The guide calls Malice a strong Barony planet because influence and money are both scarce, and because it can stage ships for later leverage. It suggests taking a valuable equidistant when the table position supports it. This remains a map and diplomacy decision: the guide notes competition is lower than it used to be, but does not promise the planet will be safe or available. **[02:34:06–02:34:47; s001958–s001969; 02:36:10–02:36:48; s001988–s001997]**

## Supported connections and caveats

Graveliche Maneuvers, the agent, and ship-type diversity reinforce one another in combat; the movement clause also makes destroyers valuable escorts and lets separate fleets converge only if each has a suitable movement ship. Non-Euclidean Shielding and Sustain Damage components improve durability; the commander can turn repeated sustains into income when combat remains finite. The flagship’s repair and shield removal, the mech’s reserve-dependent deploy, and the War Funding note each have separate timing limits. These links are conditional, and the episodes’ unusual sustain loops are not ordinary economy advice. **[00:52:41–00:54:12; s000621–s000645; 01:11:17–01:19:59; s000876–s001003]**

The speakers explicitly reject routine double-tech compared with older Barony advice, and their card priorities differ on Warfare versus Construction and on how aggressively to discard a secret for the breakthrough. Their strong center/Fracture schedule assumes the discussed Thunder’s Edge environment and enough plastic, tokens, and access. They mention a possible Fighter II tugboat interaction, but as an edge case rather than a dependable plan.
