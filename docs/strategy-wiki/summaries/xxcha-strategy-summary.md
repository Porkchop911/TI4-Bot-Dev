# Xxcha strategy summary (draft)

**Source:** SCPT 264, “Super Xxcha Kingdom Guide,” published 2022-09-14; complete ASR transcript, episode `scpt-264-4e5271acc518`. Times below refer to overlapping transcript segments and identify advice, not rules proof. This guide discusses the then-new Codex 3 “Omega” Xxcha leader set. The speakers repeatedly reason from that changed kit, but do not establish present-day compatibility. Verify current edition, errata, objective deck and expansion mix before applying a line. Proper nouns and some technical phrases come from ASR and may be imperfect.

## Strategic thesis

Xxcha’s revised hero changes the faction’s center of gravity. The guide portrays a slow, awkward opening that must earn its way to an economic and agenda-phase spike: score objectives early enough to unlock Xxekir Grom, then spend the resulting combined planet values toward objectives and votes. The hero does not increase production capacity or alter printed planet values for unrelated effects. Xxcha has late-game potential, while the early game, scoring tempo, limited starting lift and table response remain real constraints. [C01–C04, 00:18:08–00:20:08; 00:22:03–00:28:48; 00:31:20–00:33:06; 01:03:38–01:05:03]

The practical ordering is conditional: first ask what is needed to score the revealed objective; otherwise secure money or planet-refresh value, with Diplomacy a frequent strong pick. Make the round-two hero unlock a plan, not an assumption. The source suggests two public objectives and one secret, often by the end of round two; the Custodians point does not count toward that unlock. If scoring that quickly would wreck the position, the guide entertains taking Imperial later to catch up and draw another secret, or stretching the game while preparing a late win-slay. [C02, C05–C07, C10, C13, C30, 00:27:32–00:31:45; 00:49:18–00:50:08]

## Opening and strategy cards

The speakers describe a difficult opening: one carrier, modest home resources and production, and a starting yellow technology they consider low-impact. Four commodities help, but do not erase the need to build plastic and claim accessible planets. They advise looking at the revealed objectives and slice before choosing a secret: take a secret with a credible round-one or round-two route where possible, including straightforward action-phase opportunities, rather than choosing a speculative late-game secret by habit. An early score has unusually high value because it advances the hero condition. Custodians is explicitly deprioritized; chasing it can distract from the points that unlock the hero. [C01, C02, C05, C06, 00:05:15–00:07:14; 00:21:57–00:23:39; 00:27:53–00:29:36; 00:30:03–00:31:23]

**Diplomacy** is the guide’s standout flexible opening pick. Its primary can refresh planets and protect a system; Xxcha can also use Peace Accords after resolving either Diplomacy primary or secondary to take an adjacent unoccupied planet (not Mecatol Rex). The speakers particularly value taking the primary early: it refreshes planets and can add a planet without spending a command token. The agent can ready a planet too, so the primary and agent provide multiple refresh opportunities. Diplomacy’s value rises further once Xxcha’s hero makes planet spending more valuable, and exploration attachments can make refreshed planets especially productive. The secondary may also be useful, but the speakers call it less common and don’t equate it with taking the card. [C07–C09, C16, 00:07:31–00:08:35; 00:13:51–00:15:44; 00:31:48–00:34:45]

**Trade** is the clearer alternative when the table, neighbors, commodities, or immediate objective make its payout attractive—especially a known high-value first-pick situation. Diplomacy has less visible but potentially greater upside; compare what each actually gives this round. Xxcha’s four commodities and two starting cruisers can make it a workable trading partner, but the guide expects early neighbors to be wary of helping Xxcha reach its stronger later game. Do not treat a prospective deal as guaranteed income. [C10, 00:41:03–00:43:01; 00:44:20–00:46:23]

**Leadership** is not a routine preference in the source’s plan; the speakers name an early “Lead from the Front” objective as a possible exception. **Politics** is also usually behind Diplomacy. Consider it where speaker order and a visible next-round objective setup make it materially improve scoring, or where an unusually constrained secret objective makes its action cards relevant. **Imperial** can be a round-two recovery line: if the table is unlikely to take it and Xxcha can prepare two stage ones, a second public score plus a second secret draw can improve its odds of reaching three objective scores; the secret draw itself is not a point. This is a submitted listener idea the hosts endorse conditionally, not a reliable guarantee. They reject an ordinary round-one Imperial pick as weak unless the available cards and table state are exceptionally unusual. [C11–C13, 00:30:03–00:31:45; 00:33:27–00:34:46; 00:48:58–00:50:07]

**Warfare** is usually better taken by someone else so Xxcha can use its secondary if extra expansion is required. The faction begins short on carriers; a Warfare primary may help in a specific control-objective or adjacent multi-planet situation, but creates awkward timings with Diplomacy and Technology and can leave Xxcha without the plastic it needs. **Technology** is not an automatic round-one pick either. The source prefers money or expansion unless an objective, slice, timing constraint, or rich opening makes a specific technology worth its cost. It advises against double-teching in ordinary openings because the lost early ships make an already fragile position worse. If Xxcha does take Technology, it can control the timing of the secondary and save four resources for ships; this is a situational counterpoint, not the main recommendation. [C14–C15, 00:42:23–00:48:55]

## Hero, economy, scoring and agendas

The episode’s revised, non-purge hero, Xxekir Grom, is its central strategic premise. When Xxcha exhausts planets for a spend, the guide says to combine each planet’s printed resources and influence, then treat that sum as both resources and influence. This benefits spending for objectives and votes. It does **not** raise production capacity; it does not change printed values for commander unlock requirements or other effects that inspect a planet’s inherent value. Keep “spend value” separate from production and printed-value checks. The speaker says Xxcha begins with four influence toward the commander’s 12-influence unlock, and that the hero itself does not count toward it. [C02–C04, 00:18:03–00:20:08]

Attachments improve planet value, which the hero can combine when spending. Refreshing developed planets can make that value available again, though map access, survival, objectives and opportunity cost still matter. Diplomacy, the agent and exploration connect through refreshes and attachment accumulation. Concentrating planet value under the hero can let Xxcha focus its defense on fewer planets. [C09, C18, 00:32:50–00:35:17; 01:32:49–01:39:26; 01:13:03–01:17:32]

The agent Ggrocuto Rinn can ready a planet. The hosts say the opposing-player infantry-removal clause is rarely worth choosing over refreshing one of your own planets. Use the agent primarily as an extra refresh, especially in combination with the hero’s spending window. Peace Accords is a separate faction ability: resolving Diplomacy’s primary or secondary can grant an adjacent unoccupied planet. Do not conflate the agent’s ready effect with gaining control through Peace Accords. [C07, C08, C16, 00:13:51–00:15:44]

Xxcha’s commander Elder Qanoj adds one vote for each planet exhausted to vote and cannot be prevented by game effects from voting, according to the episode. The speakers expect the unlock to follow naturally if Xxcha expands into enough influence, but it requires planets totaling 12 printed influence; it is not a reason to count the hero’s combined spend value. Along with the hero, the commander can create a striking agenda-phase vote total. [C03–C04, 00:16:00–00:17:18; 00:18:50–00:19:26]

Quash lets Xxcha spend a strategy-pool command token when an agenda is revealed to discard it and reveal another. The guide says it has returned to a situational veto rather than the old hero’s centerpiece. Tokens are valuable before the late game, and the revised hero’s increased spending does not immediately create a large token pool. Save Quash for an agenda harmful enough to justify the token; the hosts mention occasional “fishing,” including looking for a point-granting agenda, but caution against repeatedly replacing harmless agendas or spending into a worse outcome. Political Favor duplicates Quash and is difficult to sell when Xxcha can wait to use its own ability, so the speakers usually keep the note unless offered an unusually good deal. [C19–C20, 00:08:05–00:09:21; 01:19:42–01:24:17; 01:25:03–01:26:34]

The guide argues that Xxcha’s agenda leverage makes its Alliance less attractive to give away than in the pre-Codex-3 version: the old shared-vote bargain is displaced by a much larger solo voting position. Treat this as dated table advice about the cited hero change, not a current diplomacy rule or categorical market value. The table can respond by coordinating its votes, while Xxcha may try to divide that coordination; both are political tactics discussed in the episode. [C21, 01:19:59–01:22:07; 01:24:06–01:24:49]

## Technology and military plans

The hosts favor a red-leaning War Sun route as a way to turn Xxcha’s later money into a meaningful fleet despite poor production, limited movement and a slow start. They identify AI Development Algorithm as a flexible entry, then consider Plasma Scoring or Self-Assembly Routines depending on immediate value and whether the mechs or PDS matter. A red skip can make the route faster. War Suns provide movement, bombardment, and a way to spend a large late-game economy in one build; the guide emphasizes their value for resisting or making a win-slay and buying time for another scoring window. This is an ambitious mid/late-game plan, not a claim that Xxcha can afford or should rush it in every map. [C22–C24, 00:51:48–01:00:03]

Cruiser II is a useful bridge when Xxcha needs movement or a timely point-denial attack while waiting for its larger plan. The guide suggests using it to disrupt an opponent’s objective tempo in rounds three through five, not only as a final-round defense. PDS II is another conditional route: paired with Xxcha’s flagship and other PDS, it can make a system unattractive to attack and support a defensive or win-slay posture, particularly if Construction or a structure objective makes the investment efficient. In the source’s ordering Cruiser II is more broadly useful; neither upgrade is presented as mandatory. [C25–C26, 00:56:36–00:59:13; 01:18:27–01:19:13]

Blue technology is a fallback when skips, movement needs or production make it attractive. The hosts say Gravity Drive and conventional carrier/dreadnought development do less to solve Xxcha’s cash-to-production mismatch and may leave it short of late-game force. Space Dock II is a luxury that can improve production and support a blue plan, though the speakers prefer red in ordinary conditions. Scanlink Drone Network can help find attachments, especially early, but they do not recommend detouring for it automatically. [C27–C28, 01:01:02–01:06:03; 01:10:39–01:17:56]

The two faction technologies, Instinct Training and Nullification Field, are described as improved in relative value because the revised hero can eventually make command tokens easier to afford. They still require investment, exhaust when used, and are once-per-ready-cycle tools; the hosts do not prioritize them over the main economic or military route. Instinct Training may suit an action-card-heavy late game. Nullification Field can matter if Xxcha wants to send ships away and interrupt an activation, but the source notes that opponents’ abilities or timing can bypass it. A useful skip and a clear tactical need make either more plausible. These are source judgments, not current rules verification. [C17, 00:23:59–00:27:26; 01:05:56–01:08:42]

## Opponents, counters and closeout

Expect other players to resist helping Xxcha in rounds one and two once they understand the hero’s payoff. Build enough plastic to avoid becoming an easy target; Diplomacy’s self-sufficient value and a credible PDS II/flagship deterrent can help. The speakers describe Xxcha as temporarily behind, then suddenly rich, which means an early point lead can attract heat before the faction has the force to close. They do not recommend assuming that opponents will coordinate trades or strategy-card timings to optimize Xxcha’s plan. [C26, C29, 00:59:59–01:00:55; 01:17:25–01:19:15]

As Xxcha, track point tempo continuously. Early scoring can unlock the hero but may consume secrets or expose an early lead. The guide sees stage-two objectives, economic spending objectives, and control objectives supported by a substantial fleet as possible routes. It also describes using a longer game as a recovery plan: build War Suns, disrupt leaders’ scoring tempo, negotiate a Support for the Throne swap where appropriate, and seek another opportunity to score. Mecatol Rex plus Imperial is a possible catch-up route, but the hosts worry it may require multiple picks and speaker control that Xxcha cannot count on. Decide by around round three whether the hoped-for round-five finish is realistic; if not, prepare to extend the game and coordinate a win-slay without assuming the table will do it for you. [C30–C31, 00:51:53–00:56:18; 01:27:10–01:30:18]

Opponents can withhold early help, watch for Xxcha’s sudden spending and vote surge, and coordinate to deny a decisive agenda or score. The podcast suggests blocking both agenda outcomes may be more realistic than excluding Xxcha from all decisions; five opponents may struggle to coordinate. Xxcha can exploit that disagreement. These are table tactics, not deterministic counters. [C21, C32, 01:22:07–01:24:17]
