# Naalu strategy summary

**Source:** SCPT 270, “Super Naalu Collective Guide” (2022-10-26), discussing Codex 3 / Prophecy of Kings era. This is a source-based draft, not a current-rules ruling.

Naalu’s central advantage is predictable scoring tempo. Telepathic places them first in initiative and allows them to score in the status phase before other players; the hosts treat round five as a powerful baseline. That advantage is most reliable when Naalu avoid looking like the immediate leader too early. Their recurring advice is to develop ships and money, stay close to the scoring pack, and make opponents spend attention on a different credible threat. Early Custodians, an aggressive Mecatol push, or a flashy action combo can turn a strong position into an exposed one. It is a posture, not a command to sit still; a real chance to finish can justify aggression. [C01–C02]

## Early game: cash, ships, safe movement

The guide describes Naalu as starting with one carrier, a cruiser, a destroyer, three fighters, four infantry and a PDS, plus Neural Motivator and Sarween Tools. The speakers say the single carrier makes round-one timing awkward, and the starting technologies do not provide a movement engine. Their first priority is therefore plastic and money, especially another carrier and a credible fighter count. Technology can wait if it would consume resources needed to establish a safe board. A wealthy start can change that calculation, but the baseline recommendation is to avoid building a clever technology path before the fleet is ready. [C03 s000174 12:51–12:59, s000186 14:02–14:06, s000729 48:18–48:23, s000743 48:59–49:01]

Z’eu is the key change to the opening in this Codex 3 discussion. The agent can choose a player to perform a tactical action in a non-home system without placing a command token, although that system still counts as activated. The speakers use it to extend the starting carrier, fill out the slice, and get around some of the old dependence on Warfare. Think through the route before using the ability: the chosen action may claim the planet that later makes a forward dock possible, and placing a dock can limit remaining movement. In the midgame, the same ability can activate a forward dock and build units for immediate use. [C04–C05]

The episode repeatedly discourages treating Mecatol Rex Custodians as Naalu’s automatic first opportunity. Taking the point in round two can put Naalu far ahead on the visible score track before their fleet can protect the rest of their position. Opponents may answer by threatening the new dock or exploiting an empty center of the slice. If another player takes Custodians, the resulting attention can even help Naalu by splitting the table’s concern. This is conditional: if several factions are already distributing early points, or the game presents an unusually safe path, Custodians may be appropriate. Price in the target the point creates. [C06–C07]

Round-one strategy-card advice is mixed rather than a fixed ranking. One speaker favors Diplomacy because Naalu need money and because it can make their first action productive; another notes Trade can pay well when accessible neighbors and the slice support it. Compare the actual Trade primary with Diplomacy’s refresh, and distinguish taking a card from paying to follow its secondary. Trade is particularly appealing at an early pick if another player may take Diplomacy, allowing Naalu to combine economies and fund production. The answer changes with map and timing. [C09–C10]

Construction is useful if no one else will provide a forward dock, but can be a poor choice when Naalu lack the money to exploit it. Warfare is debated for late picks: one speaker would take it to guarantee the second carrier, while the other says the agent makes Warfare less essential and prefers Construction in a no-Warfare table. The hosts disagree. Technology ranks below cash and ships in the ordinary opening; Antimass Deflectors or Dark Energy Tap are considered based on the slice, with Z’eu already solving part of the early movement problem. Politics is usually low priority unless it sets up a required next-round primary or a deliberate later Imperial plan. [C08, C11–C13]

## Fighters, technology, and combat

Hybrid Crystal Fighter II is the guide’s clearest technology priority. The speakers describe the faction fighter as hitting more reliably than a standard fighter, and its upgrade as improving it further while gaining independent movement and a reduced fleet-pool burden for fighters beyond capacity. That combination makes a large fighter force both an efficient defense and a way to collect scattered units into a late attack. The “better than War Suns” comparison is podcast rhetoric; the actionable claim is that Naalu can solve many fleet problems through a substantial fighter core. A strong home fighter screen may deter an attack, but does not remove the need to protect planets or account for specialized counters. [C14–C15]

The suggested core technology route is modest: take Antimass or Dark Energy Tap as appropriate, then work toward Hybrid Crystal Fighter II. Gravity Drive is valuable later when the Matriarch needs reach, but the speakers do not regard it as urgent for filling the starting slice because Z’eu can cover early movement. Fleet Logistics, Light/Wave Deflector, and Carrier II are possible additions rather than mandatory purchases. Fleet Logistics especially tempts players into taking Mecatol, popping Imperial, or building and moving in quick succession. Reserve it for a decisive second action: acting first can leave opponents the whole round to respond. [C17–C18]

Neuroglaive is not treated as a reason to force a difficult triple-green path. Opponents can plan for its fleet-pool tax, particularly if they see the technology coming, and the episode argues that a large upgraded fighter force is often a stronger deterrent than the tax itself. There is some potential value in early rounds before other players build flexible token economies, so the advice is not that the technology is useless in every game. Its benefit must justify the opportunity cost against blue technologies and the faction’s fighter plan. [C16 s000394 28:06–28:08, s000415 29:14–29:15, s000439 30:53–30:58, s000465 32:28–32:30]

The Matriarch complements this air-centered strategy. The hosts describe its capacity and ability to commit fighters as ground combatants during an invasion. Their tactical reminder is to bring an actual ground force to initiate ground combat, then use fighters to add combat strength. Because those fighters return to space after the invasion, plan how to hold the planet afterward. A mech can also support the fleet: the guide praises the Iconoclast Omega’s described protection from anti-fighter barrage, particularly against Argent or a Destroyer II plan, and notes a mech can serve as the lone ground invader alongside the Matriarch. These claims reflect the episode’s Codex/Omega-era component discussion; check the intended edition. [C21–C22]

## Abilities, trade, and table position

Foresight is a niche defensive tool, not a core plan. After an opponent moves into a system containing Naalu ships, it can spend a strategy-pool token to retreat those ships into an eligible adjacent system before combat. That can preserve a fleet or deny a secret objective, but the token has an opportunity cost: Naalu may need it to follow Leadership or another useful secondary. Use Foresight when avoiding the battle matters more than the token and an ordinary retreat will not do. [C20 s000210 15:58–16:02, s000225 16:59–17:03, s000233 17:38–17:40, s000245 18:04–18:07]

The hosts describe M’aban’s described Omega unlock requires ground forces in or adjacent to Mecatol Rex. The hosts say Z’eu plus ground forces on a nearby planet can make the unlock possible in round one. Its information ability lets Naalu inspect a neighbor’s promissory notes and the top and bottom agenda cards. Agenda information is the more useful part in their view, but they emphasize waiting until another agenda ability has completed before checking again. Some discussion concerns the Tabletop Playground interface and does not generalize to every environment. [C23–C24]

The Oracle hero is considered weak on average. Because players must give Naalu a promissory note from hand, the chance of a useful result is higher early, when others may hold political secrets or trade agreements that still have time to matter. The hosts discourage elaborate note-trading schemes designed just to improve the hero’s payoff. Use it promptly when available, but do not build the game around receiving a particular note. [C25 s000607 41:42–41:48, s000618 42:07–42:11, s000639 43:02–43:07, s000646 43:34–43:39, s000664 44:29–44:32]

Keep Gift of Prescience in the older and agent-era discussion presented here. The note gives its holder first initiative for the round but prevents Naalu from using Telepathic that round. Earlier guides had entertained selling it to improve Naalu’s weak opening; the hosts say Z’eu has made that Custodians gambit less necessary and that giving away the core tempo ability rarely pays. Their mention of when the note returns is explicitly framed in base-game wording, so treat edition timing carefully. [C26 s000306 21:51–21:56, s000310 22:14–22:17, s000313 22:29–22:35, s000319 23:04–23:10, s000536 36:28–36:34]

## Scoring pace and win-slay defense

Naalu’s challenge is managing the threat their predictable tempo creates. Do not assume that a round-five status-phase score means no one can win first. Another faction may close in the action phase, or an objective reveal may change the race. The hosts describe a loss caused by waiting for the expected status-phase advantage while a rival ran to an earlier finish. Conversely, if Naalu are threatened, their agent, Matriarch and fighter fleet can contribute to a late win-slay. Consider holding Z’eu for that intervention in rounds four or five rather than always using it for the first build. [C27–C28]

Make the win order difficult for the table to read without creating an uncontrolled leader. Helping another player’s economy or accepting their Custodians point can spread attention, but stop if that player becomes the immediate threat. Preserve ships, money, and timing options for the right scoring opportunity. [C07, C27–C28]

## Component connections

The supported interactions are causal links discussed in the episode, not mere co-occurrence: Z’eu enables token-free movement and forward-dock production; Hybrid Crystal Fighter II complements the Matriarch’s capacity and invasion ability; the Iconoclast is described as countering anti-fighter barrage; Gravity Drive supports the Matriarch’s late strike; Gift of Prescience conflicts with Telepathic for the round. The JSON connection list carries source citations. [N01–N06]
