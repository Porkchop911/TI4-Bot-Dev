# Nomad strategy summary — SCPT 309

**Draft thesis.** Nomad is a flexible, money-rich faction whose strong start, sellable pieces, and unusual flagship movement let it solve early tempo and then contest speaker control or block a rival’s score. The guide repeatedly warns against treating Memoria II or Oxil Siven’s “hero parade” as a guaranteed game plan: Nomad can travel widely, but the faction has few special tools for winning ground combat, and economy must be converted into board presence. These are episode opinions, not current-rules verification; the guide was published in July 2023 and its compatibility with later rules or expansions is unreviewed.

## Opening and round-one priorities

- **C01 — Use the strong start to avoid a forced opening.** The speakers describe a blue starting technology, a four-resource/four-influence home system, a carrier and ground forces, Sling Relay, and a starting flagship as a flexible launch. This is a general baseline, not proof that every map permits the same first-round line. (13:53.26–15:09.14; segments: `s000203`–`s000227`)
- **C02 — Treat Trade as the preferred pick when available.** Four commodities, sellable Cavalry and commander access, plus Artuno’s ability to bank supply trade goods make Trade an unusually productive Nomad pick. The speakers prefer it in round one and later rounds, but their ideal of taking it repeatedly assumes the speaker order allows it. (45:19.58–46:11.42; segments: `s000625`–`s000632`)
- **C03 — Politics is a top-tier opening because speaker control can be sold.** Politics can seek Custodians or retain an early pick; selling speaker position to a neighbor can pay, especially beside Trade. Ideally, sell the speaker token while keeping first pick, if order permits. (43:56.22–45:00.34; segments: `s000594`–`s000620`)
- **C04 — Diplo can fund plastic when a valuable planet is available.** The speakers favor refreshing a resource-rich planet after spending home resources on Technology; they prefer money for ships and a possible command token over Leadership’s command-token value in their usual Nomad games. A poor slice or objective flip can change this comparison. (42:22.62–43:53.52; 41:55.18–42:40.58; segments: `s000570`–`s000592`)
- **C05 — Construction is usually a low-priority opening.** One speaker says that if Diplo is attractive, Construction is generally not; the objective flip and map can still make a dock useful. The guide gives no universal prohibition. (45:00.82–45:18.50; segments: `s000621`–`s000624`)
- **C06 — Usually pass on Warfare in round one.** Nomad’s carrier-plus-flagship transport capacity and starting infantry and Sling Relay reduce the need for Warfare’s primary. Its secondary can replace ground forces; take Warfare for a specific Custodians line or tempo need. (48:10.58–49:53.42; segments: `s000663`–`s000683`)
- **C07 — Technology is useful but not an automatic first pick.** Gravity Drive is a sensible target, but Trade and Politics usually rank higher, and Diplo can be better for resources. Prioritize Technology when movement, timing, or a useful double-tech line demands it; Sling Relay supplies a stall. (49:53.42–51:44.18; segments: `s000684`–`s000707`)
- **C08 — A demanding round-one Mecatol line exists, but is not the default.** The example uses a carrier to take Hope’s End, a mech placed at home, Sling Relay for another carrier, Gravity Drive, moving the new carrier with that mech next to Mecatol, and the flagship’s mech adjacency to reach Mecatol. It requires the right money, technologies, planets, and sequence; the speakers present it as an illustrative maneuver, not a routine promise. (46:11.62–47:23.62; segments: `s000633`–`s000649`)

## Economy, agents, and trade

- **C09 — Remember Future Sight’s delayed income.** After an outcome Nomad voted for or predicted resolves, Future Sight gains one trade good from the supply. It is easy to forget; an agenda-phase gain arrives after status and cannot fund the action phase just completed. (15:40.52–16:49.04; 24:08.36–25:03.78; segments: `s000240`–`s000250`, `s000350`–`s000361`)
- **C10 — Artuno doubles only trade goods gained from the supply, with a timing cost.** Artuno banks an equal amount until it readies. Trade’s primary and Future Sight qualify as supply sources; goods already on a strategy card do not. Bank only if delayed money will help. (23:33.18–25:39.94; segments: `s000342`–`s000371`)
- **C11 — Combine Artuno and Scuttle only when the costly setup is worthwhile.** The guide cites Scuttle on Memoria plus a Dreadnought as a 24-trade-good ceiling with Artuno. It is an extreme, conditional interaction, not a baseline plan; the guide does not recommend chasing Scuttle or the setup by itself. (25:40.66–26:05.98; segments: `s000372`–`s000378`)
- **C12 — Sell the commander after an early secret if the unlock is available.** Nomad’s commander unlocks on scoring a secret objective. The speaker says they choose whichever of their first two secrets can be scored in round one or two, even if it is an action-phase secret, because unlocking and selling the commander can repay the early point opportunity cost. This is a personal strategy preference, not a scoring rule change. (26:06.28–28:15.08; segments: `s000379`–`s000403`)
- **C13 — Free production does not make the flagship valueless.** With the commander unlocked, Nomad can produce a flagship without spending resources, but the speakers emphasize that it remains an eight-resource unit for effects that check unit value, including the discussed capture and Scuttle interactions. (28:15.08–29:02.92; segments: `s000404`–`s000411`)
- **C14 — Offer Cavalry for value and expect to recover it after combat.** The note improves one non-fighter ship to the Nomad flagship’s combat, sustain, and anti-fighter-barrage values in a combat against someone other than Nomad. The guide favors selling it, often to make an already favorable fight safer and less costly, then selling it again when returned. Buyers may refuse; it does not reliably turn a losing fight into a winning one. (39:20.96–40:58.82; segments: `s000542`–`s000562`)
- **C15 — Keep the three agents’ different jobs distinct.** Thundarian can reroll the standard combat roll-dice step once, but not Anti-Fighter Barrage, bombardment, or space cannon; Mercer moves up to two ground forces from the board onto planets in the active system at turn end; Artuno banks supply trade goods. Its window is narrow, and it may be saleable during a critical combat. (19:41.62–21:57.84; 22:02.14–23:59.82; segments: `s000290`–`s000317`, `s000319`–`s000347`)
- **C16 — Use Mercer for forward reinforcement, often a mech bridge.** The speakers describe moving a freshly built home mech to a forward planet to establish a future flagship route or reinforce a position. They do not cast Nomad as a faction that should routinely seize and hold Mecatol with this agent. (22:02.14–23:28.30; segments: `s000319`–`s000339`)

## Flagship, combat, and mobility

- **C17 — Mechs make the flagship’s movement hard to predict.** Memoria may treat itself as adjacent to systems containing Nomad mechs. Mercer can place a new forward mech, creating routes that opponents may not anticipate. This aids reach and point-blocking; mechs are not independent flying units, and carried forces still need capacity. (17:00.72–17:57.34; 22:02.14–23:28.30; segments: `s000253`–`s000264`, `s000319`–`s000339`)
- **C18 — A Nomad mech can soak a space hit, but that may weaken the invasion.** Its sustain can cancel a hit against Nomad ships while the mech is in the space area. Because a damaged mech remains damaged if later used on the ground, the guide usually weighs this offensive soak against preserving the mech for ground combat; it does sometimes use the ability early. (18:00.74–19:32.54; segments: `s000266`–`s000287`)
- **C19 — Do not assume Memoria II is the best use of early technology.** The upgrade improves combat, movement, anti-fighter barrage, and capacity, and the commander can make a replacement free to produce. But the speakers question whether its benefits repay the prerequisite and opportunity cost compared with Carrier II, Dreadnought II, or other technologies. They explicitly say the hero parade’s frequency and value were overstated in earlier discussion. (36:37.26–38:16.32; segments: `s000505`–`s000528`)
- **C20 — A no-skip path can prioritize Gravity Drive and ground-force capacity.** The proposed baseline is Gravity Drive, then Carrier II to bring enough ground forces, with Scanlink and Dreadnought II as later useful options; additional blue technologies can add flexibility and tempo. This is a proposed path, not a fixed sequence for every objective or board. (64:46.10–65:52.06; segments: `s000869`–`s000881`)
- **C21 — Let skips and objectives decide whether to pursue Memoria II.** A green or yellow skip can make the upgrade fit more efficiently; a yellow skip can also support a later path into it. If no skip is available, speakers prefer a standard path over spending another technology solely to force Memoria II. Evaluate after the stage-two objective when possible, since an objective suited to the hero parade changes its value. (65:39.28–66:57.86; 69:24.06–69:56.42; 62:09.98–62:53.52; segments: `s000834`–`s000840`, `s000879`–`s000898`, `s000940`–`s000945`)
- **C22 — Neural Motivator and Bio-Stims are situational tools, not universal must-haves.** The guide likes Neural Motivator because action cards add flexibility and can interact with Artuno when they award supply trade goods; Bio-Stims can ready Sling Relay for a second use or refresh a valuable planet. Choose them when card flow, planet value, or tempo justifies the slot. (67:11.20–68:27.60; segments: `s000904`–`s000920`)
- **C23 — Sarween is a poor fit for the described Sling Relay plan.** The speakers point out that Sarween does not reduce Sling Relay’s ship-production cost, so it does not support the specific production interaction they value. This is a narrow assessment of that path, not a full technology ranking. (68:08.14–68:20.70; segments: `s000916`–`s000918`)
- **C24 — Use Fleet Logistics to recover tempo when the game calls for it.** The guide values the option to take Imperial and score promptly, or to chain two activations during a hero-parade round. It is most attractive after missing Custodians or when catching up; if possible, wait for the stage-two objective before committing. (65:55.72–66:27.58; 71:15.46–71:41.74; 68:44.18–68:52.56; segments: `s000883`–`s000890`, `s000926`–`s000928`, `s000968`–`s000974`)
- **C25 — Oxil Siven’s parade is an objective tool, not a universal win button.** The hero allows the flagship and transported units to leave systems containing Nomad tokens for that game round; it does not let Nomad activate a system twice. It shines on a late, far-reaching space-control attempt, with mechs positioned to extend routes and few opponents left to respond. (29:11.62–29:55.72; 61:03.12–61:21.00; 79:37.20–80:54.84; segments: `s000413`–`s000420`, `s000814`–`s000818`, `s001120`–`s001135`)
- **C26 — Start a parade before every opponent has passed when the route is still hard to read.** Begin mid-round if scattered mechs keep the route unclear; save decisive moves until opponents cannot disrupt the line. An unlocked commander can also make the flagship expendable for a local objective if it can be rebuilt and the parade continues elsewhere. (80:55.22–81:57.52; segments: `s001136`–`s001150`)

## Scoring, counters, and endgame

- **C27 — Solve tempo first, then protect speaker control.** Custodians in round one or two is the preferred tempo solution when the line is realistic; otherwise the guide mentions another tempo advantage (the ASR-rendered "shard or Obsidian" is uncertain). Then use deals, action cards, and speaker order to keep the game pointed toward Nomad. (75:23.30–75:54.24; 77:58.28–78:52.84; segments: `s001034`–`s001038`, `s001091`–`s001103`)
- **C28 — Convert wealth into pieces across the map.** Trade, Future Sight, and Artuno can build a strong income floor, but cash at home is not enough. Spend early and spread plastic where objectives, defense, or movement routes may matter. (52:15.84–54:14.90; 78:52.88–79:32.32; 23:33.18–25:39.94; 45:19.58–46:11.42; segments: `s000342`–`s000371`, `s000625`–`s000632`, `s000715`–`s000736`, `s001104`–`s001116`)
- **C29 — Lean into point-blocking more than direct win-slaying.** The speakers describe Nomad as able to redirect tokens and threaten unexpected routes that can block a rival’s win, but say the faction is not reliably built to eliminate a leader by force from behind. This matters only when the board and token economy make a block consequential. (54:22.94–55:47.34; 78:23.94–78:52.84; segments: `s000737`–`s000753`, `s001098`–`s001103`)
- **C30 — Ground combat is the major weakness to plan around.** Most Nomad combat tools described here affect space combat; the mech’s sustain is not a ground-combat bonus, and the hero does not supply a second activation. Use carrier capacity and numbers, and consider bombardment where a difficult planet such as Mecatol is central. Without early tempo, a faction already entrenched there may be hard to dislodge. (59:21.26–60:14.46; 61:03.12–61:24.42; 65:14.38–65:52.06; segments: `s000798`–`s000805`, `s000814`–`s000820`, `s000875`–`s000881`)
- **C31 — Reserve the most dramatic movement for objectives that reward it.** Spread-out Stage II space-control objectives are the guide’s clearest parade targets; a flagship on Mecatol can support a supremacy objective only if the ground defense can be cracked. Fighter II is suggested as a way to populate distant space late in the round, while Bio-Stims can supply extra stalls. A plan to move widely is not itself scoring: check the exact objective and every prerequisite before claiming the point. (79:37.20–80:54.84; segments: `s001120`–`s001135`)
- **C32 — Prepare the midgame so the final round has pieces, routes, and allies.** Build early, make useful deals, and use speaker control and movement to close; the guide says early momentum is more reliable than a late comeback. Commander access and Cavalry can support paid cooperation when opponents agree. (78:52.88–79:32.32; 73:01.42–73:31.54; 39:22.68–40:16.80; segments: `s000543`–`s000553`, `s000994`–`s001002`, `s001104`–`s001116`)
