# Crimson Rebellion strategy summary draft

Crimson’s game is built around unusual movement, not a conventional early technology race. Its opening economy is thin, but the protected home and breach network let it move out aggressively; the commander can later turn combats into substantial income. The central tradeoff is that the same alliance and public breach network that fuel Crimson can help rivals. This is SCPT 430 beginner-guide advice, not current-rules verification.

## Breaches, movement and combat

**C05 — The hosts report a playtester-informed ruling that Crimson cannot move through non-epsilon wormholes, while other wormhole effects remain usable.** This restriction concerns movement through wormholes in the reported interpretation. Apply when: Episode-time interpretation may be reversed by a stricter reading; two games used this ruling. [21:10–23:40; s000260–s000286]

**C06 — Exploit the protected, off-board home system to send units forward rather than maintain a home garrison.** The protected home system lets Crimson use starting infantry elsewhere rather than leave a defensive stack behind. Apply when: This rests on Sundered and its stated scope and exceptions. [23:40–25:02; s000287–s000317]

**C07 — Keep most breaches inactive near valuable destinations: activate them when ready, since active breaches create shared adjacency and expose your slice.** Inactive breaches are private route options. Apply when: Maintain active routes when movement warrants it; other players can use and remove active breaches. [26:13–27:50; s000342–s000364]

**C08 — Remove your own active breaches when their public access is no longer useful; inactive breaches are easier to move when the component limit matters.** Breach supply management is not just a bookkeeping issue: active tokens are harder to reclaim. Apply when: Activating a breach is harder to undo than leaving it inactive. [28:44–30:28; s000374–s000394]

**C09 — Send starting destroyers toward empty systems and combats: Exile places an inactive breach after combat in its system or an adjacent one, while Dark Energy Tap can add exploration value.** The destroyers perform several jobs early: explore, threaten small combats, and place breaches. Apply when: A destroyer destroyed in its own combat cannot place a breach afterward. [30:28–31:30; s000395–s000406; 32:22–32:48; s000423–s000427]

**C10 — Exile II matters when two-system reach opens a difficult destination or win-slay route; choose inactive when placing directly on the destination.** The upgrade is less valuable if all useful destinations are already reachable. Apply when: The speakers disagree about active placement and have not personally tested the win-slay case; do not rush it when range will not matter. [31:48–34:08; s000412–s000449; 34:50–35:35; s000458–s000466]

**C12 — Resonance Generator can replace Gravity Drive on many routes: ships starting at home or an active breach gain movement, and its action can flip or place an active breach where Crimson has units.** Because Resonance Generator grants movement independently of its exhaust action, timing the action does not switch off the movement paragraph. Apply when: Sorrow route access makes DET and Sling Relay plausible alternatives; active routes also expose systems. [38:57–39:59; s000505–s000516; 40:30–41:24; s000517–s000528]

**C13 — Prioritize Carrier II or Fighter II when pursuing AI Development Algorithm and a fighter-heavy hero fleet; Crimson needs punch and capacity more than extra reach.** The episode repeatedly describes Crimson as needing more punch. Apply when: Research competes with early plastic and economic tempo. [41:24–42:00; s000529–s000534]

**C14 — Stage Quietus where an active breach and likely battle matter—often Sorrow or Mecatol—so opposing unit abilities are suppressed at the fight.** Quietus must be in a system with an active breach; its suppression applies to other players’ units in every system with an active breach, not just its own system. **C15 — Pair Quietus with fighters and enough carrier capacity: suppressing sustain damage and fighter barrage can swing combat math, and Carrier II can retain fighters afterward.** The suppression affects combat abilities that shape attrition, including sustain damage and fighter barrage. **C16 — Keep a Revenant or two in reserve for tactical surprise control: during your tactical action, an active breach lets you commit a mech without your units already there.** The mech’s surprise value is tied to your own tactical activation and an active breach. ## Scoring and game horizon

**C11 — Subatomic Splicer is optional and can be slow in short action-phase games; its home-system limit reduces forward projection.** The effect replaces a destroyed ship at the home dock, so it does not directly solve forward production. Apply when: It may matter more in longer games; blockade and production wording are unresolved. [35:35–38:48; s000467–s000501]

**C25 — Do not force early technology objectives: Crimson can delay research and favor plastic while staying near common two-color and unit-upgrade thresholds for later flexibility.** The speakers do not see a faction technology as a mandatory early fix. Apply when: Late technology objectives may be manageable after income arrives. [129:18–131:11; s001696–s001720]

**C26 — Prefer difficult control objectives when possible: breaches and reserve mechs can reach unusual systems, while commander income also enriches a rival who may score alongside you.** Breach mobility and remote mechs make some control tasks more feasible, but each objective’s exact requirement still governs scoring. Apply when: Control scoring still requires the objective’s systems and prerequisites; economic upside cuts both ways. [126:11–128:13; s001658–s001679; 128:56–130:11; s001689–s001707]

**C27 — Use Sorrow as a safe staging point for a prepared flagship or route, supporting a Mecatol/Imperial push or later Fracture and Styx transition.** The hosts describe a path from Mecatol toward Styx and the Fracture, but they admit they lacked enough strength to complete it in some games. Apply when: The hosts describe the Styx sequence as a plan and admit lacking the muscle; setup is not scoring. [104:11–105:41; s001348–s001372]

**C28 — Treat Crimson as a weak-start, strong-ramp contender rather than an automatic leader: keep fleets resilient and account for opponents targeting your economic partner.** The episode calls Crimson strong and fun while emphasizing its vulnerable start and uncertain close. Apply when: Rating is provisional; research games kept a win path but did not close. Map, player count, scoring pace and table response matter. [135:24–136:41; s001789–s001802]
