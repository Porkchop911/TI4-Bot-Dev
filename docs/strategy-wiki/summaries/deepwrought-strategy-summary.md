# Deepwrought Scholarate strategy summary draft

The guide presents Deepwrought as a technology broker that can turn shared research into an early economic lead. Its opening is unusually flexible: choose reach, breakthrough access, or a Mecatol race based on the slice and strategy-card order. Coexistence then creates deals, objective options, and ocean-card income. The speakers also warn that broad generosity can distract from the win condition, and that the faction's late catch-up tools look weaker than its ability to build a lead. These are podcast recommendations, not current-rules verification.

## Opening and economy

**C01 — Let the slice choose your opening technologies.** Gravity Drive is attractive when important planets or equidistants are hard to reach. Bio-Stims offers a different line: refresh a planet with a tech specialty to obtain the breakthrough cheaply. The hosts generally favor blue or green and see less default value in red or yellow, while stressing that the nearby skips, planet access, and overall route decide the choice. A Mahact race can make the specialty-dependent plan less reliable. [24:23–25:35; s000336–s000354; 26:16–26:47; s000359–s000366]

**C02 — Double blue can support round-one Custodians.** Gravity Drive and Carrier II access can help reach Mecatol Rex; the four-influence home system plus another planet may supply the six influence. Leadership or follows of Diplomacy, Technology, or Warfare can complete the timing. Treat this as an assembled plan, not a faction guarantee: the slice and opponents decide whether the pieces line up. The speakers say the attempt is possible but not easy or certain. [25:40–27:08; s000356–s000371]

**C03 — Sling Relay is a timing-focused alternative.** With useful empty systems, Dark Energy Tap and Sling Relay can give you stalls around Technology. One proposed sequence waits for the breakthrough, then negotiates for Gravity Drive. That can shape when research happens and offer another player a valuable follow, but the other player must cooperate. The episode calls it a viable alternative, not the universal best start. [27:14–28:34; s000376–s000396]

**C04 — Coexistence can defend and bargain.** Research Team can make an attack on the home system more complicated and let two players share an objective that explicitly recognizes their presence. The hosts also describe using it to share an edge-system objective. Check the objective text and current rulings: the podcast says coexistence has had many interactions debated and changed, so shared presence does not count for everything. [30:27–31:44; s000418–s000434; 31:53–32:52; s000436–s000443]

**C05 — Place oceans for future value, and assess the other player's incentives.** The hosts suggest edge planets near a neighbor's home, tech skips, anomalies, or planet traits that could help a later control objective. Placement may also connect trade with the target or their neighbor. Coexistence is not always safe: an anecdote describes Stellar Converter destroying a shared planet to block a secret objective. Consider what the other player wants and can do before treating an offer as harmless. [32:13–33:44; s000441–s000457; 66:08–67:21; s000934–s000950]

**C06 — Ocean cards add flexible spending capacity.** The episode describes five ready cards, each worth one resource and one influence, gained as coexistence begins. The hosts value the reserve and note that losing cards when units leave planets, then establishing new coexistences, can sometimes refresh cards for another spend. They call this sequencing opportunity uncommon. The full five-card value takes time to establish; track the cards actually in play. [33:52–35:23; s000461–s000478; 35:31–36:23; s000480–s000492]

**C13 — Hydrothermal Mining scales with coexistence.** It gains one trade good per ocean card at status, making it more attractive after several cards are active. The speakers frame it as a possible round-two technology, not an automatic opener: early ocean count may be low and ordinary research often needs unit upgrades first. A tech specialty can make the faction technologies an appealing package, but immediate fleet and movement needs still compete for research. [52:04–53:38; s000720–s000742]

## Mobility, builds, and technology deals

**C07 — The Luminous is a prepared late-game reach piece.** Its movement can chain through systems containing Deepwrought units, and its six capacity gives the route a substantial payload. A network of coexistence positions can create long moves, but the speakers call this setup-dependent and late-game. The movement does not appear without prior positioning. [38:10–39:47; s000520–s000543]

**C08 — Stage a fleet through coexistence routes.** Wormholes and other map linchpins can extend the flagship's path. The hosts describe staging ships, then sending the Luminous through when the target is ready; they call it a playmaker and report cases where they preferred building it to a War Sun. That is anecdotal rather than a universal purchase rule: it is strongest when the route and useful payload already exist. [40:00–41:36; s000547–s000576]

**C09 — Eanautic enables useful forward production.** The mech's Production 1 is highlighted as a way to build near the front. One example uses Integrated Economy to produce ships after placing the mech; Self-Assembly Routines can add value when repeated mech builds are affordable. The playtester recalls accumulating mechs this way, but it is a game anecdote. Compare the sequence with the ships and infantry your actual plan needs. [41:50–43:17; s000579–s000591; 43:11–43:42; s000593–s000599]

**C10 — Sell Share Knowledge for an immediate problem.** The note temporarily lends a non-unit-upgrade technology. The hosts suggest helping a buyer meet a technology objective, lending Light/Wave Deflectors for a win-slay, or giving Mahact a technology it may purge toward a relic. Because the faction has many other deals, sell it when the buyer has an immediate reason to pay. [43:55–44:49; s000603–s000610; 45:32–46:45; s000614–s000629]

**C11 — Share Knowledge can sell timing.** A player waiting for Technology may value a stall, Sling Relay, or access to Gravity Drive or Fleet Logistics. Offer it when that timing helps the buyer and the deal returns value to Deepwrought. [47:04–48:43; s000660–s000681]

**C12 — Radical Advancement is flexible, but not an automatic pick.** At status, it replaces a non-unit-upgrade tech with one of the same color and exactly one more prerequisite. One host doubts the value of exchanging technology; the other points out that an early copy can turn into a stronger option or blunt opponents withholding a desirable technology. Its appeal may increase where the breakthrough is less cooperative. It competes with Bio-Stims as a starting green tech, and the speakers do not call it mandatory. [49:05–50:38; s000686–s000700; 50:38–51:56; s000701–s000718]

**C20 — Ta Zern can deny a leader.** Purging a technology removes it for everyone, so pick a target that hurts a rival more than yourself. Players who purge an owned copy then research another technology; denial does not simply lower their technology count. Examples include Antimass when anomaly access matters, X-89 against a ground-combat threat, Gravity Drive when ahead and slowing pursuit, or Fleet Logistics when it mainly helps the likely winner. [72:50–74:29; s001023–s001046; 74:30–75:35; s001047–s001057]

**C21 — Use the hero to fill a fleet gap when denial is not worthwhile.** It can research a missing unit upgrade: the episode describes using Cruiser II with Sling Relay and Bio-Stims to build a fleet that could reach Mecatol late. Destroyer II and Plasma Scoring are other situational examples. [75:35–76:39; s001058–s001088]

**C14 — Visionaria Select is the central table-wide research engine.** Each other player may spend three trade goods and provide a note to research an eligible technology; Deepwrought gains it too. The hosts describe subsidizing a technology they want or returning notes to encourage participation. They also say order matters: the first key technology can be more valuable than later ones. Coordinate price and timing rather than paying everyone without a reason. [55:00–56:40; s000770–s000809; 56:53–58:28; s000798–s000815; 60:12–61:24; s000839–s000856]

**C15 — Keep the offer attractive without giving away your lead.** The playtester describes selective subsidy, while the hosts describe generous participation and returning notes. They expect wealthy factions such as Hacan or Nomad to buy in, with others joining to avoid falling behind. Their view is that Deepwrought can still come out ahead, but they advise negotiating instead of paying for nothing. The right posture depends on the technologies, player order, economy, and opponents' ability to exploit them. [58:28–60:04; s000816–s000838]

**C16 — Resolve research in an order that serves unit upgrades.** When following Technology, consider using the breakthrough first if it supplies prerequisites for Carrier II or another needed upgrade. That can also unlock the commander before the table researches. Initiative affects who gets a subsidy and whether a key technology is still available. This is timing advice; groups may differ in how they handle the note, subsidy, and printed component text. [60:11–61:45; s000843–s000873; 62:55–63:25; s000885–s000892; 98:34–99:18; s001386–s001397]

**C17 — Explore cross-faction deals, but verify the component wording.** Jol-Nar's Research Agreement might help Hacan fund a shared technology that Deepwrought also receives. The hosts also suggest Nekro might trade three trade goods for three command tokens through the research interaction. These combinations need the named factions and willing partners; one speaker calls the Nekro idea newly noticed, so confirm the relevant text before building a plan around it. [61:32–62:37; s000860–s000880; 62:36–63:25; s000878–s000892]

**C18 — Doctor Carrina can start the ocean economy and a relationship.** The agent grants another player a prerequisite skip and establishes coexistence on a non-home planet they control. The target gets an immediate technology benefit; Deepwrought gains an ocean and may establish a trade partner or unlock the commander. Choose the player and placement with objectives and neighbors in mind. Its skip may become less valuable once the table has accumulated technology. [63:26–64:57; s000894–s000910; 65:53–67:21; s000933–s000968; 67:22–68:08; s000951–s000965]

**C19 — Aello turns other players' research into income.** With an ocean in play, the commander can lower another player's Technology cost and yield a commodity or trade good to Deepwrought. The hosts suggest circulating it broadly because multiple discounts may encourage use, and Deepwrought can benefit from other players' discounts when researching. They favor access over demanding a premium every time. The payoff depends on opponents actually researching and the other discounts in the game. [68:52–69:56; s000973–s000985; 70:37–71:27; s000990–s001017]

## Scoring and strategy-card choices

**C22 — Coexistence is not the same as control or scoring.** Sharing Mecatol does not give the Custodians point or enable Imperial's Mecatol bonus, according to the speakers. They name Occupy the Seat of the Empire as an objective that expressly permits scoring while coexisting. Read each objective: shared presence can prepare or satisfy some requirements, but does not confer control for every purpose. [78:44–80:42; s001118–s001124]

**C23 — Leadership and Diplomacy depend on the opening.** One host calls Leadership low priority, the other medium because it improves a round-one Custodians attempt. Diplomacy becomes more attractive when a later pick needs the breakthrough; if Bio-Stims already supplies access, that need may fall. A double-blue start can also use Diplomacy to ready home-system influence for the Mecatol line. [80:58–82:36; s001129–s001155; 82:43–83:50; s001161–s001170; 83:51–84:24; s001175–s001182]

**C24 — Politics preserves a round-two plan.** When round-one Custodians looks unlikely, the hosts like Politics for its options: prepare an early Imperial point, gain action cards for the breakthrough, or stay flexible if another player unexpectedly takes Mecatol. They prefer this to spending every extra resource on a race that may not happen. This is a strong preference, and predicting the table can be hard, particularly when a late player takes Diplomacy. [84:32–85:48; s001185–s001198; 85:49–86:59; s001199–s001218]

**C25 — Construction is conditional.** It is medium-to-low priority because home and mech production reduce the need for extra docks. It can still shore up home or meet a structure objective, while Sling Relay can make it redundant. [87:33–88:59; s001227–s001245; 89:00–89:56; s001246–s001262]

**C26 — Trade supports money, adjacency, and research deals.** The hosts consider it reliably useful: adjacency makes repeated technology exchanges practical, and Trade can fund table participation in the breakthrough. Distance still matters to ordinary transactions; non-binding exchanges depend on table practice. [89:57–91:25; s001263–s001285]

**C27 — Warfare solves a particular timing need.** The starting fleet can often take useful systems, and coexistence adds planets, so the hosts rank Warfare low. It rises when the map or a Custodians plan needs more early reach. The three starting infantry and slice still matter, and Sling Relay may supply a stall or build option. Check that a late Diplomacy follow is realistic before depending on Warfare for a race. [91:27–92:41; s001286–s001300; 92:41–93:16; s001301–s001310]

**C28 — Technology and Trade can be near substitutes in round one.** Technology gives timing control and access to unit upgrades; Trade can fund breakthrough participation and return comparable economic value. If you take Technology, the hosts suggest resolving the breakthrough first when it unlocks a commander or prerequisite, then coordinating agent and commodity exchanges. Player order matters because early money is more useful than surplus after the game is nearly decided. [94:04–95:18; s001323–s001344; 98:34–100:09; s001387–s001408]

**C29 — Do not let the broad menu crowd out unit upgrades.** The breakthrough does not supply unit upgrades, so normal research remains important. The hosts emphasize Carrier II and Dreadnought II; Fighter II can fit a Carrier II and Dock II fighter line. In their view, the mech and X-89 reduce pressure to rush Infantry II. Pick upgrades that answer actual reach, fleet, or objective needs rather than pursuing every technology. [101:42–103:18; s001431–s001478; 104:03–105:09; s001473–s001490]

## Counterplay and limits

**C30 — The hosts see a strong leader and a weaker catch-up finish.** Deepwrought can build and maintain a lead through economy and technology tempo, but the speakers find it less able to catch an opponent racing ahead. The flagship improves reach without guaranteeing a successful win-slay. A leader should expect opponents to learn coordinated attacks; the hosts stress that the faction and its counters are new, so their assessment may change with experience. [106:01–107:29; s001495–s001507; 107:29–108:23; s001508–s001527]
