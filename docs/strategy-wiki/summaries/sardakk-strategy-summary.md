# Sardakk N’orr strategy summary

**SCPT 379, “Super Sardakk N’orr Guide (THE FINAL SUPER GUIDE),” published 2024-11-27.** Draft synthesis of the complete verified ASR transcript. Compatibility with the current rules is unreviewed; speakers are not identified. Timestamps refer to audio positions in the publisher episode.

## Strategic thesis

Sardakk’s combat modifier is most useful when it secures planets, deters attacks, or helps win ground combat without a space fight. The guide warns combat wins do not automatically score points. Since Sardakk starts behind in technology and economy, its plan combines research, carriers, and selective force projection. The opening trades commander access against economic repair.


## Commander, invasion reach, and hero timing

**C01 — Prioritize unlocking G’hom Sek’kus early when Sardakk can reach five non-home planets without stretching its position.** The guide treats the commander as a transformative faction ability: it lets ground forces enter a target during the active system’s commit step from nearby planets, changing how Sardakk can contest objectives and threaten a neighbor. Plan toward the five-planet unlock, but do not force a bad expansion merely to unlock it. _Conditions:_ Nearby planets must be accessible without overcommitting ships; the unlock requires five planets outside the home system. [SCPT 379 29:06–30:31; s000477–s000491]

**C02 — Use adjacent-system commitment to attack planets without sending ships into the target system, while respecting its timing and route restrictions.** The guide says this can bypass a space battle Sardakk might lose, letting it contest a planet while its fleet remains in place. The reach itself can surprise opponents. In discussing the semi-official Dane FAQ, the hosts say Ceasefire affects this “move” and flag restrictions for anomalies and wormholes under the relevant law. _Conditions:_ Requires the commander, eligible ground forces in the active or adjacent systems, and a tactical action that reaches the commit step; do not assume tokened systems or prohibited routes qualify. [SCPT 379 31:29–32:49; s000508–s000524; 32:54–33:38; s000525–s000533; 34:28–34:46; s000548–s000550]

**C03 — Project the possibility of a ground invasion to negotiate access, objective cooperation, or peace before starting an expensive fight.** The hosts value the threat more than actual violence. With Warfare, Sardakk can often reach an equidistant planet and keep a credible force there while leaving its ships parked. They suggest using that position to secure planets and perhaps a peaceful Support swap, rather than treating every threat as a commitment to attack. _Conditions:_ Most useful with connected planets, an unlocked or imminent commander, and neighbors open to deals; intimidation is table-dependent. [SCPT 379 66:14–67:36; s001071–s001098]

**C04 — Use T’ro to add two infantry to a controlled planet in the active system after a tactical action, especially to reinforce a forward invasion lane.** The guide likes this agent because Sardakk can otherwise run short on production capacity. Infantry appear on the front line rather than needing to be built at home and transported. The hosts explicitly connect this placement with the commander’s adjacent-system reach. _Conditions:_ Once per round, after a tactical action, on a planet Sardakk controls in the active system. [SCPT 379 27:58–28:38; s000457–s000468]

**C05 — Use Sh’val’s hero for a high-value ground strike only when ships can reach the system and a likely win justifies returning them to reinforcements.** After ships move into the active system, the hero can skip directly to committing ground forces; after the commitment, the card is purged and Sardakk’s ships there return to reinforcements. This can reach a fortified home system or bypass a dangerous space battle, but the guide frames it as a situational win-slay tool, not a routine early-game plan. _Conditions:_ Needs the unlocked hero, ships in the active system, enough ground forces to win, and a target worth the ships’ loss. [SCPT 379 38:10–38:28; s000599–s000605]

**C06 — Treat Light/Wave Deflectors as the key reach enabler for using Sh’val against a chosen target.** Sardakk lacks ordinary movement advantages, so a powerful ground force may fail to reach the target. The guide says Light/Wave makes carriers better at passing through blocking ships and therefore gives Sardakk more choice over where to use its hero. _Conditions:_ Most valuable in a tech-rich game or when a decisive hero strike is plausible; the ordinary path expects it around round five. [SCPT 379 76:20–76:45; s001237–s001242]

## Combat strengths and limits

**C07 — Account for the mech’s sustain trigger and direct counters before committing Sardakk mechs.** Valkyrie Exoskeletons produce a hit against opposing ground forces after the mech uses sustain during ground combat. The guide names Mentak’s mech as a counter because it turns off sustain. A PDS hit that destroys a mech first can also prevent the extra hit. _Conditions:_ Strongest when mechs survive long enough to sustain in ground combat; check the target’s abilities and pre-combat losses. [SCPT 379 24:36–25:33; s000405–s000416]

**C08 — Count Unrelenting only on combat rolls; do not add it to space cannon, anti-fighter barrage, or bombardment ability rolls.** The hosts distinguish combat rolls from ability rolls and warn against extending the modifier. They also say its advantage is more noticeable on many inexpensive units such as fighters and infantry, where a large pool creates more incidental hits. _Conditions:_ Applies when estimating space or ground combat dice; keep separate ability rolls distinct. [SCPT 379 20:38–21:06; s000321–s000327; 46:23–46:36; s000747–s000749; 47:45–48:04; s000770–s000780]

**C09 — Do not plan around Exotrireme I bombardment or a dreadnought-heavy identity; the guide sees Sardakk mainly as a carrier-and-ground-force faction.** The speakers find the special dreadnought’s extra bombardment less useful early than carrier capacity and say Sardakk can sometimes function without ships through its ground-force abilities. Their preferred combat plan uses carriers, fighters, infantry, and mechs. _Conditions:_ Early capacity and broad planet reach usually matter more than bombardment; a board that rewards bombardment can change this. [SCPT 379 21:41–22:47; s000343–s000357]

**C10 — Treat combat as a means to secure a point, planet, defense, or bargaining position—not as a scoring plan by itself.** The guide calls combat Sardakk’s visible strength but argues that fighting can cost more than it earns. It says the faction’s stronger distinction is versatile ground combat, while other factions bring combat advantages with added utility such as space cannon or sustain. _Conditions:_ Before a fight, name the objective or position it advances; avoid fighting only because the dice are better. [SCPT 379 53:08–55:48; s000873–s000910]

## Economy, objectives, and opening

**C11 — Plan around zero starting technologies and a weak economy instead of assuming Sardakk can keep pace without early research.** The guide calls the technology deficit a serious tempo problem, alongside three commodities and a difficult economy. It argues Sardakk normally needs a round-one research follow; missing a round can make round two feel like a delayed opening and widen the gap. _Conditions:_ Especially relevant with technology objectives, prerequisites, or a desired unit upgrade. [SCPT 379 52:08–52:36; s000849–s000860; 63:13–64:09; s001025–s001036]

**C12 — Expect planet-control objectives to be the more accessible category; do not confuse combat strength with broad scoring strength.** The hosts say Sardakk is poor at most objectives and identify control asks involving planets as the relative exception. They also say the faction has no special advantage in occupying planetless systems, and that winning a battle does not necessarily convert into points. _Conditions:_ Draft and expand toward the revealed objectives; technology and non-planet control asks can be unusually difficult. [SCPT 379 64:51–65:19; s001050–s001057; 88:40–89:19; s001484–s001496]

**C13 — In a Milty-style draft, seek a planet-rich slice and connected planets or a “land bridge,” especially if premium strategy cards are unlikely.** The guide’s example takes slice first, then uses a later speaker-order pick to sit beside another planet-rich slice. A path of planets can support commander reach toward a neighbor or Mecatol. The hosts acknowledge that the ideal combination is hard to draft and say to prioritize planets if the bridge is unavailable. _Conditions:_ Slice geometry matters more than assuming a favorable card pick; avoid a planetless gap along the intended route. [SCPT 379 90:05–92:35; s001507–s001550]

**C14 — Consider Warfare the preferred round-one primary pick when commander access and expansion leverage matter.** Sardakk can benefit from early expansion and the threat it creates. The guide says the main risk is another player not taking Warfare and calls Sardakk a faction that may be better off taking it itself. This concerns selecting the primary card; it does not mean the Warfare secondary replaces following Technology. _Conditions:_ Compare Warfare with Technology and Trade according to slice and economy. [SCPT 379 60:15–61:35; s000966–s000990; 63:13–64:09; s001025–s001036]

**C15 — Follow Technology in round one even if another card is the primary pick; double technology only when its immediate value justifies a round-one build sacrifice.** The hosts call a first-round Tech follow central to their plan. Double tech can catch Sardakk up and deliver immediately useful DET plus Gravity Drive, but its cost is spending commodity value and available cash on research instead of units. They prefer some balance when possible. _Conditions:_ Double tech suits a position with favorable costs and enough time to recover the fleet. [SCPT 379 63:10–64:09; s001023–s001036; 67:58–69:15; s001115–s001135]

**C16 — Take Trade as a round-one alternative when cash is needed to build units and still afford a Technology follow.** Trade is not the guide’s first pick over Warfare, but is highly helpful for a faction that must research and build plastic. The opening is framed as a tradeoff: improve the economy or unlock the commander, usually not both at once. _Conditions:_ Favor Trade when cash or commodity exchange is binding; favor Warfare when expansion and threat projection dominate. [SCPT 379 59:27–59:57; s000954–s000960; 63:02–63:21; s001020–s001025]

**C17 — Prioritize Dark Energy Tap (DET) as an early exploration investment when the slice offers safe access.** The speakers call Sardakk unusually reliant on DET because exploration can provide useful cards and bonus-point chances for a faction that struggles to score. They also say DET improves retreat flexibility for carriers without fighter escort, though a nearby ASR phrase says “BET,” likely a recognition error. Exploration remains a gamble. _Conditions:_ Budget the command tokens for exploration; avoid routes made dangerous by anomalies or enemy space cannon. [SCPT 379 70:00–70:59; s001148–s001157; 81:58–83:09; s001348–s001366]

## Research paths and tradeoffs

**C18 — Treat Gravity Drive and Carrier II as the core movement-and-capacity package, not a complete winning plan by themselves.** The guide names both as core technology and says Sardakk still needs other tools in some combination. Carriers expand access for infantry and fighters; Gravity Drive can extend their reach and occasionally support another capital ship. _Conditions:_ Prioritize in blue-focused paths; skips and bonus research can alter order. [SCPT 379 69:20–70:00; s001140–s001147]

**C19 — Use Sling Relay to ease production limits, especially when it can add a capital ship alongside normal fighter and infantry builds.** The standard path uses Sling Relay because home production alone may not last the game. The example supplements ordinary builds with a capital ship, fighters, and infantry; T’ro can add more infantry at the front. _Conditions:_ Useful when research tempo permits and production is binding; an alternative Exotrireme path gives it up. [SCPT 379 71:40–72:11; s001165–s001176]

**C20 — Treat Fighter II and Bio-Stims from a green skip as optional additions after the core blue path and Light/Wave.** Fighter II benefits from Sardakk’s combat modifier; Bio-Stims can ready Sling Relay again to create a stall action. The guide says these are extras, not substitutes for reaching key movement and capacity technologies. Once the core path is secured, later spending may be better directed to units and defense. _Conditions:_ Requires bonus tech tempo; protect resources and tokens needed for scoring and production. [SCPT 379 75:51–77:29; s001233–s001258]

**C21 — Pursue Exotrireme II on the flashy late-game path only when both yellow and blue skips make room without sacrificing Light/Wave.** The proposed five-tech line is DET, Gravity Drive, Carrier II, Exotrireme II, then Light/Wave. The speakers like the late-game punch but explicitly require a blue skip in addition to yellow. Without both, use the ordinary path or bonus tech. _Conditions:_ Needs both skips and enough research tempo; the default route already expects Light/Wave near round five. [SCPT 379 77:41–78:50; s001271–s001292]

**C22 — Usually avoid a red-skip detour and Valkyrie Particle Weave; the guide calls it costly win-more research, not a needed ground-combat fix.** The hosts could not identify a necessary red-skip technology and reject early AI Development Algorithm merely to hedge toward a possible third unit upgrade. Particle Weave can be potent, especially with mechs, but the guide says Sardakk already fights well on the ground and the deep path does not add enough to its toolkit. _Conditions:_ Reconsider only when concrete objectives, discounts, or bonus research make it timely. [SCPT 379 80:08–81:58; s001312–s001347]

**C23 — Remember Exotrireme II’s ship-destruction ability when attacking Sardakk, but first check that the upgrade is actually present.** The guide describes Sardakk destroying its Exotrireme II after a round of space combat to destroy up to two ships in the system. That can remove attacking carriers and make a later invasion harder against Sardakk’s ground defense. The hosts warn this is easy to overlook because the upgrade is expensive and not researched every game. _Conditions:_ Attackers should plan capacity for possible ship losses; the ability matters only if the unit survives to use it. [SCPT 379 41:16–43:09; s000653–s000683]

## Deals and alliance value

**C24 — Advertise and sell Tekklar Legion near the ground-combat window; accept a modest offer rather than expecting it to sell itself.** Sardakk needs trade goods, so the guide recommends making the note visible and explaining its value to a player facing a difficult ground combat. It suggests flexibility on price. Buyers may not plan ahead. _Conditions:_ Best when the buyer expects a consequential ground fight and can use the note at the relevant timing window. [SCPT 379 26:15–26:33; s000423–s000427; 84:08–85:10; s001397–s001408]

**C25 — Arrange an Alliance buyer early and favor a partner that can keep ground forces positioned for adjacent-system commitments.** G’hom Sek’kus may help factions with useful mechs or scattered infantry; the hosts name Titans, Sol, and Naaz-Rokha as possible buyers. An ally who did not plan infantry placement may not realize the value in time, so begin the conversation early. _Conditions:_ Buyer, terrain, and unit mix determine value; Sardakk itself is particularly built to exploit the commander. [SCPT 379 85:10–87:00; s001410–s001446; 87:54–88:36; s001465–s001481]
