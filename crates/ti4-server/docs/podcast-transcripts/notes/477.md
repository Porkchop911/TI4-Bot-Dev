# SCPT 477 Ultimate Guide to Winslaying and Point-Blocking (2026-10-07, Hunter + Blasto) — own transcription tiny.en, 0:19-1:22
- 0:20 definitions: winslaying = stopping a player from scoring at all (usually take their home system; PoK-era, heroes e.g. Sardakk/L1 pushed it). Point blocking = setting a player back one tempo-locked point (a public this round, the Imperial MR point, Styx) — any phase.
- 0:23 TE speeds game up (ends R4 status / R5 action): factions have one round less of plastic/tokens, not enough gas for big winslays -> point block earlier instead. Waiting until R5 to act "doesn't work anymore": too many contenders.
- 0:25 Styx in ~70-75% of games + 3 secrets = ~4 VPs not tied to home system; home-system defence matters less in TE; defend the forward fleet instead.
- 0:28 Sardakk breakthrough/hero weaker in TE; Sardakk poor economy; L1Z1X hero now used for Styx not slaying.
- 0:31 Who to block: count everyone's tempo; assume all secrets scoreable (3 secrets in hand R4 => at least one action-phase); look at board (Warfare in hand, way to reach Styx R4, Argent/Yin hero reach). Track speaker tempo: speaker next round can take Imperial — assume they can score MR point.
- 0:32 Block players AHEAD of you, knock them from 1st to ~4th but not out (they keep playing for points, no revenge). Blocking someone not ahead of you justifies their retaliation.
- 0:33-0:36 Don't do mutually-assured-destruction revenge; take the licks and keep playing for points (better over many games). Hunter: play to win from ahead so a setback leaves you on par.
- 0:44 Winnu must be point-blocked whenever they get momentum (assume Winnu hero = 2 points).
- 0:45-0:52 Point blocks are team efforts: rich/utility factions (Creuss, Jol-Nar, Deepwrought, Nomad) should ENABLE combat factions (Sardakk, Yin, L1Z1X) for free (IFF/wormholes, agents, promissory notes, money) — the bruiser pays the token and risks ships. Ideal cost: 1 command token + 1 capital ship (e.g. one carrier + infantry). Never over-invest.
- 0:53 cooperation is temporary; afterwards back to neutral; no ride-or-die.
- 0:55 Priority targets: player who took custodians AND scored R1 (can reach 10 w/o support if they also get Imperial). Custodians without scoring = just on tempo; not urgent.
- 0:56 Types of points to block: CONTROL objectives (take their 4th industrial / edge / empty systems; Push Boundaries by attacking a neighbour) — best when the planet helps you too; ECONOMIC objectives: trade-good objectives -> propose round-long trade embargo on leader (forces suboptimal tech like Psychoarchaeology); spend-X influence/resources -> wait until they've exhausted planets then take one; EXTORT instead of attack (money, X-89 from Jol-Nar). TECH objectives basically unblockable, but declining to take Technology/Construction primary can stop a leader's double-score with Imperial.
- 1:08 blocking custodians to choose who gets it: keep it away from S-tier/home-insensitive factions (Saar, Deepwrought, Mahact? "star lancers"...), prefer giving it to a slayable faction (e.g. Last Bastion).
- 1:12 Blasto: Saar #1 (runaway potential); Hunter: Deepwrought #1 (excels at control objectives, home system hard to take via coded systems). Disagreement.
- 1:14 combat margin: always more HP + better dice than defender (carrier+fighter vs carrier risky; carrier+fighter+destroyer vs carrier "perfect"); failed point-blocks are embarrassing — over-prepare small fights.
- 1:16 keep helping the bruiser if their attempt failed (you'll need them again).
- 1:19 Anti-block defence: score control/contestable objectives EARLY and leave locked ones (tech, trade goods) for later — so blockers can't stop you late.
