# SCPT 285 What is the Midgame? (2023-02-08, Matt + Hunter; PoK) — base.en, read 0:06-0:48 of 95 min
- 0:14 "Early game" = opening expansion + until custodians is taken ("pre-game" mini game about who gets custodians; Milty draft is part of it); also learning the table's customs (X-1 etc.).
- 0:22 R1: decide immediately whether you can score; it changes your whole mid game. Hunter's R1 card logic: custodians > what makes me score; often Trade, then Diplomacy — "if Diplomacy would be the difference for the last-pick player, rely on them; otherwise just take Diplomacy yourself"; ideal R1 strategy phase: 1st Trade, 2nd Politics, 3rd Diplomacy.
- 0:26 Late game starts (per player) when you can envision your exact path to 10 — for many when the first stage II flips; players are in different phases; the non-late players' job is to push the late players' win back to the round when everybody matters.
- 0:36 Mid game is defined by SECRETS: publics give flexibility (2-3 scoreable options per round), secrets are dealt slowly (sometimes only ~3 seen in a game) and status-phase secrets take several rounds to set up (9 ground forces, 4 PDS, tech secrets) — spend mid-game effort setting up secrets, plan cycling bad ones.
- 0:40 Public order: save tech objectives for last (auto-score), spend objectives later (spend money on plastic now), do hard control objectives early — some (e.g. "more planets than two neighbours") only get harder as the board fills.
- 0:42 Aggression for points: weigh plastic committed, counter-attack exposure (Cruiser II snipes get retaken), and how much harder the point gets later. Hunter: think from your faction's specifics; sometimes you WANT a clear leader for the table to slay (don't point-block too early if there are many slayers and you're next in line).
