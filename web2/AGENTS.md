# web2: rules for agents

Read this before you change the UI in `web2/`. These are decisions of the project owner (2026-10-07).

## Consistency

- The UI must feel consistent. A new UX feature must work the same way on every screen where a player can expect it.
- Before you add an interaction (a key, a gesture, a navigation, a kind of control), check each other screen: does it apply there, and does it mean the same thing there?
- If it applies to one screen or a few screens only, do not add it. Use an interaction that the UI already has, or find one that applies everywhere.
- An exception needs a reason that a player can see on the screen, and a line in this file.

## Patterns

These rules hold on every screen. The feature sections below give the specifics and the exceptions.

1. **Controls are where the choice is made.** A control is next to the thing that it changes, not in another place that only has the room. Cases: the payment controls are on the map; the editor is in the row of the viewer in the seat list; the selected technology cell is the control.
2. **Everything that a choice needs is on one screen, with no click.** Prefer one overview to a view for each item. A view that needs a click for each item is the last resort. Case: the seat list shows what every player did.
3. **A staged change shows "now → after" in game values.** Cases: pools "3 → 5", "Trade goods 2 → 5", "Strategy pool 2 → 1".
4. **Colour is never the only carrier of a state.** A state also has a sign, a word or a shape. Cases: ▶ ✓ – ✎ · in the seat list; "+" and the empty outline on tokens; hollow prerequisite marks; "⚠ 1 wasted".
5. **An amount against a cost has three states, always the same.** Short is red and blocks the main button. Exact is green. More than the cost is orange with "⚠", and it is allowed.
6. **One signal for one state.** A state is a pill or a sign. Do not add a frame, a tint and a badge for the same state, and do not repeat it in several places of one screen.
7. **An element that never changes and repeats other content is removed.** Case: the pills for the stages of a strategy card.
8. **An amount changes in steps of one.** A button for the usual target can stand next to the counter ("Pay rest", "Auto-pay"). There is no all-or-nothing button for an amount.
9. **When a screen does not fit: move, restructure, make dense. Do not scroll.** In this order: put a summary into a row that exists on every screen (the footer, the heading, the header of a block); remove what repeats; make secondary rows dense. Only then ask the owner for an exception. Cases: the payment line in the footer; the chosen technologies in the header of the tree; the 24px rows of the seat list.
10. **Live, Draft, History and the event log use the same screen.** Read-only is the same screen with nothing open, not another view.
11. **Hidden information.** A choice of another player is visible when it is resolved, never before. A row of another player is not a control.
12. **Symbols follow the physical game** where the game has a convention. Case: command tokens are triangles, and the fleet pool points up.
13. **Names and long texts.** A name is in a column of fixed width and is cut with "…"; the full name is on hover. The demo data keeps two long player names, 8 players, and the rows that only some factions have (faction technologies). Check every screen against them.

Checklist for a new screen or a changed screen:

- Where does the player make the choice? Are the controls there?
- What must the player see to decide? Is all of it on this screen?
- Does every state have a sign or a word, and only one signal?
- Does it fit at 1920×1080 with 8 players and the long names?
- On a phone: is nothing cut at the side, and is the main button in view?
- Does History show the same screen read-only?

## Screen size

There are two layouts, and no others.

- **Desktop** is the design target: a viewport of 1920×1080 or larger. Every rule in this file is written for it.
- **Phone** is a portrait phone, checked on a Pixel 9 (412×923, the installed app with no browser bars). The owner added it on 2026-10-08. It shows the same views with the same blocks; only the shell differs.
- Tablets, landscape phones and narrow desktop windows are a **non-goal**. They get whichever of the two layouts their width selects.
- There is one breakpoint: `phone:` in `src/styles/theme.css`, and `usePhone()` in `src/ui/usePhone.ts` with the same width. Do not add another breakpoint or a container query.
- `touch:` (a coarse pointer) is only for the size of a control under a finger. It does not change the layout.
- Desktop screenshots and layout tests use 1920×1080. Phone tests use a Pixel 9 in portrait. The projects are in `playwright.config.ts`: `desktop` and `phone` for the real game, `slow` and `slow-phone` for its long tests, `shots` and `shots-phone` for the demo (see "Tests").

### Phone

- The shell shows one pane at a time: **Map · Players · Action**, with a tab bar at the bottom. The toolbar stays on top.
- The footer of the action (note, payment line, main button) is under every pane, so the main button is always in reach. This is how pattern 2 holds on a phone: the choice and the button that sends it are on one screen, the rest is one tap away.
- Exception (owner, 2026-10-10): while the fleet of a system is open on the map, the footer is not shown. The sheet has its room. The "✓" of the sheet closes it, and the footer is back. The landings of an invasion are such a sheet too.
- The pane changes only with a tap on its tab (owner, 2026-10-14): an update, a new choice or an undo never switches it. The tab of the pane where the open decision of the viewer is made has a blinking dot while another pane is in view: a choice on the board is Map, a choice of a player is Players, every other decision is Action. A game opens on the pane of its choice.
- A pane may scroll down. Pattern 9 and "the open step must not need vertical scroll" are desktop rules. Nothing may be cut at the side: `e2e/phone.spec.ts` checks every example for this (`npm run shots`).
- The player table keeps the names in view and scrolls the values sideways. A tap on a row opens the sheet of the player; there is no hover sheet.
- A phone has no hover and no keys. A shortcut key is not shown. A name that desktop cuts with "…" takes a second line where the full name is only on hover (the technology tree, the action picker).
- The map zooms with two fingers; the "+" and "−" buttons are not shown under a finger. "Fit task" and "Fit board" stay, in the row of the map views.
- Exception to "no short variants of labels" (owner, 2026-10-10): the map views and the two fit buttons are icons on a phone, so that they are one row. The name is the tooltip and the accessible name. Desktop keeps the words.
- The reference sheets, the draft menu and (in a local game) Dev are one menu in the toolbar. A sheet covers the pane.
- Exception (owner, 2026-10-11): while a sheet covers the pane (a reference sheet, the sheet of a player, Dev), the footer and the tab bar are not shown. The sheet has their room, and its "×" gives them back. A sheet on the map (a fleet, the landings, a build) keeps the tab bar: the Action pane says what is staged.
- The app can be installed (`public/manifest.webmanifest`): fullscreen, locked to portrait. There is no service worker: the game is live, and the CDN already caused stale bundles once. The icons come from `scripts/make-icons.mjs`.
- A new screen is checked in both layouts. Short variants of labels are still not allowed; restructure the row instead.

## Action panel

- The action panel has one fixed width. It does not change with the step.
- There is no control to change the panel width.
- The board for 8 players (61 systems) must stay usable in the space that remains. Improve the board; do not make the panel narrower.

## Text

- The users are experts of the game.
- Text that states a rule or explains the UI is not visible by default. Put it behind hover/click (`Hint` in `src/ui/overlays.tsx`).
- Exception: a list where the player chooses one card (the list of action cards) shows the printed text of each card. The list has the room, and the text is what the player chooses by.
- The action picker itself keeps its rule text behind hover/click. Inline text on every row was tried (2026-10-07) and was too much.
- Visible text states the game state: costs, counts, errors, and who the game waits for.
- The open step must not need vertical scroll at 1920×1080. `e2e/shots.spec.ts` checks this for every example (`npm run shots`).
- Do not abbreviate labels when there is room. The player table uses full column names.
- **Rules that a player may need to look up are in the UI, behind hover or click** (owner, 2026-10-11). These are: every text that is printed only on a component and is not in the rule book (a unit, a card, a technology, a leader, a faction ability), and special abilities that are not used often. General rules are not in the UI: the flow of the game, and the rules of every game such as combat dice or the unit keywords (SUSTAIN DAMAGE, BOMBARDMENT). A keyword is shown with its values and no explanation. These are examples, not a list: decide by "would an expert look this up?".
- Rule text is never written in web2. It comes from `ti4-content` through the engine (a fact of the update, or the catalogue).

## Reference sheets

General points from the Objectives sheet (owner, 2026-10-11). Use them for the next sheet (agendas, technologies, relics).

- **A sheet answers one question, and its section here says which in its first line.** Ask the owner for the question before the layout. Two sheets do not answer the same question: the Objectives sheet says what points can still be scored or lost, the sheet of a player says why the seat has its points.
- **A sheet about what comes does not show what is done.** A thing that every seat has, that is used up, or that can no longer come is not listed. It is in the sheet that is about what was.
- **A thing is on a sheet once.** A list of a deck does not repeat what the sheet shows above it.
- **A deck is a list in groups by meaning**, not by a printed field alone. The groups are written by hand in `ti4-content`, beside the corpus (`secret_objectives.rs`, `public_objectives.rs`), with a test that every card has one. A group is closed until the player opens it, and its heading has a count.
- **Printed text is in `ti4-content`.** Look there before you say that a text is missing; a fact of `ti4-view` or the catalogue brings it to the page. A card of a sheet shows its text. A line of a list has the text behind the name (`Peek`, with the dotted underline).
- **Seats.** The viewer is first, then the other seats by their points. A seat on a card is a tag: the symbol and the faction. Eight seats are one row of a phone as symbol, number and faction in two lines. When a sheet can be seen for one seat, the row of the seats is the filter.
- **A phone sheet has the whole pane**: no footer and no tab bar. It does not scroll to the side. A heading that stays in view has two lines at most; a caption goes into a line that is there already.
- **Which rows a sheet shows is a flag of the view model** (`ScoreRowView.open`), set in `session/select`. The component does not decide it from names.
- **How to work on a new sheet.** The owner looks at it on the phone, in a late-game scenario, after each step. The owner may ask for typechecks only while the layout changes; the tests are brought up to date with each step and are run before the commit. Format only the files that you changed (`npx oxfmt <files>`): a run on a folder changes other files.

## Action picker and shortcuts

- There is no separate turn bar. The action picker is the content of the action panel when the viewer has the turn and no action is open.
- The picker has fixed groups in a fixed order: Tactical, Strategic, Component, Trade, Pass. A row that cannot be used stays in its place and shows the reason.
- The picker is one list. The whole row is the control: key, group, name, and the state at the right edge. Rows have no buttons of their own.
- A choice in the picker sends nothing. A tactical action opens as the private draft. A strategic or component action is staged. The main button of the footer sends it.
- After the viewer's action, the panel shows what uses no action, and the footer has "End turn".
- A shortcut key is data: `ActionButtonView.key`. The button shows the key. Enter is the main button of the footer. Esc goes one level back.
- The picker is static: it has the same rows in every turn of a game. Only the number of strategy cards changes it, with the player count. Action cards have one row ("Play an action card"), other components have one row. A second list asks which one, with the keys 1, 2, 3.
- A strategy card has its number as key.
- Browser back is not used for game choices. Esc goes one level back; a draft has undo and redo. Browser back was tried for the picker (2026-10-07) and removed: it could not apply to the other screens.
- Shortcuts do not act in a text field or while a dialog, a menu or a rule text is open. A control that the player reached with Tab keeps Enter.

## Decisions

A decision is any choice that a card, an ability or a rule asks for. Every decision uses the same parts.

- A decision appears where its cause is: as blocks in the open action. The panel does not switch to another screen, and there is no dialog.
- The source is on top: the card, ability or rule that asks, as printed (the `source` block). The heading says what the player must do.
- There are three shapes of choice. The thing that is chosen decides the shape:
  - on the board, when it is on the board (a system, a planet, a place). The panel shows the choice and the limits, not a list of candidates;
  - in the player table, when it is a player. The table already shows the values of each player;
  - in a list, for everything else (answers, rewards, cards). Rows are the controls, with the keys 1, 2, 3 and the effect in game values at the right edge.
- An amount is a counter row. A limit is a gauge above the rows.
- A choice is staged, then sent. A staged choice shows "Selected" and "Not sent". The main button of the footer sends it. Esc clears the choice first, then leaves the action.
- A thing that cannot be chosen stays in view and shows the reason.
- Draft and History use the same blocks.

### Reaction windows

The owner asked on 2026-10-13 for the printed text of every reaction and for a setting of every reaction.

- A reaction window is the question of the action panel, with the title "Reaction". The heading states the trigger in game values ("After Hacan’s tactical action in system 18"), never an id of the engine. An event with no wording of its own is said in its words, with the seat and the system (`select/reaction.ts`, `triggerText`).
- The window lists what the viewer can use now, one row each: an action card, a leader, a faction ability, a technology, a promissory note, a unit, a breakthrough. A row has the printed name, the kind at the right, the printed timing and text, and the keys 1, 2, 3. No window has code of its own: the engine says what every option stands for (`reaction` of the update, `ti4-view/src/reaction.rs`, from the id of the option).
- The main button of the footer is Pass. A row stages its reaction; the main button then says "Use", and Esc clears it. Enter always does what the main button says. The decline is no row.
- **The setting of a reaction** is in its row of the window, at the right edge, and on its card in a sheet (pattern 1: the window is where a reaction is muted; the sheet is where it is set back, because a muted window does not open). It is one list (`OfferSelect`): "Offered", "Only on my turns", "Never". It applies to the windows after it and leaves a toast. The window that is open stays open and is answered by the player: the list only sets, it never passes (owner, 2026-10-13). Exception to "a choice is staged, then sent": it is a setting of the seat and no decision of the game.
- "Only on my turns" is of the action phase: the reaction is passed while another seat is the active player. Outside the action phase nobody has the turn, the reaction is offered, and the list of a window there has "Offered" and "Never" only.
- The sheets: the Cards sheet for an action card of the hand; the sheet of the viewer for a leader, a faction ability with a timing and a technology that is used. A reaction that is not always offered and has no card there (a promissory note, a unit, a breakthrough) is a row of "Other reactions" in the sheet of the viewer. The sheet of another seat has no setting.
- A window that does not open because of the setting leaves a toast that names what was not offered.
- The setting is applied where the game runs, by `ti4_view::reaction_modes::NeverOffer`: the engine of a local game and the server use the same wrapper. A reaction is set by its key (`ReactionOffer.key`): the printed name of an action card, so every copy is covered, or the kind and the id of the card of an ability ("leader:sardakkagent"), so an ability that hangs on several events has one setting. The page only tells the transport (`Transport.setReactionMode`) and shows what the transport says (`modes`, `passed` of its events). A window that offers only what the seat muted is declined without asking; a window with something else is asked without it. Only a window that the seat may decline is touched. In a local game that decline is an answer like any other, so the saved game has it, and a saved game is played again the same whatever is set now. An undo takes it back together with the answer of the player before it. The modes are kept with the saved game (`cardsKey`; a page of before 2026-10-13 kept the names of the Never cards, and they are read), and by the server (`reaction_modes.json`).
- The engine says whose turn it is with the question (`details.active` of the choice, display only), so the filter needs no state.
- Not there yet: an ability that asks its own question outside a window of the timing resolver (some "you may" abilities) is a decision in the list, with no setting; the mock still has the two modes of a card.

### Action cards

The owner asked for this on 2026-10-11: the Cards sheet has the hand of the viewer and the whole deck.

- "Your action cards" has the count against the hand limit ("Hand 5 / 7"; over the limit it is red with "⚠") and one card for each printed name, in the frame of the cards of a player sheet: the name, "×2" for copies, when it is played ("ACTION:", a timing) and the text.
- A card that a window offers has its setting at the right of the name (the Reaction windows section): "Offered", "Only on my turns" or "Never". A card with "ACTION:" has none: no window offers it.
- "Action card deck" says how many cards are left and how many are discarded, then the cards in groups by when a card is played ("Action", "Your activation", "Combat", "Agenda phase"), each with the number of its names. A group is closed until the player opens it (owner, 2026-10-11), with "▸" and "▾" as the rounds of the log; there are too many cards for one list. The groups and their order come from the catalogue (`action_card_groups`, written by hand in `ti4-content`); the hand has the same order, with no headings. A group has one row for all cards of a name, as the exploration decks: "3 of 4 unseen" (not discarded and not in the hand of the viewer, so in the deck or in the hand of another player), "1 in your hand · 1 discarded", and the printed text. A card with none unseen is dimmed; "0 of 1 unseen" is its sign, with no "–" before it (owner, 2026-10-11).
- Exception to "rule text is behind hover": the sheet shows the printed text of each card, as the deck sheet of the exploration does.
- "Fake" is on the row of a card that the viewer does not hold and that can still be in a hand. A card with "ACTION:" has none (owner, 2026-10-11): no window offers it, so there is nothing to fake. A faked card is shown after the hand, with a dashed frame and the word "Faked", and "Stop faking". It is a setting of the page and is sent nowhere yet: later it gives the viewer the windows of that card.
- The facts come from the engine: the hand (`held_action_cards`), the deck count, the discard pile and the hand limit (`action_cards` of the update). The printed cards and the deck that a game begins with are the catalogue (`components.action_cards`). Copies of a card have ids of their own; the sheet counts by printed name.
- How the setting of a card is applied and kept is in the Reaction windows section.

### Secret objectives

The owner asked for this on 2026-10-11: the Secrets sheet has the secret objectives of the viewer and the whole deck. It is a reference sheet of its own, after Cards.

- "Your secret objectives" has "1 scored · 3 / 3" (scored and unscored together against the limit) and one card for each objective, in the frame of the cards of a player sheet: the name, the phase in which it is scored and the text. The state is at the right of the name: "✓ Scored" or "Not scored". The hand is first, then the scored objectives.
- "Secret objective deck" says how many objectives are unseen and how many cards the deck holds ("31 of 40 unseen · 22 in the deck"), then the objectives in groups by what an objective asks for ("Planets you control", "Ships on the board", "Win a combat"). A group is closed until the player opens it, as a group of the action card deck; its heading says how many of it are unseen ("4 of 6 unseen"). The groups and their order come from the catalogue (`secret_objective_groups`, written by hand in `ti4-content`).
- Every objective is in the deck once (owner, 2026-10-11), so a row has a word and no count: "Unseen" (in the deck or in the hand of another player), "In your hand", "✓ Scored by you", or "✓ Scored by" with the seat. A hand that the game reveals (Search Warrant) is "In the hand of" with the seat. A row that is not unseen is dimmed. The row has the phase and the printed text.
- Exception to "rule text is behind hover": the sheet shows the printed text of each objective, as the Cards sheet does.
- The facts come from the engine: the hand (`held_secret_objectives`), and the deck count, what each seat has scored and the limit (`secret_objectives` of the update). The printed objectives and the deck that a game begins with are the catalogue (`components.secret_objectives`). The "Secrets" column of the player table counts the scored objectives from the same fact.
- Not there yet: how far a seat is with an objective, and the sheet for another seat sorted by that.

### Agendas sheet

The sheet answers: which laws hold now, and what can an agenda phase still bring? The owner asked for it on 2026-10-11. It is a reference sheet of its own, after Secrets, and every viewer has it.

- "Laws in play" has the count ("2 laws") and one card for each law, by name, in the frame of the cards of the other sheets: the name, "Law" with what the vote is about ("Law · Elect Player"), and the text of the outcome that passed only ("For: …" or "Against: …"). What passed is at the right of the name: the elected seat as a tag, or a word ("For", "Against", the name of what was elected). With no law it says "None".
- The agenda under vote is not on the sheet: it is in the action panel (owner, 2026-10-11).
- "Agenda deck" says how many agendas are not in play and how many cards the deck holds ("48 not in play · 47 in the deck"), then the agendas in groups by what an agenda affects ("Victory points and objectives", "Ministers and offices", "Planets", "Units and combat", "Map and movement", "Technology", "Command tokens, trade goods and cards", "Laws and votes"). A group is closed until the player opens it, and its heading has the number of its agendas. The groups and their order come from the catalogue (`agenda_groups`, written by hand in `ti4-content/src/agendas.rs`).
- Every agenda is in the deck once, so a row has no count. A row has the name, "Law" or "Directive" with what the vote is about, and the printed text of both outcomes, one line each. A law in play is not listed again.
- A row has no state (owner, 2026-10-11): the engine keeps no discard pile for agendas, so an agenda that was voted on and is gone stays in the list. The two numbers of the heading say how many are gone.
- Exception to "rule text is behind hover": the sheet shows the printed text of each agenda, as the Secrets sheet does.
- The facts come from the engine: the laws with their outcome (`table.laws` of the view) and the deck count (`agendas` of the update). The printed agendas and the deck that a game begins with are the catalogue (`components.agendas`). A note that a card prints with what is elected ("When this agenda is revealed, …") is with its text.
- Not there yet: which agendas are discarded; a law that is blank for a turn (Keleres) is shown as in play.

### Objectives sheet

The owner asked for this on 2026-10-11. The two sheets have two goals. The Objectives sheet is about what comes: the points that a seat can still score or lose. The sheet of a player is about what was: every point of the seat with its reason (the Player sheet section).

- The sheet has the sources that are not done: a public objective that not every seat scored, the custodians while no seat has them, Support for the Throne, and every agenda that a seat has or lost a point from (a law can be repealed; the owner asked for all agendas on 2026-10-11). What is done is not there: an objective that every seat scored, the secret objectives, Imperial, a relic. A stage whose objectives are all done says "All scored by every seat".
- Every source is a card, in the frame of the cards of the other sheets: the name, what an objective is worth ("1 VP"), its printed text, and a tag for each seat that has something from it. The owner chose this on 2026-10-11. Tried before on that day: one cell for each seat under each name (the heading was too high on a phone), and a table that scrolls to the side.
- A tag is the symbol and the faction of a seat. For a source that is not an objective it has the points after the faction ("Sol 2", "Xxcha −1"), and for Support for the Throne "←" and the symbol of the seat whose note it is. A card that no seat has anything from says "Nobody yet".
- The standings are on top and stay in view while the sheet scrolls: one column for each seat, with the symbol, the points and the faction. The viewer is first, then the other seats by their points, the most first. The first group line says the points that win ("10 VP to win").
- A seat of the standings is the filter (a control that only this sheet has: the seats are its heading, and the filter is where the seat is). A tap chooses the seat, a second tap shows all again. With a seat chosen the sheet shows what that seat can still score: the public objectives that it has not scored, with the count ("3 of 5 open for Hacan", "All scored for Hacan"), and of the other sources the ones that it has points from (what it can lose) or that no seat has. The tags of the other seats stay. The filter is not kept.
- The groups are "Public objectives · stage I", "Public objectives · stage II" and "Other points". An objective says what it is worth at the right of its name ("1 VP"). The objectives are in the order of the reveal.
- The other sources of the game are rows of the same facts, with a name of their own: "Secret objectives", "Custodians of Mecatol Rex", "Imperial · Mecatol Rex", "Support for the Throne", and a row for an agenda, a relic, an action card or a planet that a seat has a point from ("Agenda · Mutiny", "Relic · Shard of the Throne"). Points that no source explains are a row "Other". A row says whether it is still open (`ScoreRowView.open`); the Objectives sheet shows the open rows, the sheet of a player all rows of its seat.
- "Public objective deck" is under the sources: the public objectives that the game can still reveal ("18 not revealed"), in groups by what an objective asks for ("Spend", "Planets you control", "Units on the board", "Structures", "Technology"). A group is closed until the player opens it, as a group of the secret objective deck, and its heading has the number of its objectives. A row has the name, what it is worth ("2 VP") and the printed text. An objective that is revealed is not listed again: it is a card above. Once an objective of stage II is revealed, the objectives of stage I are not listed (owner, 2026-10-11). A game draws five objectives of each stage and no fact says which, so the list is everything that can come. The objectives and the groups come from the catalogue (`components.public_objectives`, `public_objective_groups`, written by hand in `ti4-content`); the demo has none.
- Exception to "rule text is behind hover": the sheet shows the printed text of each public objective, agenda and deck row, as the Secrets sheet does.
- The facts come from the engine (`score` of the update, `ti4-view/src/score.rs`): the objectives as printed with who scored each, and the other sources, with the printed text of an agenda, a relic, a card or a planet, from the ledger of the engine, which has a row for every point that is given or taken (`vp_ledger`). web2 only words the rows.
- Not there yet: a point that a seat can lose from a relic (Shard of the Throne) is not in the Objectives sheet, because the engine does not say which points can be lost; how far a seat is with a public objective.

### Payment

- Every control of an open payment is on the board, in one bar on top of the map: how the payment stands ("Paying 4 / 6 resources", then "2 more" red, "✓ Paid" green or "⚠ 1 wasted" orange), the trade goods, "Auto-pay" and "Clear payment".
- Trade goods are not on the map. The bar has a counter for them, in steps of one, and "Pay rest": it sets the trade goods to what the planets leave, so the payment is exact. It is disabled when the trade goods cannot do that.
- Auto-pay stages the payment with the least waste, with planets before trade goods. The player can change it; nothing is sent.
- The panel has no payment block while a payment is open. The footer has one line for it, left of the note: what pays and the total ("Payment Jord 4 + Vefut 2 · 6 / 6"). It uses no vertical space.
- A recorded or planned payment is a read-only line in the content. The board has no bar for it.

## Strategy cards

All eight cards use one frame: one list of seats. There are no tabs, no seat column and no trail.

- The list has the primary ("P") and then the secondaries in seat order. Each seat is one full-width line: order, symbol, name, state, and what the seat did. All decisions made so far are on one screen, with no click.
- A state has a sign: "▶ Deciding", "· Waits for Blair", "✎ Private draft", "✎ Draft ready", "✓ Resolved", "✓ Followed", "– Passed".
- Only the row of the viewer opens: into the editor, in place, while the viewer has a choice or an open draft there. After it resolves the row closes and shows its result like any other row. A ready draft is closed and says what it will do.
- Other rows are not controls. A choice of another player is visible when it is resolved, never before.
- The same list, with no open row, is the read-only view of a strategy card in the event log.
- The list is dense (24px rows) so that the largest editor, the technology tree with a payment, fits with all eight seats.

- The whole primary is one screen. Every part is staged; "Resolve primary" sends all of it. It is split only where the game reveals hidden information (Politics: the agenda cards are a second screen, sent again).
- A part of a card is a block that uses one of the decision shapes. The board and the player table serve the first part that is not complete.
- A chosen thing is a row with "Change". "Change" clears that part, so it is the open part again. Esc clears the last choice, then leaves the action.
- A part that needs no choice is a value: "Trade goods 2 → 5", "Victory points 6 → 7".
- A secondary is the same editor as its part of the primary, in the row of the viewer: the pill of the heading says "Private draft" or "Your decision". It can be drafted before the seat is reached. It states what it costs: "Strategy pool 2 → 1", or who gave it for free.
- A secondary with a choice: nothing chosen is a pass, and the main button says so. A secondary without a choice (Politics, Trade, Imperial) is a list with "Follow" and "Pass", keys 1 and 2.
- In a game of the engine (2026-10-12) the list of the seats is built, with the facts of the engine (`secondary` of the update): the names are the players of the table (the factions in a game with no table), and the viewer is "You". The editors of the cards are not built: the row of the viewer opens into the question of the game as a list, with the question and "Strategy pool 2 → 1" above it. A row of another seat has its state and no text of what the seat did.
- The draft of a secondary is in Live, not in the Draft tab: it is the row of the viewer. It opens by itself from the moment the card is played (owner, 2026-10-12), also while the primary is resolved; Trade opens with its window, because the primary changes what the secondary costs. Each answer is recorded with "Record". When the draft has nothing more to decide, the main button is "Mark draft ready". A ready draft has "Change draft"; "Start over" drops the answers.
- A ready draft is sent when the game asks the seat, and the seat is not asked. The game checks it first: a draft that no longer fits is not ready, the footer says so, and the seat decides in Live. Online the server sends it (the Online play section), in a local game the page. A draft that is not ready when the seat is reached is not sent: the game asks, and "Send draft" sends the draft as it is.
- A secondary that asks the seat nothing as the game is (Leadership with no influence to spend) is no draft: the row says "Nothing to decide as the game is now".
- A draft ends where the game would show something hidden (the cards of Politics, the objective of Imperial). The row says so, and the draft can be ready: the game goes on from there in Live.
- A command pool is a row of triangles, one for each token, with the count ("3 → 5") and a counter. The fleet pool points up, the tactic and strategy pools point down, as on the command sheet. A token that stays is filled, an added token has "+", a removed token is an empty outline with "−".
- Exception to "the source is on top": the printed text of a strategy card is behind hover. There are eight cards and every player knows them.
- Several players are chosen in the player table too (Trade). A row shows what the choice gives that player ("+6 commodities").
- The technology tree is a block in the panel, not a dialog: four colour columns, a row for the unit upgrades, and a row for the faction technologies when the faction has any that are not unit upgrades. A faction technology shows its colour as a square mark before the name. A cell is the control. A filled symbol is a prerequisite that the player has, a hollow one is missing. The printed text of a technology is behind "?".
- Exception to "a chosen thing is a row with Change": in the technology tree the selected cell is the chosen thing and the control. The header of the tree names what is chosen and its price.
- Exception to "keys 1, 2, 3": the cells of the technology tree have no keys. There are too many. Enter and Esc act as everywhere.

## Movement

The owner chose this design on 2026-10-10, from three that were compared.

- The movement is staged on the map. A system that ships can leave opens its fleet there, in a sheet: one token for each ship. A tap moves a ship or takes it back. The panel says what leaves each system; it has no controls.
- A ship that moves is one row: its token, then its own hold, one slot for each unit. The game rolls for each ship at a gravity rift, so the player must see what each ship carries. Ships of a kind that stay are tokens in one row.
- What the ships of a system can load is one row of the sheet, a chip for each kind of unit with the count that is left. The chips are grouped by place, and the place is text before its chips ("Space area", "Planet Jord", "#23 Planet Vefut"). One chip is chosen (▸). A tap on an empty slot loads that kind; a tap on a used slot unloads. "Fill" loads the chosen kind on every ship that leaves the system, until the holds are full or none is left. A chip is the icon of the unit and the count; the name is its tooltip. The heading and this row stay in view while the ships scroll.
- When the ships of a system can load at more than one place, the row of a ship says in text where its cargo is from ("Planet Jord 2 · Space area 1").
- A pickup on the way has one sign, the bent arrow: on its chip, after the number of the system, and on the slot that holds the unit. A slot of a ship that does not pass the system cannot take it.
- Gravity Drive is a toggle on the row of a ship, where it changes what the ship does: it spares the ship a gravity rift, or the ship cannot move without it. One ship of the action has it; the toggle of another ship names that ship. The game chooses the path, and the row shows what the path means ("⚄ Rift roll").
- A system that the player is done with has a check mark: the movement takes something from it, or the player marked it. The "✓" of the sheet closes the sheet and marks the system; the sheet has no "×". A tap on the system closes the sheet with no mark. The mark of the player is kept in the page only, until the movement is sent: a reload loses it. The panel says "✓ Nothing leaves" for a marked system with nothing staged. The count of the player's ships shows "now → after" in the standard view.
- The paths of the ships are drawn for the open system only, with the marks of that path (rift roll, Gravity Drive). The game chooses the path. The map shows no cargo labels.
- Exception to "a choice is staged, then sent": a draft records every change of the movement at once, and the later steps are checked again. The same holds for the landings and the production of a draft. The step has no "Reset selection". Its main button sends nothing: it records a step with nothing staged ("Move nothing", "Land nothing", "Produce nothing"), or goes on to the step that the game comes to ("Go to invasion", "Go to production"). When no step follows, there is no button. The reason: a draft is private, every change is one step of undo, and the step tabs go on. Live keeps "Move fleet", because that sends to the game.
- The active system opens the list of what is committed to it: the ships that were there, then the ships that move, by the system that they leave, each with its hold. It is read-only; a system in the list opens its sheet.
- **The odds of the space combat** (owner, 2026-10-14) are shown where the movement shows what arrives: a row of the card "Arrives in …" of the panel, and the "Arrives" block of the inspector of the active system (`SpaceOdds`). They are there when another seat has ships in the active system and a ship moves in. They have only "Space combat · ‹defender›", Win, Draw and Loss of the moving seat in whole percentages, and one line for each side with the expected survivors by kind against the ships that fight ("1.8 / 2"; the name of the kind on hover). The rollouts and what the calculation counts and leaves out are behind a `Hint`. While the answer for a new movement is on its way, the last numbers are dimmed and the line has "…". `ti4-calc` computes them in the page (`ti4_space_odds` of the engine of the page, also in an online game), from the sides that the engine gives (`MovementFacts.battle`) and the staged ships (`session/movementOdds.ts`), on a fixed seed: the numbers do not change while the movement does not. A ground combat and the activation have no odds, and action cards are not counted.
- The demo data follows the game: before a movement, ships of two players are never in one system. Ground forces of two players can be on different planets of one system.
- The sheet has a small "Reset": it takes back what leaves that system, and its check mark, also one that the player set.
- The read-only view of a movement (a recorded step in Live, History) is the same tokens in the panel, one band for each system.

## Production

The owner chose this design on 2026-10-11: the build first, then the payment, both on the map.

- The step has two stages. Nothing is sent before the last button.
- **Build.** The units are staged on the map, in one sheet at the system that produces. It opens when the step opens. The panel says what is produced in each place and has the limits; it has no controls. A row of it opens the sheet.
- Every kind of unit of a production is one row (owner, 2026-10-11; a row of chips with one chosen kind and a row with all printed values were tried, and were hard to read): the icon, the name, the cost ("2", "1 for 2"), how many are left in the supply, the units that are staged, and the tokens. The columns have fixed widths, so cost and left line up over the rows; each group heading says "Cost" and "Left" once.
- The printed values are behind the name (`Peek`): hover shows them, a click or a tap keeps them open, and the card opens above the name when there is no room below. The name has a dotted underline on both layouts. The card is laid out as the printed unit card: the icon and the name, the four values in one row (cost, combat, move, capacity; the number over its word), the keywords with their values, one line each in capitals, and the printed special rule of the unit (`rules`). A section with nothing is not shown. The card has no control, and a keyword is not explained (the Text section).
- A dice value is read as the game says it: "5+", and "3+ (x3)" with more than one die; the same for BOMBARDMENT, ANTI-FIGHTER BARRAGE and SPACE CANNON. PRODUCTION has the printed value ("PRODUCTION +2").
- The rows are in two groups. "Ships · Space area" says the place once. "Ground forces" says its place in the heading when there is one place only ("Planet Jord"); with several places each unit has one line for each ("Planet Lisis"), under a row with the total.
- What is staged is the count of units (zero is dimmed, more than zero in the accent colour), then one token for each purchase, with the icon of every unit that it makes: a pair of fighters is a token with two icons. A tap on a token takes that purchase back. The dashed slot adds one purchase and shows the units that it makes. It is not shown when nothing more can be added. One step is one purchase. Fighters and infantry come two at a time, as the game sells them; the last pair makes one unit when the production limit has room for one only.
- A unit that cannot be produced in this use keeps its row. It is dimmed and has a word in place of the tokens ("No technology", "None left", "Blockaded", "No place"), with the reason in full as tooltip.
- The limits are gauges of the sheet and of the panel: "Production capacity", "Resources", "Fleet supply after", "Fighters and cargo in space". The heading of the sheet says what is staged and what it costs after a discount ("5 units · 3 resources · discount 1").
- The limits are soft, except the supply of a unit (the plastic): the player plans against them. A build over a soft limit is staged. The gauge is red, the footer says what is over ("2 resources short · 1 unit over the production limit"), and the main button and the "✓" of the sheet wait (pattern 5). The soft limits are the resources (the cost of the staged build against what the ready planets and the trade goods of the seat pay), the production limit, the fleet supply and the capacity. A purchase after the production limit makes all its units. The supply of a unit is hard: "Left" counts down, and when none is left the slot is not shown.
- "Fighters and cargo in space" has both sides after the build: a staged fighter uses capacity, and a staged ship adds its own.
- No planet pays while the build is chosen, and there is no payment bar. The main button says what is next: "Pay 3 resources", "Produce units" when the build costs nothing, "Produce nothing" when nothing is staged. The "✓" of the sheet does what "Pay …" does. "Reset" takes the build back.
- **Pay.** The sheet closes and the map is the payment (the Payment section): the bar, the planets that pay, Auto-pay. The footer has the payment line and "Produce units", which is disabled while the payment is short. "Back to build" (Esc) or a tap on the system that produces opens the sheet again; the staged payment stays and is measured against the new cost.
- Exception to "a tap on a planet of the active system acts on the open sheet" (the Invasion section): in a production a planet always pays. The reason: every ready planet pays, also the planets of the system that produces.
- On a phone the open sheet has the room of the footer, as the fleet of a system has. The payment has the whole map pane, with the footer under it.
- Every map view shows the ships and the ground forces of the system after the staged build.
- What the game asks before the production (AI Development Algorithm) is a decision in the list.

## Tests

- `npm test` runs two kinds of test (the projects in `vite.config.ts`). A `*.test.ts` has no DOM and runs in node: the view models, the drafts, the real engine. A `*.test.tsx` renders components in jsdom with `@testing-library/react`.
- A component with logic of its own (open and close, keys, a place on the screen) gets a component test next to it (`src/ui/overlays.test.tsx`). jsdom has no layout: a test of a position gives the rectangle of the trigger.
- A screen is tested over an update that the engine recorded (`src/session/fixtures/`), not over data written by hand, so the test sees what the engine sends. Such a test imports the session and the components, so it is in `src/dev/` (`src/dev/productionSheet.test.tsx`; the layers are in `src/boundaries.test.ts`). A fixture is recorded again with its command when the facts change; it is not edited.
- The page takes its facts from `src/session/ti4.wasm`, which is not in the repository. After a change of a fact in `ti4-view`, build it again (`scripts/build-wasm.sh`, or `scripts/build-wasm.sh --dev` while you work: see "Rust builds"); `wasmTransport.test.ts` checks the shape that the screens read.
- The e2e has three suites (owner, 2026-10-13). Each uses one worker (`playwright.config.ts`), because they also run on a machine with little memory, about half as fast as a desktop.
  - `npm run e2e` plays the real game: the engine in the page and the server (`e2e/local.spec.ts`, `local.phone.spec.ts`, `lobby.spec.ts`, `lobby.phone.spec.ts`, `online.spec.ts`, `factions.spec.ts`, `entry.spec.ts`). Run it for a change. A test has 5 seconds.
  - A test of a flow that cannot be shorter (a whole tactical action, the invasion, the production, a draft) has 10 seconds: `test.setTimeout(10_000)` in its first line. It is the only timeout that a test sets, and an `expect` gets no longer one. A new test starts where its screen is (a scenario, a call of the lobby), not at round 1 with many answers, so that it needs none.
  - `npm run e2e:slow` has the tests with `@slow` at the end of the title: the ones that play a game through (a game of eight through the decision list, on both layouts; a reload and the undo of twenty answers). A test has 30 seconds. **Never run them for each commit.** Read the titles (`grep -n "@slow" e2e/*.ts`) and run only the one that the change needs (`npm run e2e:slow -- -g "a reload"`): a change of the decision list, of the saved game, of the undo. The whole of it is run now and then.
  - `npm run shots` opens only the demo (`?example=…`, the code in `src/mock/`): `e2e/shots.spec.ts` and `e2e/phone.spec.ts`. It does not start the engine or the server, so it says nothing about the real game. Do not run it for each change. Run it after a change of a shared layout (the shell, the step panel, the theme, a primitive of `ui/`) or of a screen that only the demo has, and now and then for the two overflow rules.
  - The mock is removed step by step. When an example is removed, its shot is removed with it, and its two layout checks (no vertical scroll of the open step at 1920×1080; nothing cut at the side on the phone) move to the test of the real screen in `e2e/local.spec.ts` and `e2e/local.phone.spec.ts`. Do not add a new test to the shots for a screen that the engine has.
- **To look at a screen, take only the screenshots that you need.** Do not run a whole suite for this. The files go to `shots/`, which is not in the repository.
  - A state inside a flow of the real game: run the one test that comes to it, and read its file. Example: `npm run e2e -- -g "a production: the build"` writes `shots/local/production-pay.png`. If the test has no screenshot at that state, add a `page.screenshot` line there.
  - A screen of the demo: `npm run shots -- -g live-combat` (desktop and phone).
  - The first screen of an address, with no test: start `npm run dev`, then `npx playwright screenshot --viewport-size=1920,1080 --wait-for-selector='#step-panel' 'http://127.0.0.1:<port>/?scenario=late-game&humans=1' <file>.png`. For the phone use `--viewport-size=412,923`.
- A test of a game reads the board where the game decides it: the server draws the faction of a seat and the speaker, and the other seats decide by the seed. Do not write "Sol at system 01" or a count of systems into a test of a game on the server; a local game on a seed is the same until the engine changes, and then the script of the test is brought up to date, not its meaning.
- The tests of a local game (`e2e/local.spec.ts`) collect the errors of the page and expect none. A new part of a screen that opens (a card, a sheet, a menu) is opened once there.
- What a `Transport` promises is tested once for both transports (`src/session/transportContract.test.ts`): the engine in the page, and a real server. The server is `target/debug/server`; build it with `cargo b-web` after a change of the server, or the suite tests the old one (without it the server cases are skipped). A case that only one transport has (the steps, how far an undo goes) is not in this suite.
- `e2e/factions.spec.ts` starts three tables of eight on a real server, through the calls of the lobby: the 24 factions that a lobby can seat, and the three sets of strategy cards. It is the test that no screen assumes "the first seat is Sol" and that no screen shows an id. A new place that names a seat or a strategy card takes the name from the catalogue (`factionName`, `strategyCard` in `session/select/names.ts`), never from the id.

- `e2e/online.spec.ts` plays a game on a real server. `playwright.config.ts` builds it with `cargo b-web` and starts `target/debug/server`, so the first run of the e2e builds the server.

## Rust builds

- Work in `web2/` uses eight crates: `ti4-model`, `ti4-content`, `ti4-calc`, `ti4-engine`, `ti4-view`, `ti4-rule-bot`, `ti4-server`, `ti4-wasm`. - Use the aliases, and no other set of packages (owner, 2026-10-13). `cargo l-web`, `cargo t-web` and `cargo b-web` share one build of the engine (`cargo c-web` is kept only for old references; do not use it). A command for one crate (`cargo test -p ti4-engine`, `cargo build -p ti4-server`, `cargo run -p ti4-server`) gets other features of the dependencies and builds the engine and every crate above it again: 100 seconds in place of 10 on two cores, and more of the disk.
- The tests have two tiers. `cargo t-web` is the fast one: it leaves out every test whose name starts with `slow_` (under a minute on two cores after a build). `cargo t-web-slow` runs only those: the games that are played through on the server and in the wasm host. It builds the game crates again with some optimization, so it takes minutes; run it before a push, and after a change of the engine, the bots or the replay, not for each change. A bare `cargo test` runs both tiers.
- A new test that takes 3 seconds or more in a debug build gets the name `slow_…`. Keep one fast test of the same path when you can.
- Narrow a run with a filter after the alias: `cargo t-web undo::` for one file of the server integration tests (they are one binary), `cargo t-web an_unknown_preset` for one test. `cargo t-web --no-run` does not work (the alias ends with the arguments of the tests); `cargo l-web` checks that the tests compile.
- `scripts/build-wasm.sh` is the release build of the engine for the page: small and fast, and a change of the engine or of `ti4-view` builds the engine from the start (30 seconds on a desktop, minutes on a small machine). `scripts/build-wasm.sh --dev` is not optimized and incremental: about 3 seconds after a change. The file is three times the size, `npm test` takes three times as long with it, and the tests of a local game take about 30% longer, which is over the 5 seconds of a test on a slow machine. So: `--dev` to look at the page, the release build before `npm test`, the e2e and a deploy.
- Do not build or test another crate for this work, and do not use `--workspace`. The other crates (`ti4-policy`, `ti4-sim`, `ti4-training`, the libtorch crates, and the rest) are slow to test and fill the target directory. They are fixed later.
- A fact that the UI needs is added in `ti4-view`, not in `ti4-engine`, when that takes little code: a change of the engine can break replays. Change the engine when the fact obviously belongs there, or when `ti4-view` would need much code or would repeat a rule. Such a change must not alter what the game does (owner, 2026-10-10).
- Look at the machine before the first build (`free -g`, `nproc`). With less than 4 GB of memory, run one build or one suite at a time, and do not start one in the background while another runs: `cargo l-web`, `cargo t-web`, `scripts/build-wasm.sh`, `npm test` and Playwright (`--workers=1`, one project at a time) each alone. Two at once ran a machine of 3 GB out of memory on 2026-10-12, and the container was restarted.
- Before you commit Rust changes, all three must be clean: `cargo fmt --all -- --check`, `cargo l-web` (Clippy, no warnings), and `cargo t-web`. `cargo l-web` also checks that everything compiles, so `cargo c-web` is never needed: use `cargo l-web` for every check, also while you work.
- The gates, by what changed:
  - While you work on Rust: `cargo l-web`, `cargo t-web <filter>`.
  - Before a commit of Rust: the three above. When `ti4-view` or `ti4-wasm` changed, also `scripts/build-wasm.sh` and `npm test`. When the server changed, also `cargo b-web` and `npm test` (the transport suite).
  - Before a push of Rust, or nightly: `cargo t-web-slow`.
  - A change of `web2/` only: `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm test` (seconds), then `npm run e2e -- -g "<the screen>"`; the whole `npm run e2e` before the commit. `npm run e2e:slow` and `npm run shots` as the section "Tests" says.
- The build of the engine with its tests needs 2.6 GB for one compiler, and the engine without tests 1.5 GB at the same time. On a machine of 4 GB, build with one job the first time (`cargo t-web -j1`).

## Online play

- `/` is the start screen (`src/dev/StartApp.tsx`): two rows, "Single player" and "Multiplayer", each the control, with the keys 1 and 2, and the name of the player (`ti4.nickname`). A row is a page load. "Multiplayer" loads `?online`, "Single player" loads `?single` (the Lobby section, "Single player").
- `?online` makes a table on the server and goes to `?online=<game_id>&seat=<player_id>`; that address is the table, its game, and the player of the tab (`src/dev/OnlineApp.tsx`). While the table is a lobby the page is the lobby (the Lobby section); when the table says that the game runs, the page is the game. The server draws the faction of every seat and the speaker from the seed, and plays the strategy cards of Thunder's Edge (`te`) when the table names no set: the page is any faction, and it is not always asked first. The plans are `plans/2026-10-11-ONLINE_MULTIPLAYER_PLAN.md` and `plans/2026-10-12-LOBBY_PLAN.md`.
- The address that makes a table can name its seats, its seed, the nickname, the map and the wait of the seats that the server plays: `?online&players=6&seed=3&name=Ada&map=random&wait=1` (`src/dev/onlineStart.ts`). `map` is a map of the server by its alias, or "random"; `wait` is in seconds, 0 to 30. Without `players` the table has the seats that the host of this browser chose last, else four; without `name`, the name of the start screen. No page has a control for the map yet. The address of the table has none of it, so a reload is the table and makes no other one.
- The quick start is `?online&fill=random`: a game of eight, the page has one seat, the other seats decide at random, and the game starts at once. The row "Multiplayer" of the Dev sheet of a local game and the e2e use it. With `&wait=1` the other seats take a second for each answer.
- While a seat that the server plays takes its time, the server says who the game waits for and nothing else (`turn_status`). The transport tells it as a `status` event, not as an update, so what is staged stays; the toolbar says "Waiting for Rule bot 1" (the name of the player of the seat), and "Waiting for reactions" while a reaction window is open (it names no seat: who can react is private).
- The way out of a table or its game is the header link "Start", to `/` (on a phone the last item of "Reference and more" in the game). A local game keeps "Demo".
- A reload goes on as the player of the address. A game that the server does not have (it was deleted, or the address is wrong) is said in place of the game: "The server no longer knows this game.", with "Start" in the header. The page does not try again. A server that cannot be reached is not such a game: the page keeps trying.
- The server is at the address of the page: `/api` and `/ws`, through the proxy of `vite.config.ts` in development (the port is `TI4_E2E_BACKEND_PORT`, 8080 by default). Start it with `cargo b-web && target/debug/server`.
- The page is the same code as in a local game: `src/session/wsTransport.ts` is a `Transport`. Nothing in `game/`, `ui/` or `useLiveSession` asks which transport it has. A game on the server has no capability, so it has no Dev sheet.
- Undo online is the undo of the seat. The button is shown exactly when the server says that the seat may take its last decision back (`can_undo` of the update): the decision is its own, the game waits for the seat, and since the decision nothing hidden was shown (a card that was drawn, an exploration, a hand that was looked at), nothing was rolled or shuffled, and no other seat decided. So it does not go back past such a point, and a local game does (it is a tool for testing). One undo takes back what one undo of a local game takes back: one answer, or a movement, the landings or a purchase whole, staged again as it was sent. A window that is declined for a Never card goes with the answer before it. The server plays the game again without the decision and closes the socket as replaced; that is not shown as offline. An undo that the server refuses is a toast that names the barrier. The undo of the host (the event log, restore, redo) is not in web2.
- A lost connection has one signal (pattern 6): the status of the toolbar says "Offline · reconnecting". The game stays in view, no choice is open, and the page tries again by itself. It is not an error. The panel says "The game cannot be reached." in the place where it says who the game waits for.
- An answer that the server did not take is a toast with the reason, and the same choice is open again. It is never silent.
- The printed cards are those of the engine of the page, also online. The page refuses a server with another corpus or another protocol, with both versions in the text.
- **A seat for each tab.** `localStorage` is shared by all tabs, so the address says which player a tab is (`&seat=<player_id>`; the id is public, the credential is not in the address). The browser keeps every player that it has in a game (`ti4.online.<game_id>.players`: the id and its player session), so two tabs of one browser can be two seats. A tab without `seat` is the one player of this browser in the game; with none it is a visitor; with several the lobby marks them "(yours)" and a row makes the tab that player. The key of an older page (`ti4.online.<game_id>.session`) is moved to its player once. The Never cards are kept by the server.
- **What a seat staged is kept by the server** (owner, 2026-10-14; `plans/2026-10-14-SAME_SEAT_TABS_AND_ONE_DOMAIN_PLAN.md`, phase 2): the staged movement, landings and production, the private draft of a tactical action with its undo, and the draft of a secondary. It is the same in every tab and on every device of the seat, and a change in one tab is staged in the others at once. What is open on the screen is of each tab: the workspace (Live or Draft), the step of the draft in view, a sheet, the pane, the marks that the player set by hand. `SeatStore` (`src/session/seatStore.ts`) is the seam: `Transport.staged` online (`seat_staged` and `put_staged` of the protocol; the transport waits a moment for the next change, has one write of a slot on its way at a time, and takes a newer value of the server over its own), `localSeatStore` over `localStorage` in a local game and in single player. What an older page kept in `localStorage` (`onlineKeys`) is put once, when the server has nothing for the seat, and then removed. A draft that is larger than a slot of the server (48 KiB) loses its oldest undo states first.
- **One sender for the answers of a seat** (phase 3 of the plan). Two tabs of one seat can both answer the same question; the server takes the first, and tells the other `answered_elsewhere`. That is no error: the transport shows no toast and does not ask for the game again, and the update that follows shows the answer. (A page that did not ask for the staged work, web, is told `stale_nonce`.)
- **A ready draft of a secondary is sent by the server**, once for every tab and device of the seat, also when none is open: when the game asks the seat, and when the seat marks the draft ready while the game asks it. The server checks it as the page did (`run_draft` with the scope, played whole), and the seat is not asked. A draft that is sent is cleared; one that no longer fits is written back not ready, by the server, and every tab says "Your draft no longer fits the game. It is not ready." (`SeatStore.subscribe` tells a write of the server apart). The page sends no ready draft online and does not hold the question back: when the game asks the seat, the server did not send. A local game keeps the sending of the page. "Send draft" of a draft that is not ready is still sent by the page.

- `src/session/` turns an update of the game into the view models. It imports `model` only, and it decides no rule: a value that needs a rule comes from the engine.
- The page does not know where the game runs. `Transport` (`src/session/transport.ts`) is the seam: an update, an answer, a script that is run, a plan or a draft that is sent, the mode of a card, an undo. A tool for testing a local game (Step, "Play as", the scenarios, the saved game) is a capability of the transport (`Transport.capabilities`, `GameSession.capabilities`): the Dev entry and each part of the Dev sheet are shown from it, never from the kind of game. How a plan answers a question of the game is decided in Rust (`script_answer` in `ti4-view`); the wasm host has an export for it (`ti4_plan`).
- A decision without a dedicated screen is one list in the action panel.
- The activation and the movement of a tactical action use the screens of these steps. In the movement the systems that ships can leave are the choice on the board, as in the demo, so a phone stays on the map. The facts come from the engine with the update (`tactical`), and "Move fleet" sends the staged movement as one plan (`Transport.submitPlan`). A plan that the game refuses is taken back whole; one undo takes a movement back whole, and it is staged again as it was sent, so the player can change a part of it. The check marks that the player set by hand are not kept.
- The landings of an invasion use the screen of that step too (the Invasion section). The facts are the third kind of `tactical`, and "Commit ground forces" sends the staged landings as one plan, with the rules of a movement plan: refused whole, stopped by another decision with the rest staged again, and taken back whole by one undo. The staged landings are kept for a reload as the staged movement is. After the last unit the game does not ask for the end of the landings, so the plan needs no answer for it.
- The production uses the screen of that step too (the Production section). The facts are the fourth kind of `tactical` (`ProductionFacts`): every unit of a production with its price, its places, its supply and its printed values, and why the seat cannot produce it when it cannot, the limits, the discount, and the planets and trade goods that pay. "Produce units" sends the build and its payment as one plan, with the rules of a movement plan. The game asks for a payment after each purchase; the plan answers with the staged payments in their order, whenever it is asked, and leaves out what the game did not need. A build whose payment does not cover it is refused whole. The staged production is kept for a reload.
- The limits of a staged production are sums over the facts: the fleet pool and the capacity do not count what a rule excuses (fighters at a space dock, Fighter II). The game has the last word when the plan is sent.
- The Draft tab opens the private draft of a tactical action: its activation, its movement, its landings and its production, on the screens of these steps (pattern 10). A draft is a script (`src/session/draft.ts`): the tactical action, its system, then the steps of its movement, of its landings and of its production. The engine plays the script as the next turn of the seat on a copy of the game, each time the draft or the game changes (`Transport.draft`), and the screens are made from where that run ended. The answer comes later than the question (a `draft` event), also in a local game: until it is there, the screen is of the draft before and its main button waits. A change of the game alone (another seat acted) does not make it wait: the screen and what is staged stay, and the answer for the new game replaces it. What is staged is cleared only by a new question of the viewer (`fresh` in `useLiveSession.ts`), in Live and in both drafts. Nothing of it is in the game or in its saved decisions.
- The draft opens whenever the page waits in the strategy phase or the action phase and the seat has not passed; the tab says why when it does not. In the strategy phase it is the draft of the turn that comes (owner, 2026-10-10). The action menu stays a list: a tactical action chosen there is played live.
- In the draft, "Activate system" records the system, and every change of the movement is recorded at once (the Movement section). Undo and Redo of the toolbar go through the states of the draft; "Start over" and "Discard draft" are in its menu. The draft and its history are kept (`SeatStore`, slot "plan": `planKey` in `localStorage` in a local game, the server online), so a reload keeps them.
- "Apply to Live" is enabled at the turn menu of the seat, when the game played the whole draft. Its dialog says what is sent. It sends the action, the system and the movement as one request (`Transport.applyDraft`): refused whole, or stopped by another decision, as a movement plan is. The draft is then gone.
- A draft that the game no longer plays (the system got a command token, a ship is gone) keeps its script: the screen says which part does not fit, and Apply is disabled.
- The draft goes on as far as the game is certain: from the movement to the landings and to the production. A step that is not drafted is decided in Live. A step in view stays in view while it is changed; its main button goes on. The tabs open every step that the game came to with the draft. A change of an earlier step keeps the later steps, and the game checks them again.
- The draft ends where something random lies ahead: a roll (a gravity rift, space cannon, a combat), a card that is drawn (a landing on a planet that nobody explored), ships of another seat in the system. The footer says so. This ends the check of the draft, not the draft: Apply stays possible, and the game goes on in Live. Apply sends the steps that the game played; what the draft holds after that point is staged again in Live when its step opens, and nothing of it is sent.
- A production of a draft is in the draft when it is paid. Until then the build is kept, the footer says so, and the game is not asked for it.
- What an ability of the seat asks before a step (AI Development Algorithm) is a list in the draft too. Its main button is "Record": the answer is a part of the draft.
- The game asks "negotiate, or continue" between the steps of a tactical action when the seat has a neighbour. A plan that still has steps continues; so does a draft.
- The Dev sheet has the row "Multiplayer", after "Start from". It loads the address of the quick start, on the seed of the page (`?online&fill=random&seed=<seed>`; the Online play section). The row says the seed and the seats; what it does is behind "?". A page load, not a game choice: the local game stays saved under its own address.
- The Dev sheet has "Other seats": Run (the default) or Step. With Step the game waits before each decision of a seat that is not played here; the panel names the seat, and the main button "Step" lets it decide. The seats decide the same either way, so the saved game does not change. The setting is of the page (`STEPPING_KEY`), for testing: a draft can be opened while another seat has the turn. A reload plays the saved decisions again and then waits at the same step.
- The Dev sheet has "Play as": one row for each seat, with "✓ Playing" or "Random". A row of a random seat is the control: that seat is played here from now on, and the seat that the page was shown as decides at random, from its open decision on (`Transport.capabilities.switchSeat`). With a mask of several seats only the shown seat is given up. The staged movement and the private draft of the seat that is left are dropped. Undo does not go back before the change: to play the old seat again, choose it (owner, 2026-10-10). The changes are part of the saved game (`switches`: the number of the decision and the seats), so a reload, Export and Import keep them.
- In local play the game decides when a ship uses Gravity Drive: only when the ship cannot arrive without it. The toggle is then on and locked. The free toggle of the Movement section is not offered there.
- The draft of a secondary (the Strategy cards section) is a script too: the answers by their ids (`src/session/secondaryDraft.ts`). The engine plays it with the scope of the secondary (`Transport.draft(script, scope)`), and "ready" sends it with `Transport.applyDraft`. It is kept as the draft of a tactical action is (slot "secondary": `secondaryKey` in a local game, the server online). In a local game the page is shown the game only when a seat of the page is asked, so a secondary is drafted before the seat is reached only with "Other seats: Step", or with several seats played here. A reload goes on at the same decision of the game.
- The game is saved in `localStorage` as the server keeps a game (owner, 2026-10-13): its seed, and the option id of every decision of every seat, in order (`SavedGame.decisions`, format 3, `src/session/savedGame.ts`), with the table that the game was set up as and its wait. Export and Import keep all of it. A reload and an undo play it again from the seed: the engine is given each saved decision, whoever the seat is, so no bot decides again and no seat waits, and the game goes on where it was. The transport learns what the other seats decided from the engine (`ti4_journal`) and keeps the places of the page's own answers in memory (`own`): an undo cuts the list at the answer that it takes back, with what the other seats decided after it. The Dev sheet says both counts ("3 answers saved · 57 decisions of all seats").
- Formats 1 and 2 had the answers of the page only. They are not read: the page says so in place of the game, with "New game". A game that another build of the engine saved may not play again (the game asks another question, or offers other options): the page says that the save is of another engine before it plays it.
- The staged movement of the open movement step is saved too (`draftKey`), with the number of its choice. After a reload it is staged again, so its systems keep their check marks.
- The details, the measurements and the limits are in `crates/ti4-wasm/README.md`.
