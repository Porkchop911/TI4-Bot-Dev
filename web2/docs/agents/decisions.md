# web2: decisions, reaction windows, action picker

Part of `web2/AGENTS.md`: the Patterns and the rules of Screen size and Text there apply here too.

## Action picker and shortcuts

- There is no separate turn bar. The action picker is the content of the action panel when the viewer has the turn and no action is open.
- The picker is the turn menu of the engine (`kind: "turn_menu"` in the details of its question, `session/select/turn.ts`). It has fixed groups in a fixed order: Tactical (`t`), Strategic (one row for each strategy card, its number as key), Component (`c`, one row for every component action: the action cards and every other component), Trade (`r`), Pass (`p`). A row that cannot be used stays in its place and shows the reason, from the facts of the menu (the strategy card that is not used yet, the reason of a partner that cannot trade). An option that is none of the fixed rows is in the list of `c`, with the other components.
- The picker is one list. The whole row is the control: key, group, name, and the state at the right edge. Rows have no buttons of their own.
- A choice in the picker sends nothing, except a partner of a transaction. A tactical action opens as the private draft, at its activation; "Activate this system" in the inspector of a system does the same with that system staged. A strategic or component action and Pass are staged: the screen shows the staged action as printed, and the main button of the footer sends it ("Use Leadership", "Play War Effort"). A player in the list "Transaction" opens the transaction with that player at once: opening it is free (94.1a) and commits to nothing, and the engine then asks the transaction itself, so there is no staged screen; undo takes it back to the picker. "Back to actions" (Esc) goes one level back: from the draft to the picker, from a staged action to its list, from a list to the picker.
- The row of Component opens one list with the keys 1, 2, 3, in two groups: "Action cards" (the cards that can be played now), then "Other components"; the keys run on over both, and a group with nothing in it is left out. Each card with its printed name and text from the catalogue (an action card by the hand of the viewer, a leader, a technology, a relic, a faction ability, a legendary planet, an exploration card), and what its play changes when the engine says it (`action_card_effects`). The row of Trade opens the list of the other seats: their trade goods, commodities and promissory notes, or why they cannot trade now.
- **A transaction** is one screen, the editor `transaction` (`session/select/transaction.ts`), built from what the engine says each offer moves (`details.transaction`, the engine's `TransactionHints`), never from its labels or its ids. The heading is "Transaction with <seat>" for the proposer and "Answer <seat>’s offer" for the partner. The deal is the block `deal`: two cards side by side (one column on a phone), "You give" and "You get", each with the seat (its symbol and name) and what it holds now in the heading ("Trade goods 3 · Commodities 2"). An amount (trade goods, commodities, relic fragments of a trait) is a row with its counter, up to the most that an offer has; a card is a chip, in one row for each kind that names the kind once ("Promissory notes", "Action cards", "Secret objectives"), with its printed text behind "?". A staged chip has "✓" and the gold frame.
  - The proposer stages both sides. The staged deal is an offer of the engine, or it cannot be sent: the main button "Propose" sends the offer that is exactly the staged deal, and with nothing staged it is "Offer nothing" (the decline). A staged deal that no offer is says "No offer to <seat> is this deal." in the footer and blocks the button. Under the deal is the list of the offers that hold everything that is staged, the closest first, at most six with the keys 1 to 6 and a row "N offers more"; with nothing staged it is the offers in the order of the engine; when no offer holds the staged deal it is "the nearest offers" (three). A row stages its offer whole, on both sides. So every offer is reached by the counters and the chips, and by the list. Esc clears the deal.
  - The partner sees the deal on the table in the same two cards, read-only (an amount is its count), and the answers of the engine as a list with keys ("Accept", "Refuse", "Counter-offer"). The main button says the staged answer; nothing is sent before one is chosen.
  - A row of the list is "Give … for …" (`worded` in `session/select/notes.ts`), the seats named as the page names them (`seatLabel`). A note is its printed name from the catalogue (its record, `records.promissory_notes`), with the faction of its owner in brackets for a note that every faction has when the owner is not the viewer ("Trade Agreement (Nomad)"); an action card and a secret objective are their printed names; trade goods, commodities and relic fragments are counts. A note's printed text names the owner where it prints "<color>".
  - Not there yet, because the engine does not say it: a deal that is no listed offer (the engine takes one, `details.free_form`, but the page has neither the ids that it is written in nor what each seat may hand over), what each seat holds after the deal ("now → after": a commodity that changes hands becomes a trade good, a rule), and that a second counter-offer ends the negotiation.
- A text of the engine that names a seat, a promissory note or a card by its id (a note given with an ability, "{faction} becomes speaker", "Kyver: {seat} showed {card}") is worded from what the engine says it names (`details.phrases`, the engine's `PhraseHints`: the prompt and each option as a template with a typed subject for each `{key}`; `phraseWords` in `session/select/notes.ts`): a seat as the page calls it (`seatLabel`), a note and a card by its printed name, with their printed texts behind "?". web2 never reads ids out of a label: a text without phrases is shown as the engine says it.
- After the viewer's action (the engine's question "end your turn", `closing` in its details), the panel is "End your turn": the block "Before you end your turn" has the rows of the picker that the engine still offers (a transaction; with Fleet Logistics a second action), and the footer has "End turn".
- While another seat has the turn in the action phase there is no step sheet: whose turn it is and whom the game waits for is the status of the toolbar, and what the seat does is the live row of the Log, which opens its sheet (`log.md`). A local game shows the turn as it goes only with "Other seats: Step"; online it follows every step.
- A shortcut key is data: `ActionButtonView.key`. The button shows the key. Enter is the main button of the footer. Esc goes one level back.
- The picker is static: it has the same rows in every turn of a game. Only the number of strategy cards changes it, with the player count. The component actions have one row ("Play an action card or use a component", "3 ready", or "None is ready"). A second list asks which one, with the keys 1, 2, 3.
- A strategy card has its number as key.
- Browser back is not used for game choices. Esc goes one level back; a draft has undo and redo. Browser back was tried for the picker (2026-10-07) and removed: it could not apply to the other screens.
- Shortcuts do not act in a text field or while a dialog, a menu or a rule text is open. A control that the player reached with Tab keeps Enter.

## Decisions

A decision is any choice that a card, an ability or a rule asks for. Every decision uses the same parts.

- A decision appears where its cause is: as blocks in the open action. The panel does not switch to another screen, and there is no dialog.
- The source is on top: the card, ability or rule that asks, as printed (the `source` block): an exploration card that is drawn, or the action card, faction ability or component of `DecisionContext.source`, from the catalogue. A strategy card is the heading of its own screen and has no source block; a rule has none, because the catalogue does not print the rules. The heading says what the player must do.
- **An ability that asks in the middle of another action** (Scheming in a Politics or in the status phase, the agent of another seat in a strategic action, a card that was played in a window) is a window like a reaction window: the band on top of the action that it interrupts (`interrupt`, `selectAction`). The head of the band is the card in one line: its name, its kind, its printed text behind "?"; under it the question of the engine without the name of the card ("Choose and discard 1 action card from your hand"), then the choice. The action under the band is said in short (`interruptedAction`): the strategic action of its card, the tactical action of the active player, the turn, or the phase. A strategic action under a band is its list of seats with nothing open, the seats that wait in one line, "Then: …", as always (`strategy-cards.md`); these inform, so they are in the Log and the sheet has the band. It is any decision whose source is printed, but not the viewer's own component action, an agenda (the flow of its phase), an exploration card (the step that drew it), a research or an editor.
- The question of the viewer's own component action (its card, ability or component asks a target in the action phase) is "Component action": the heading is the card, its printed text is behind "?" of the heading, and the trail says that the card is played and what is chosen now.
- There are three shapes of choice. The thing that is chosen decides the shape:
  - on the board, when it is on the board: every option (but a decline) is a system (an activation, a system of a card, a system to recall from) or names a planet in its payload. The panel shows the choice in a block ("System" or "Planet", "Choose on the map"), not a list of candidates; a decline stays a row of a list;
  - in the player table, when it is a player: the engine names the seat of each option (`details.seats`). The table already shows the values of each player, and a row has what the choice gives that seat when the engine says it ("Commodities 0 → 6");
  - in a list, for everything else (answers, rewards, cards). Rows are the controls, with the keys 1, 2, 3 and the effect in game values at the right edge when the engine gives it.
  - **An action card in any list** (a discard, a card to give or to play, the hand of Scheming) has its printed text as a second line of its row, from the catalogue (`printedActionCard`): the engine names the card by its id, the alias of a copy, or by its printed name in the label.
- An amount is a counter row. A limit is a gauge above the rows: a unit that is removed over the fleet supply or the capacity has the gauge of that limit as the engine counts it; the engine asks for one unit at a time, so the rows are the units and not counters.
- A choice is staged, then sent. A staged choice shows "Selected" and "Not sent". The main button of the footer sends it. Esc clears the choice first, then leaves the action.
- A thing that cannot be chosen stays in view and shows the reason.
- Live and Draft use the same blocks.
- **The step sheet has the decision, the Log what informs** (owner, 2026-10-16). A block says whom it serves (`BlockRole`, set in `session/select`): what the choice needs stays in the sheet (the question, the source that asks with its printed text, the options, the costs, what is staged "now → after", the odds, the gauges of a limit, the row of the viewer with its editor); what happened or what the other seats do is not in the sheet: it is the Log and the sheet of a turn that a row of the Log opens (the rows of the other seats and "Then: …", the turn of another seat, a wait, a combat that the viewer watches, the steps of a tactical action that are resolved). The list by block and screen is in `log.md` ("Decide and inform"). A new block decides unless its selector marks it "inform".

## Reaction windows

The owner asked on 2026-10-13 for the printed text of every reaction and for a setting of every reaction.

- A reaction window is the `interrupt` of the action panel: a band on top of the action that it reacts to, which stays in view under it (`select/reaction.ts`). The band has no line of its own for the trigger: the heading and its context say it ("Hacan’s tactical action · Quann · #25"; "Mining Initiative · Action card · Hacan"). The trigger in game values is the name of the band ("Reaction · After Hacan’s tactical action in system 18", `triggerText`, the seats named as the page names them, `seatLabel`), never an id of the engine, and the context of a turn or a phase, which say nothing of it ("Agenda · After Minister of Industry is revealed").
- **The system of a window** is marked on the map while the window is open (`BoardView.marked`, a wide ring in the colour of the window): the system or the planet of the trigger, the target of the question, else the active system (`windowSystem`). The panel does not repeat it in a block. The band links it on hover.
- **On a phone a window is a decision on the map**: it is the band of the step sheet over the board (the heading of the action, then the band, then the action under it), with its system marked, and the footer under the pane. A window that opens is no choice of the viewer: it marks the tab Map, as every decision of the game does (the Phone rule of `AGENTS.md`).
- The action under the window comes from the trigger of the engine (`reactedAction`): a played or discarded action card is the frame "Action card" with the card as printed (the `source` block), and in a window "when" it is played the engine's sentence of what it will do when the engine has a preview of that copy, else "Not resolved yet."; a strategic action is its card, with the primary as printed; a tactical action is the seat's tactical action with its active system; any other trigger is the turn of the seat, or the phase. Nothing else of that action is shown: the engine does not say what the action will do.
- The window lists what the viewer can use now, one row each: an action card, a leader, a faction ability, a technology, a promissory note, a unit, a breakthrough. A row has the printed name, then at the right the kind and its setting, a small list with no frame until hover; what it would do now, when the engine words it (`EffectPreview.phrase`, `MenuRowView.effect`: game state, so it is visible), is a line under the name, with the cards that it names behind "?"; the printed timing and text are the next line in the whole width of the row; and the keys 1, 2, 3. No window has code of its own: the engine says what every option stands for (`reaction` of the update, `ti4-host/src/view/reaction.rs`, from the id of the option). Every window has this one shape (`interruptOf`): a window inside a space combat is the same band over the combat.
- The main button of the footer is Pass. A row stages its reaction; the main button then says "Play" and the card ("Play Sabotage"), or "Use" and the ability ("Use T'ro"), and Esc clears it. A slot of several action cards is one option of the engine, so its main button is "Play an action card". Enter always does what the main button says. The decline is no row.
- **The setting of a reaction** is in its row of the window, at the right edge of the line of the name, and on its card in a sheet (pattern 1: the window is where a reaction is muted; the sheet is where it is set back, because a muted window does not open). It is one list (`OfferSelect`): "Offered", "Only on my turns", "Never". It applies to the windows after it and leaves a toast. The window that is open stays open and is answered by the player: the list only sets, it never passes (owner, 2026-10-13). Exception to "a choice is staged, then sent": it is a setting of the seat and no decision of the game.
- "Only on my turns" is of the action phase: the reaction is passed while another seat is the active player. Outside the action phase nobody has the turn, the reaction is offered, and the list of a window there has "Offered" and "Never" only.
- The sheets: the Cards sheet for an action card of the hand; the sheet of the viewer for a leader, a faction ability with a timing and a technology that is used. A reaction that is not always offered and has no card there (a promissory note, a unit, a breakthrough) is a row of "Other reactions" in the sheet of the viewer. The sheet of another seat has no setting.
- A window that does not open because of the setting shows nothing: the viewer was not asked.
- The setting is applied where the game runs, by `ti4_host::view::reaction_modes::NeverOffer`: the engine of a local game and the server use the same wrapper. A reaction is set by its key (`ReactionOffer.key`): the printed name of an action card, so every copy is covered, or the kind and the id of the card of an ability ("leader:sardakkagent"), so an ability that hangs on several events has one setting. The page only tells the transport (`Transport.setReactionMode`) and shows what the transport says (`modes` of its events; its `passed` events are not shown). A window that offers only what the seat muted is declined without asking; a window with something else is asked without it. Only a window that the seat may decline is touched. In a local game that decline is an answer like any other, so the saved game has it, and a saved game is played again the same whatever is set now. An undo takes it back together with the answer of the player before it. The modes are kept next to the saved game (`ti4.save.<id>.cards`; a list of the names of the Never cards is read too), and by the server (`reaction_modes.json`).
- The engine says whose turn it is with the question (`details.active` of the choice, display only), so the filter needs no state.
- Not there yet: an ability that asks its own question outside a window of the timing resolver (some "you may" abilities) is a decision in the list, with no setting.
