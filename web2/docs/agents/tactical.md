# web2: movement, invasion, production, battle table

Part of `web2/AGENTS.md`: the Patterns and the rules of Screen size and Text there apply here too.

## Unit names

- A unit is named as its card prints it, from the catalogue (`Catalog.records.units`, `unitLabel` in `session/select/names.ts`): "Cruiser II", "PDS II", "Advanced Carrier I", "Hel-Titan I", "Z-Grav Eidolon". This holds on every screen that names a unit: the movement, the landings, the production, the rift rolls, the space cannon and battle tables, the odds, the review rows of a draft, the inspector and the sheets on the map, and the starting fleet of a player sheet. Only without the catalogue is a unit named by its base unit ("Cruiser").
- A printed name has no plural: "2 Fighter II".
- Units that are counted are grouped by the printed unit, so "2 Infantry I · 1 Infantry II" stays apart; without the catalogue by the base unit. The icon is that of the base unit.
- A name in a column of fixed width (the battle table, the production sheet) is cut with "…", with the full name on hover; on a phone it takes a second line.
- A structure that Construction places is named by its kind ("PDS", "Space dock"): the engine asks for a kind, not for a unit card.

## Activation

- The activation is the choice of a system on the map, with the facts of the system in the panel. An activation that places no command token (Thunder's Edge's Warfare, `costs_token: false` of `ActivationFacts`) is the same step: its card says "No command token" in place of "Costs 1 tactic token", the title of the frame names the card, and every system that the game offers is a target, also one with the seat's token.

- In a draft the recorded activation is read-only with "Edit activation" in the heading of the step. The edit opens the same choice on the map, with the recorded system chosen; the pill says "Editing", and "Cancel edits" or the tab of another step leaves it as it was. "Activate system" with another system replaces the activation and keeps the later steps: the game plays them again, and a part that no longer fits is a marked row of its step with Remove, its tab says "Needs review", and Apply is disabled (the drafts section of `local-play.md`). Undo takes the change back.

## Movement

The owner chose this design on 2026-10-10, from three that were compared.

- The movement is staged on the map. A system that ships can leave opens its fleet there, in a sheet: one token for each ship. A tap moves a ship or takes it back. The panel says what leaves each system; it has no controls.
- A ship that moves is one row: its token, then its own hold, one slot for each unit. The game rolls for each ship at a gravity rift, so the player must see what each ship carries. Ships of a kind that stay are tokens in one row.
- What the ships of a system can load is one row of the sheet, a chip for each kind of unit with the count that is left. The chips are grouped by place, and the place is text before its chips ("Space area", "Planet Jord", "#23 Planet Vefut"). One chip is chosen (▸). A tap on an empty slot loads that kind; a tap on a used slot unloads. "Fill" loads the chosen kind on every ship that leaves the system, until the holds are full or none is left. A chip is the icon of the unit and the count; the name is its tooltip. The heading and this row stay in view while the ships scroll.
- When the ships of a system can load at more than one place, the row of a ship says in text where its cargo is from ("Planet Jord 2 · Space area 1").
- A pickup on the way has one sign, the bent arrow: on its chip, after the number of the system, and on the slot that holds the unit. A slot of a ship that does not pass the system cannot take it.
- Gravity Drive is a toggle on the row of a ship, where it changes what the ship does: it spares the ship a gravity rift, or the ship cannot move without it. One ship of the action has it; the toggle of another ship names that ship. The game chooses the path, and the row shows what the path means ("⚄ Rift roll").
- A system that the player is done with has a check mark: the movement takes something from it, or the player marked it. The "✓" of the sheet closes the sheet and marks the system; the sheet has no "×". A tap on the system closes the sheet with no mark. The mark of the player is kept in the page only, until the movement is sent: a reload loses it. The panel says "✓ Nothing leaves" for a marked system with nothing staged. The count of the player's ships shows "now → after" in the standard view.
- The paths of the ships are drawn for the open system only, with the marks of that path (rift roll, Gravity Drive). The game chooses the path. The map shows no cargo labels. Hovering the row of a kind lights the paths of its ships; hovering the row of one ship lights its own path (with Gravity Drive it can be another).
- Exception to "a choice is staged, then sent": a draft records every change of the movement at once, and the later steps are checked again. The same holds for the landings and the production of a draft. The step has no "Reset selection". Its main button sends nothing: it records a step with nothing staged ("Move nothing", "Land nothing", "Produce nothing"), or goes on to the step that the game comes to ("Go to invasion", "Go to production"). When no step follows, there is no button. The reason: a draft is private, every change is one step of undo, and the step tabs go on. Live keeps "Move fleet", because that sends to the game.
- **What no ship can load** comes from the engine (`MovementFacts.unloadable`): the fighters and ground forces in a system that a ship of the movement starts in or passes, outside the active system, that no ship is offered, with the reason (a command token of the seat there, the ships there pass other players' ships with Myru Vos and carry nothing, or no ship with capacity starts or passes there). The engine decides both from one set of checks (`ti4_engine::transit::unloadable`, next to the load offers of `CargoWindow::for_ship`); the host only maps it. In a system that ships can leave it is the "⚠ Stay: …" line of its card and of its sheet; in another system it is a row of "Cannot move to …" and the cargo sign on the map. The movement is allowed: the units stay, so the main button is not blocked. The units that a staged departure leaves without capacity in its system (removed at the end of the turn, 16.3) are not said: the staging is in the page, and web2 does not count capacity.
- The path text of a ship names the wormholes that it goes through ("via Beta Wormhole · #40 · β wormhole", `MoveFact.wormholes`). A damaged ship is its own kind in the sheet ("Dreadnought (damaged)").
- The active system opens the list of what is committed to it: the ships that were there, then the ships that move, by the system that they leave, each with its hold. It is read-only; a system in the list opens its sheet.
- **The odds of the space combat** (owner, 2026-10-14) are shown where the movement shows what arrives: a row of the card "Arrives in …" of the panel, and the "Arrives" block of the inspector of the active system (`SpaceOdds`). They are there when another seat has ships in the active system and a ship moves in. They have only "Space combat · ‹defender›", Win, Draw and Loss of the moving seat in whole percentages, and one line for each side with the expected survivors by kind against the ships that fight ("1.8 / 2"; the name of the kind on hover). Draw: both fleets are destroyed, or the combat does not end (the four rates of `ti4-calc` are exclusive; Draw is `mutual_destruction_rate + unresolved_rate`). The rollouts and what the calculation counts and leaves out are behind a `Hint`. While the answer for a new movement is on its way, the last numbers are dimmed and the line has "…". `ti4-calc` computes them in the page (`spaceOdds` of `web-shared/src/engine/odds.ts`, the query `space_odds` of the engine of the page: the Worker of a local game, an instance on the main thread in an online game), from the sides that the engine gives (`MovementFacts.battle`) and the staged ships (`session/movementOdds.ts`), on a fixed seed: the numbers do not change while the movement does not. The activation has no odds, and action cards are not counted. The ground combat has its own odds (the Invasion section).
- The sheet has a small "Reset": it takes back what leaves that system, and its check mark, also one that the player set.
- **The dice of the movement** are read from the public rolls of the action (`GameEvent.roll`, kept by the transport: `session/rolls.ts`). A roll of a rift or of a space cannon names its unit (`PublicRoll.subject`, a `RollSubject`: the `unit`, `damaged`, the `origin` and `cargo` of a ship, the `target` seat of a gun). "Gravity rift" has a row for each ship that left a rift: the ship, "Leaves ‹rift› · from ‹origin› · carries 2 Infantry I · survives on 4+", the die and "Survives" or "Destroyed"; a destroyed ship and its cargo are "Removed after movement". "Space cannon offense" is a battle table: a side for each seat that fired, with a row for each gun (PDS II, 6+, its dice), and a side for each seat that was fired at, with its ships in the active system. They are in the open movement once rolled (an applied draft that a rift roll stopped). In Live a resolved step is no tab that opens: its result (the activation, the movement with its dice) is in the live turn of the Log (`log.md`, "Decide and inform"); the tab says "in the Log" on hover. A draft opens its recorded activation from its tab. A draft holds no dice: the step where its check ends has the pill "Preview ends here".
- **The space cannon offense before the roll** comes from the engine (`MovementFacts.cannon`, from `space_cannon_shots` of the engine): every gun of every seat that fires in the active system after the movement, also a gun on a planet of a system with no ships and one that reaches it from a neighbour (PDS II), with its dice and what it hits on. The open movement shows them as the same table with empty dice and the expected hits ("≈1.0"), the ships of the viewer after the staged movement as the side that is fired at. When another seat has ships there the table is left out: the odds of the space combat count the guns.
- The heading of the movement has its parts (the trail): "Move ships", "Gravity rift" when a ship rolls for one, and "Space cannon offense": to come when the facts have guns, "Space cannon offense · no guns" (skipped) when they have none, a check mark when it was rolled. After the movement no facts are left: the part is there only when it rolled.

## Space combat

- A space combat is the step "Space combat" of its tactical action (`BoardView.combat`, `session/select/combat.ts`). Every seat has this screen: the seats that fight, and a seat that does not fight, which watches it read-only (pattern 11). A draft has no combat: it ends before it.
- The tab of the step says the round ("Round 2"). The steps before it are read in the live turn of the Log. A combat that the viewer watches informs: it is in the Log, and there is no step sheet; a combat in which the viewer is asked is the step sheet, with the table of its round and the question.
- The step has one battle table, of the round as the engine started it (`CombatView.round_start`): the attacker and the defender, each printed unit with its count, what it hits on, and "1 damaged" for the damaged ones. The header of the table has the round and its stage ("Before the roll", "Anti-fighter barrage", "Hits to assign", "Retreat"), and once someone owes hits, "3 hits for … to assign".
- Before the roll a row has the dice that the units roll and the hits that they score on average ("≈0.6"), and the value is the printed one. After the roll the value is what the dice of the engine needed, in the accent colour with the printed value on hover when it differs, and the hits of each side are in its header. The engine rolls the ships of one value together, so the dice are not shown on the rows yet.
- Whom the game waits for is the pill of the step ("Waiting for Letnev") and the note of the footer. The open question of the viewer is "Your decision" in the bar of the game; the pill of the step says only what the bar does not ("Movement goes on").
- The question of the viewer that has no place on the rows is the list of a decision, with its prompt, above the table; the footer sends it. A reaction window is the band on top of the panel, as on every screen.

## Invasion

The owner chose this design on 2026-10-10, from three that were compared.

- The landings are staged on the map, in one sheet at the active system. It opens when the step opens. The panel says what lands on each planet and what stays in space; it has no controls. A row of it opens the sheet.
- The ground forces that can land are one row of the sheet, the same chips as the load row of the movement: a chip for each kind with the count that is left, grouped by place, one chip chosen (▸).
- Each planet of the system is one row: its name, `res/inf`, its type and what the exploration left there (the Exploration section of `board.md`), who controls it, the units that are there by seat, then one token for each unit that lands and one free slot ("+"). A tap on the slot lands the chosen kind; a tap on a token takes it back. A planet where nothing can land stays in its place and says so.
- A tap on a planet of the active system on the map lands one more unit of the chosen kind there. The planets that can be landed on stand out, as in a payment, and a planet with a staged landing has the check mark.
- Every map view shows the ground forces of a planet after the staged landings.
- **The odds of the ground combat** are shown where the landings show what lands. They are there when a unit lands on a planet where the engine names an opponent (`InvasionView.odds_context`); a planet with a defense that the calculation cannot count (`available: false`) says "No odds", in the sheet and in the row of the panel.
  - The planet row of the sheet has the block of the space odds (`CombatOdds`): "Ground combat · ‹defender›", Win, Draw and Loss of the landing seat in whole percentages, and one line for each side with the expected survivors by printed unit against the units that fight ("0.7 / 2"). Draw: both sides are destroyed (the planet stays with the defender). A combat that does not end is a Loss: the defender holds the planet (Loss is `defender_win_rate + unresolved_rate`; the four rates of `ti4-calc` are exclusive). The rollouts and what the calculation counts and leaves out are behind a `Hint`.
  - The panel has, under the rows of the planets, the first round of each ground combat as a battle table ("Qucen'n · Ground combat · Round 1"): each kind of ground force with its count, what it hits on after the faction's modifier (accent, the printed value on hover), its dice and the hits it is expected to score ("≈0.6"). The odds are in the headers (the Battle table section): the win of each side and its survivors ("~0.7 of 2 survive"), and in the heading the average rounds and the share where both sides are destroyed.
  - `ti4-calc` computes them in the page (`groundOdds` of `web-shared/src/engine/odds.ts`, `Transport.groundOdds`), one question for each planet whose landings changed (`session/groundOdds.ts`): the ground forces of both seats on the planet and those that land, the guns of the context for the defender and its Harrow ships. `GroundOddsResponse` has the rates, the rounds, the survivors and fielded units by unit id, and what each kind rolls (`attacker_rolls`, `defender_rolls`). While the answer for new landings is on its way, the last numbers and the last table are dimmed, with "…". The same in Live and in a draft (scenario `invasion_draft`).
- "Reset" of the sheet takes every staged landing back. The "✓" closes the sheet and sets no mark: there is one system. A tap on the active system opens it again. On a phone the open sheet has the room of the footer, as the fleet of a system has.
- The footer has "Commit ground forces", or "Land nothing" when nothing is staged, and "Reset selection".
- What follows the landings (space cannon defense, ground combat, control, exploration) is not on this screen yet: these are decisions in the list. A decision of an exploration card names the card.

## Production

The owner chose this design on 2026-10-11: the build first, then the payment, both on the map.

- The step has two stages. Nothing is sent before the last button.
- **Build.** The units are staged on the map, in one sheet at the system that produces. It opens when the step opens. The panel says what is produced in each place and has the limits; it has no controls. A row of it opens the sheet.
- Every kind of unit of a production is one row (owner, 2026-10-11; a row of chips with one chosen kind and a row with all printed values were tried, and were hard to read): the icon, the name, the cost ("2", "1 for 2"), how many are left in the supply, the units that are staged, and the tokens. The columns have fixed widths, so cost and left line up over the rows; each group heading says "Cost" and "Left" once.
- The printed values are behind the name (`Peek`): hover shows them, a click or a tap keeps them open, and the card opens above the name when there is no room below. The name has a dotted underline on both layouts. The card is laid out as the printed unit card: the icon and the name, the four values in one row (cost, combat, move, capacity; the number over its word), the keywords with their values, one line each in capitals, and the printed special rule of the unit (`rules`). A section with nothing is not shown. The card has no control, and a keyword is not explained (the Text section of `AGENTS.md`).
- A dice value is read as the game says it: "5+", and "3+ (x3)" with more than one die; the same for BOMBARDMENT, ANTI-FIGHTER BARRAGE and SPACE CANNON. PRODUCTION has the printed value ("PRODUCTION +2").
- The rows are in two groups. "Ships · Space area" says the place once. "Ground forces" says its place in the heading when there is one place only ("Planet Jord"); with several places each unit has one line for each ("Planet Lisis"), under a row with the total.
- What is staged is the count of units (zero is dimmed, more than zero in the accent colour), then one token for each purchase, with the icon of every unit that it makes: a pair of fighters is a token with two icons. A tap on a token takes that purchase back. The dashed slot adds one purchase and shows the units that it makes. It is not shown when nothing more can be added. One step is one purchase. Fighters and infantry come two at a time, as the game sells them; the last pair makes one unit when the production limit has room for one only.
- A unit that cannot be produced in this use keeps its row. It is dimmed and has a word in place of the tokens ("No technology", "None left", "Blockaded", "No place", "Other unit only", "Too costly", "Barred", "Barred here"), with the reason in full as tooltip. The reason is the engine's (`BuildFact.unavailable`): the production window says, for each unit that it does not offer, the first of its checks that excluded it (`ProductionWindow::withheld`, from the same checks that build the offers, written into the question as `details.withheld`), and the host only maps it: the technology, the supply, a blockade, no place, a use of PRODUCTION that makes one unit only or units up to a cost, a ban of a faction on a production by an ability, producers that an ability bars from the unit (Mitosis). A unit that only the resources or the production limit leave out has no reason: these are limits of the sheet. "Not offered" is left for a question that does not say why.
- The limits are gauges of the sheet and of the panel: "Production capacity", "Resources", "Fleet supply after", "Fighters and cargo in space". The heading of the sheet says what is staged and what it costs after a discount ("5 units · 3 resources · discount 1").
- The limits are soft, except the supply of a unit (the plastic): the player plans against them. A build over a soft limit is staged. The gauge is red, the footer says what is over ("2 resources short · 1 unit over the production limit"), and the main button and the "✓" of the sheet wait (pattern 5). The soft limits are the resources (the cost of the staged build against what the ready planets and the trade goods of the seat pay), the production limit, the fleet supply and the capacity. A purchase after the production limit makes all its units. The supply of a unit is hard: "Left" counts down, and when none is left the slot is not shown.
- "Fleet supply after" and "Fighters and cargo in space" are read by the engine (`fleet_standing` of the engine of the page, `Transport.fleetStanding`, which is `ti4_engine::fleet::FleetCounts::standing`; the wire types are the engine's `FleetCounts` and `FleetStanding`): the page adds the staged units to the counts of the facts (`ProductionFacts.fleet`, `stagedCounts`: a ship of the fleet pool, the capacity of a ship, a fighter or a ground force in the space area), and the engine applies the rules: the fighters that the space docks hold use no capacity, and with Fighter II the fighters that nothing holds count against the fleet pool. While the answer for a changed build is on its way, the gauges keep the last one.
- No planet pays while the build is chosen, and there is no payment bar. The main button says what is next: "Pay 3 resources", "Produce units" when the build costs nothing, "Produce nothing" when nothing is staged. The "✓" of the sheet does what "Pay …" does. "Reset" takes the build back.
- **Pay.** The sheet closes and the map is the payment (the Payment section of `board.md`): the bar, the planets that pay, Auto-pay. A planet pays with its best face, as the engine names it (`PayingPlanet.face`: its influence with Archon's Gift or Freelancers), and commodities pay as trade goods when the Keleres agent allows it (`ProductionFacts.commodities`). The footer has the payment line and "Produce units", which is disabled while the payment is short. "Back to build" (Esc) or a tap on the system that produces opens the sheet again; the staged payment stays and is measured against the new cost.
- Exception to "a tap on a planet of the active system acts on the open sheet" (the Invasion section): in a production a planet always pays. The reason: every ready planet pays, also the planets of the system that produces.
- On a phone the open sheet has the room of the footer, as the fleet of a system has. The payment has the whole map pane, with the footer under it.
- Every map view shows the ships and the ground forces of the system after the staged build.
- What the game asks before the production (AI Development Algorithm) is a decision in the list.
- A production outside a tactical action (Warfare's secondary, the PRODUCTION of Thunder's Edge's Construction) is the same step in the frame of its strategic action: no steps of a tactical action, the card as the heading, its printed text behind "?".

## Battle table

- The headers of the attacker and the defender have the same lines, so the unit rows start at the same height.
- The name of a seat in a header is cut with "…", with the full name on hover; a phone wraps it.
- The simulated odds are in these headers (win % and survivors). They have no card of their own.
- A player can have more than one strategy card (a game of four or fewer players). The player table shows all of them.
