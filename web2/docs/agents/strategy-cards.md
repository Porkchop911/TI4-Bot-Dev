# web2: strategy cards

Part of `web2/AGENTS.md`: the Patterns and the rules of Screen size and Text there apply here too.

## Strategy cards

All eight cards use one frame: one list of seats. There are no tabs, no seat column and no trail.

- The list has the primary ("P") and then the secondaries in seat order. Each seat is one full-width line: order, symbol, name, state, and what the seat did. All decisions made so far are on one screen, with no click.
- A state has a sign: "▶ Deciding", "· Waits for Blair", "✎ Private draft", "✎ Draft ready", "✓ Resolved", "✓ Followed", "– Passed".
- Only the row of the viewer opens: into the editor, in place, while the viewer has a choice or an open draft there. After it resolves the row closes and shows its result like any other row. A ready draft is closed and says what it will do.
- Other rows are not controls. A choice of another player is visible when it is resolved, never before.
- The same list, with no open row, is the read-only view of a strategy card in the event log.
- The list is dense (24px rows) so that the largest editor, the technology tree with a payment, fits with all eight seats.

- The whole primary is one screen. Every part is staged; "Resolve primary" sends all of it. It is split only where the game reveals hidden information (Politics: the agenda cards are a second screen, sent again).
- A part of a card is a block that uses one of the decision shapes. The board and the player table serve the first part that is not complete.
- A chosen thing is a row with "Change". "Change" clears that part, so it is the open part again. Esc clears the last choice, then leaves the action.
- A part that needs no choice is a value: "Trade goods 2 → 5", "Victory points 6 → 7".
- A secondary is the same editor as its part of the primary, in the row of the viewer: the pill of the heading says "Private draft" or "Your decision". It can be drafted before the seat is reached. It states what it costs: "Strategy pool 2 → 1", or who gave it for free.
- A secondary with a choice: nothing chosen is a pass, and the main button says so. A secondary without a choice (Politics, Trade, Imperial) is a list with "Follow" and "Pass", keys 1 and 2.
- In a game of the engine (2026-10-12) the list of the seats is built, with the facts of the engine (`secondary` of the update): the names are the players of the table (the factions in a game with no table), and the viewer is "You". The editors of the cards are not built, but for the technology tree (below): the row of the viewer opens into the question of the game as a list, with the question and "Strategy pool 2 → 1" above it. A row of another seat has its state and no text of what the seat did.
- The draft of a secondary is in Live, not in the Draft tab: it is the row of the viewer. It opens by itself from the moment the card is played (owner, 2026-10-12), also while the primary is resolved; Trade opens with its window, because the primary changes what the secondary costs. Each answer is recorded with "Record". When the draft has nothing more to decide, the main button is "Mark draft ready". A ready draft has "Change draft"; "Start over" drops the answers.
- A ready draft is sent when the game asks the seat, and the seat is not asked. The game checks it first: a draft that no longer fits is not ready, the footer says so, and the seat decides in Live. The game sends it, not the page: the server online (the Online play section of `online.md`), the engine in a local game. A draft that is not ready when the seat is reached is not sent: the game asks, and "Send draft" sends the draft as it is.
- A secondary that asks the seat nothing as the game is (Leadership with no influence to spend) is no draft: the row says "Nothing to decide as the game is now".
- A draft ends where the game would show something hidden (the cards of Politics, the objective of Imperial). The row says so, and the draft can be ready: the game goes on from there in Live.
- A command pool is a row of triangles, one for each token, with the count ("3 → 5") and a counter. The fleet pool points up, the tactic and strategy pools point down, as on the command sheet. A token that stays is filled, an added token has "+", a removed token is an empty outline with "−".
- In a game of the engine (2026-10-16) every redistribution of command tokens (`mode: "redistribute"`: the status phase with its gained tokens, Warfare's primary) is the pool editor, not a list (`select/tokens.ts`). The staging starts at the sheet of now, and the header says "<placed> / <total> placed"; the footer says what is left to place, then the change ("Tactic 2 → 3 · Fleet 3 → 4"). The bounds of each counter come from the arrangements that the game offers (the least count of that pool among them, and the tokens left), not from a rule; "Send" waits for an offered arrangement. Esc puts the pools back. A restack (Predictive Intelligence) stays a list.
- In a game of the engine (2026-10-16) a gain of command tokens and the purchase of tokens for influence are one screen (`select/purchase.ts`): every question with `kind: "command_tokens"` and mode "gain" (Leadership's three tokens, Warfare's one), and the purchase question of Leadership (mode "buy" with `details.purchase`), which is also the window of its secondary. "Buy command tokens" is a counter up to `purchase.max`, at `purchase.cost` influence each; "Command pools" places the gained and the bought tokens from the sheet of now ("3 gained · 2 bought · 4 / 5 placed"). While tokens are bought the map is the payment, as in a production: the planets of `purchase.planets` with their worth, the trade goods in the payment bar, Auto-pay and "Clear payment", and the payment line in the footer. The main button is "Buy 2 tokens" (or "Buy nothing" for a purchase question, "Send" for a gain); it waits until every token is placed and the payment covers the cost. It sends one plan (`pool`, `purchase` and `pay` steps of `PlanStep`): the game takes a pool for each gained token, "yes" and a pool for each bought token, then "no", and a payment from the staged ones whenever it asks for one (`plan_answer` in `ti4-host/src/planning/steps.rs`). Esc clears the whole screen. In the draft of a secondary the purchase stays a list, one answer at a time.
- The printed text of a strategy card of the engine is behind "?" of the heading: the primary and the secondary of that card from `Catalog.records.strategy_cards`, so each set of strategy cards has its own text.
- Exception to "the source is on top": the printed text of a strategy card is behind hover. There are eight cards and every player knows them.
- Several players are chosen in the player table too (Trade). A row shows what the choice gives that player ("+6 commodities").
- The technology tree is a block in the panel, not a dialog: four colour columns, a row for the unit upgrades, and a row for the faction technologies when the faction has any that are not unit upgrades. A faction technology shows its colour as a square mark before the name. A cell is the control. A filled symbol is a prerequisite that the player has, a hollow one is missing. The printed text of a technology is behind "?".
- Exception to "a chosen thing is a row with Change": in the technology tree the selected cell is the chosen thing and the control. The header of the tree names what is chosen and its price.
- Exception to "keys 1, 2, 3": the cells of the technology tree have no keys. There are too many. Enter and Esc act as everywhere.
- In a game of the engine (2026-10-15) every question that researches a technology is the tree: the primary, the secondary and its draft, and any other card that asks for a research (`select/research.ts`). The game offers what can be researched (the options of kind `research`); a cell of another technology is not a control. The tree around them is printed: `research` of the update (`ti4-host/src/view/research.rs`) has the colour, the prerequisites and the owner of each technology of the seat, and its ready planets with a specialty, which the header of a column names ("Green 1 · +1 Rigel III"). A filled prerequisite mark counts the owned technologies of its colour and these planets; whether a cell can be chosen is the engine's answer. One question is one research: the second research of the primary is the tree again, with its price. A research that may be declined has "Research nothing" as the main button while no cell is chosen. The engine pays a research by itself (the cheapest plan), so the tree has no payment on the map.
